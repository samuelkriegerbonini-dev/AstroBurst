## [Unreleased]

## [0.9.0] - 2026-11-13

### Added
- JWST time-series spectra (`x1dints`):
  - Time-series spectra, in both table layouts, as level 3 products or as the segment files of one exposure, are listed in Files with their integrations, channels and series, and open as one series per detector and spectral order; the segment files of an exposure are stitched, and the Spectra tool says what a set holds (series, completeness of each exposure, caveats).
  - The Spectra tool shows one integration, the median or the mean over a range of integrations; a time-series file opens on the median spectrum of its first series.
  - A Light curves section in the Spectra tool builds the white light and wavelength bins in pipeline mode (the rows and wavelength limits of the JWST pipeline; it reproduces the archived `_whtlt.ecsv` to rounding) or in masked mode (only the elements valid in every integration, with outlying elements rejected and listed). The limits in use and their origin are always shown, times are BMJD (TDB) from `TDB-MID`, else `MJD-AVG` (UTC), else the integration number, with a line that says which, and a click on a point shows the spectrum of that integration.
  - The pipeline white-light table (`_whtlt.ecsv`, old and current layout) is compared with the app's white light, row by row on time and column by column, and can be drawn over it.
  - Depth spectrum: the box depth of each wavelength bin (1 - F_in / F_out) with its error against wavelength, beside the white-light depth, with the ranges of molecular bands shaded; it is labelled as a quick look without limb darkening or a systematics model, and warns when the extraction lost pixels during the transit.
  - Fitted transmission spectrum: Fit every bin fits a transit model (Mandel and Agol 2002, quadratic limb darkening) to every wavelength bin with the orbit of the white-light fit or a typed one, and draws (Rp/Rs)^2 with its error beside the quick-look depths, each series labelled and switchable, with the warnings of every bin and the pixel-loss and grazing warnings in sight.
  - Light curves, the depth spectrum and the fitted transmission spectrum are saved as CSV with their provenance (input files with SHA-256, mode, limits, quick-look label, the model and fixed orbit of a fit) and added to the measurement log, one row per fitted band.
- Light curves, period search and transit fit (Photometry tool):
  - One light-curve view serves the Series section, the Light curves section of the Spectra tool and saved tables; a light curve measured earlier in the session is shown again when the Photometry tool is reopened on its reference frame.
  - A saved light-curve table (the app's own light-curve or transit-fit CSV, a JWST `_whtlt.ecsv`, or a CSV with a time column and a flux or magnitude column) opens in the Series section from its row in Files (Open as light curve), with its columns chosen automatically or by hand.
  - Quick look: normalised flux, a typed transit window whose edges are shown with every digit needed to give the same window back, the out-of-transit scatter in ppm and mmag against the formal error, and a box depth labelled as a quick look; points flagged as saturated, with a saturated comparison star or with a centroid jump are left out by default, while pixel-loss points of a time-series spectrum are marked and kept.
  - Binning by points or minutes, sigma clipping against a running median, and points left out by a click; left-out points stay visible.
  - Period search and fold: a generalised Lomb-Scargle periodogram, checked against astropy, gives the best period with the false-alarm probability of the highest peak and the grid resolution. On a regularly sampled curve the automatic range stops at the Nyquist frequency of the cadence (five times the mean Nyquist frequency for irregular sampling); a typed shortest period is searched as given, and a highest peak above the Nyquist frequency of the cadence is flagged as a possible alias. The periods searched and the rule that set the limit are printed under the plot; the curve folds on the period found or on a typed period and epoch, and Log period search writes the result to the measurement log.
  - Transit fit: weighted least squares of a transit (Mandel and Agol 2002, quadratic limb darkening, circular orbit) times a baseline polynomial of order 0 to 2, with the period typed and mid time, Rp/Rs, a/Rs, b, u1 and u2 each free or fixed, and the quick-look window and depth as starting values. The model is drawn over the data divided by the fitted baseline with the residuals in ppm below; cards give the depth, residual scatter, reduced chi2 and the time-averaging factor beta, and a table gives every fitted, fixed and derived quantity (inclination, durations T14 and T23, mean stellar density) with its 1 sigma error from the covariance and its unit. Save fit CSV and Copy fit CSV write data, baseline, model and residuals with the fit and its provenance, with the time column named after the time scale (`bmjd_tdb`, `bjd_tdb` and so on) so that the file opens again as a light curve, and Log transit fit records it.
  - Eclipse fit: the planet-to-star flux ratio of a secondary eclipse, with the transit geometry fixed or free.
  - Time series: stars beyond the limit of 64 start as ignore, the selected star is marked in the Stars list, the Stars list and Measure stay usable while another frame is shown, and the RA and Dec of the light-time target can be typed, with the target in use shown. The frame table states the unit of each column and whether a time is relative, shows a frame from the keyboard and marks the frames left out of the analysis.
  - The Series section shows the photometry status line of the reference frame above the Stars block, and calibration warnings of the frames are listed once each (at most 8, then a count).
- Spectra tool and 1D spectra:
  - Selecting a 1D spectrum in Files shows its plot in the main viewer with the same axis, frame, masks, line marks and line range as the Spectra tool, and a drag in the viewer selects the line range the tool measures. The choices made for a spectrum survive closing the tool or switching files.
  - The spectrum plot has a y title with the flux unit, an error band for spectrum files that carry errors, wheel zoom, Shift plus drag to pan, a double click to reset, log y, limits from the 1st to the 99th percentile, a keyboard cursor with a spoken readout, and sharp lines on scaled displays.
  - Display units: wavelengths in µm, nm or Å, and the flux of a spectrum file in its own unit, Jy, mJy, µJy, F_lambda (cgs or SI) or AB magnitudes; the axis, readout, error band and CSV follow the choice.
  - A spectrum file can be shown binned by an integer factor, with the error of each bin; the line measurement keeps the channels of the file, and Copy CSV and Save CSV write the bins.
  - A line above the plot says what the file is (instrument, disperser, target, where the errors and flags come from), and a picker chooses another `EXTRACT1D` table of an x1d (order, slit, source) or the series and pick of a time-series file. A 1D array whose header names no wavelength unit opens with a unit you state (µm, nm or Å), and the spectrum says whether its axis unit came from the file, was assumed or was set by you.
  - Band strips: the absorption bands of H2O, CO2, CO, CH4, SO2, NH3, H2S, HCN and C2H2, the Na I D, K I, He I 1.083 µm and Hα lines are drawn as labelled strips at the redshift of the line list, and the NIRSpec G395H detector gap in the observed frame, chosen with species chips beside Rest lines; labels that would overprint move to a second or third row, and a click in a strip sets the line range to the band.
  - The line range and the continuum windows are typed in the unit of the axis, with the channel as secondary text and a switch back to channel boxes.
  - The line setup (axis, frame, rest wavelength, redshift, families, bands, picked line, line range and windows, kept as wavelengths) carries over to the next spectrum taken with the same instrument, disperser, filter and pupil (the `INSTRUME`, `GRATING`, `FILTER` and `PUPIL` cards; the detector may differ), with a note that says where it came from and offers to clear it; a cleared setup is carried no further.
  - The line list gains the Na I D and K I doublets (47 lines).
- Line measurement:
  - Line measurements report one-sigma errors on the equivalent width, centroid, sigma, FWHM and the velocities derived from them, with two flags that say when those first-order errors can be trusted (line S/N of 10 or more; a sigma error no larger than 15 % of sigma).
  - They report the redshift of the line with its error, the wavelength of the peak, the air or vacuum frame of the centroid and the identity of the line picked, and say when a continuum window overlaps the line range.
  - They give the integrated line flux in W m^-2 and erg s^-1 cm^-2 with its error when the unit of the spectrum allows it (Jy and F_lambda spectra, region sums of MJy/sr cubes), and say how the number was obtained or why it is absent.
  - When two or more lines of one source in one file are measured, the line card shows their combined redshift with its error and chi2.
  - A line can be measured on a spectrum picked from a JWST time series (median, mean of a range or one integration), with the same errors, physical flux and notes as a file measurement and the warnings of the series in the notes.
  - Auto continuum windows are as wide as the line range, leave a guard gap for the line wings and say their rule.
  - The cube Fit lines takes the redshift of the line list: the source-frame maps get labels, units and the diverging velocity display, the spaxel card shows source-frame velocity, width and flux beside the observer-frame values, and the log records the redshift.
- Tables in Files and the Archive:
  - `.csv` and `.ecsv` files can be dropped or opened: CSV and ECSV tables, and FITS files that hold only binary tables, are listed as table rows with their size and format instead of failing, and a CSV spectrum written by the app opens as a 1D spectrum. A table row can be selected and says what it holds in the viewer; a light-curve table offers Open as light curve and a catalogue table Use as catalogue, which show again the file that was on screen last (an image, or for a light curve an image or a spectrum) and open the Photometry tool at the Series or Compare section, which stays in view while the panels above it fill in; with no such file the table is kept for later and a note says so.
  - The Archive opens ECSV tables (pipeline white-light curves, source catalogues) in Files, opens the Spectra tool for a downloaded spectrum, cube or ramp, lists the pipeline white-light table of a time-series observation with the files that open (the TSO preset asks for it), and suggests a time-series, a 1D-spectrum and an IFU search on its first screen.
  - Files lists an x1dints file as time-series spectra with its integrations, channels and series, says how many `EXTRACT1D` tables an x1d holds, and offers product chips for c1d, crfints and the white-light table.
- Survey files:
  - bzip2-compressed FITS files (`.fits.bz2`, `.fit.bz2`, `.fts.bz2`), such as SDSS frames, open like gzip FITS; they are listed under their stem, and Compose and the product chips read the filter and product type from their names.
  - DQ and mask planes stored in tile-compressed HDUs (fpacked JWST and HST products, the Legacy Surveys maskbits file) are read as their exact integers.
  - Pan-STARRS asinh-scaled stack images (`BSOFTEN`, `BOFFSET`) are linearised on load, the plane description says so, and derived files no longer copy the two cards.
  - One band of a multi-band cutout cube (a Legacy Surveys `griz` cutout) opens as its own image, addressed as `<file>#hdu=<N>&plane=<K>`. The HDU list shows one button per band under each cube, and a band takes the same band of the inverse-variance cube as its error and the mask HDU as its flags, with the saturation flags of that band.
  - The filter registry knows the bands of Euclid, HSC, DECam, SDSS, Pan-STARRS, 2MASS, WISE, GALEX and VISTA with their pivot wavelengths, and identifies dataset and band from the headers these surveys write, including the band of one plane of a cutout cube.
- Error and mask layers:
  - An image whose file holds a `VARIANCE`, `VAR`, `IVAR`, `INVVAR`, `WGT`, `WEIGHT` or `RMS` layer (named by `EXTNAME`, or by `EXTTYPE` when the HDU has no `EXTNAME`, as in LSST Science Pipelines exposures), or a layer named by an `IMAGETYP`, `IMTYPE` or `DES_EXT` card, takes it as its error, converted once to one sigma in the unit of the image, with a sentence that says where it came from and a count of the pixels without data.
  - A mask layer named `MASK`, `MSK`, `FLAG` or `MASKBITS` (by `EXTNAME` or `EXTTYPE`) is the DQ layer of its image, with the bit names the file gives (Rubin and HSC `MP_` cards, Legacy Surveys `MBIT_` cards, Pan-STARRS `MSKNAM` cards), or the built-in DR10 table for a Legacy Surveys maskbits layer without `MBIT_` cards. The DQ menu lists those names, says where the mask came from, labels the exclusion with the flags really excluded and lets you tick the bits to exclude; in its list a tick marks a flag drawn on the image and the tag `excl` a flag that the histogram, statistics and photometry leave out, with a line above the list that says so; a mask without names excludes nothing until bits are chosen, except a DES coadd mask (`DES_EXT = 'MASK'`), where every non-zero pixel is excluded.
  - Opening a Legacy Surveys brick image, a Euclid MER mosaic or a Pan-STARRS stack pairs the error and mask files of the same folder when their names follow the survey's rule and the grids match, and says which file was taken and why (for a Pan-STARRS weight file, its `PS_COVAR` card, which states that the weight is a variance); a file that matches the name but not the grid is named and left out.
  - A Layers of the shown image block in the Headers tool says where the error and the mask of the image come from and why a layer was not used, and assigns a layer by hand from another file or another HDU, switches a layer off or returns to the automatic choice. The grid is checked, and a layer of another size, a floating-point or 64-bit mask or an error layer of unknown kind is refused with the reason. Assignments last until the app closes.
  - An SDSS frame says that its error can be rebuilt: after the gain and the dark variance of its camera column and band are typed, the one-sigma error is rebuilt from the calibration vector and the sky table of the file and used like any other error layer.
  - The HDU rows say what each layer is and in which unit. The hover readout and the pixel table say where a ± value came from when the error layer was converted, assumed, taken from another file, set by hand or rebuilt; the pixel table marks flagged cells without relying on a tint and has a key; Files and the Info panel say when the open file is itself an error or mask layer.
  - A copied pixel table or Statistics table starts with lines naming the file, plane, unit and pixel, and layer assignments, switched-off layers, rebuilt SDSS errors and chosen flag exclusions are written to the measurement log.
  - A cutout records in its ERR extension where the one-sigma error came from, says why it could not use a layer, and pads a mask that has named flags with the file's own no-data flag.
- Calibration by unit and survey zero points:
  - Images whose BUNIT is a flux density per pixel (Jy, mJy, µJy, nJy, MJy, `10*nanoJansky`, nanomaggy) are calibrated by their unit; the unit wins over leftover `PHOTFLAM` and `PHOTPLAM` cards, and a zero-point card that contradicts the unit by more than 0.005 mag gives no calibration.
  - Survey zero points are read with their origin: `FLUXMAG0`, the Pan-STARRS stack zero point (`FPA.ZP` + 2.5 log10(EXPTIME)) and the Legacy Surveys cutout without unit cards (nanomaggy assumed, with a warning).
  - A zero point can be typed in the Photometry and Table sections when the header gives none (AB or Vega, per unit or per unit per second); the status line and the result say that the image is calibrated with a user zero point.
  - Every calibration carries a sentence that says where it comes from, shown in the photometry result with the formula behind it; an uncalibrated image says why, by cause, and still names the unit of its fluxes.
  - Cube spectra carry a Jy series for every cube unit with a known conversion (Jy/sr, Jy/arcsec², a flux density per pixel), so the SED of such a cube plots fluxes.
- Forced multi-band photometry and the SED:
  - A Multi-band section of the Photometry tool measures one list of sky positions in up to 12 band images with one aperture in arcsec or pixels, through the WCS of each band and without recentring, with an optional local sky annulus and an optional aperture correction from the growth of the stars of each band. Errors come from the error layer, a progress line has Cancel, and one card per band gives its filter and pivot, pixel scale, aperture, calibration, error and flag layers, correction and noise factor, or why it could not be measured.
  - The table of sources gives the flux in µJy with its error or a 3 sigma upper limit, AB magnitudes on a switch, the colours of neighbouring bands with their errors or bounds and what is wrong with a cell in words, and copies or saves as a long or a wide CSV with provenance lines.
  - The selected source shows its integrated SED (one point per band at its pivot wavelength, an arrow for an upper limit) and, when one of its bands is on screen, its apertures on the image.
  - A band without a header calibration takes a typed zero point, and the noise factor measured by the Depth section with the same aperture is offered with one click.
  - A Size column and the Resolved or not card say whether each source is unresolved, marginally resolved or resolved against the PSF. A source whose FWHM fit finds no width (too faint, or far wider than the PSF, such as a large galaxy or a blend) reads `no FWHM` in the Size column, and the legend and the note of the source say why and point to the Profiles section of the Image tool to measure its size.
  - The SED panel names what it plots, lists its values in a table, copies and saves as CSV, states the PSF of a matched cube, and switches to log y or to AB magnitudes; multi-band runs and SED exports go to the measurement log.
- Catalogue comparison:
  - A Compare section of the Photometry tool measures the sources of a catalogue table saved from a survey archive on the image on screen, at the catalogue positions and in the catalogue's own aperture. Presets for Legacy Surveys DR10, DES DR2, SDSS DR17, Pan-STARRS1 DR2 and Euclid Q1 fill the columns, unit, aperture and cuts, every one visible and editable. The preset starts on the band of the open image (`Legacy Surveys DR10, r` on an r image) until you choose a preset or edit a field.
  - The summary gives the count of every stage, the median of measured over catalogue flux with its error, robust scatter and N, the error ratio and the zero point the catalogue implies. Every row is listed with its ratio or the reason it was left out, the rows can be marked on the image, and the report copies or saves as CSV and goes to the measurement log.
  - The Gaia cross-match says which Gaia list it used (cone, G limit, row cap, VizieR table), can take the same cone and G limit as the search above it, labels its zero point as a Vega zero point of the Gaia band, and its CSV files start with lines naming catalogue, epoch, band, system, aperture, zero point and units.
- Depth, PSF and the resolved-or-not check:
  - A Depth section of the Photometry tool measures the N sigma limit of empty apertures on source-free sky (5 sigma, 1 arcsec radius and 500 apertures by default) in AB and µJy with its definition, the factor f_corr by which the errors of the error layer understate the real scatter and, with a PSF, the formal point-source limit; the apertures are drawn on the image, and the result exports as CSV and goes to the log.
  - A Resolution section measures the PSF FWHM of the image on its stars (in pixels and arcsec, with the scatter between stars), or takes a typed value or a seeing card of the header, and holds the redshift, the cosmology and the thresholds of the rule.
  - A Resolved or not card, under the photometry result, beside R50 in the Profiles panel and in the Multi-band section, says whether a source is unresolved, marginally resolved or resolved against the PSF, with every input of the verdict and, with a redshift, the sizes in kpc.
  - The Profiles panel marks the PSF on the surface-brightness plot, gives its linear y axes the unit of the image and writes a CSV with the ellipse, background, calibration and radii; the Info readout gives kpc per pixel and per arcsec when a redshift is set; the PSF panel prints its FWHM in pixels and arcsec with the scatter between its stars.
  - The SAGUI cube check reports the PSF FWHM, pixel scale and PSF area of each band before the run, and whether the requested number of regions leaves at least one PSF area per region.
  - Cube Prep shows which error and mask layers each input will use, its pixel scale and, on request, its measured PSF FWHM; after a run a table gives the source, target and matched PSF FWHM per filter, the redshift field says what it is for, and `.bz2` and `.fz` inputs are accepted.
- Photometry panels:
  - A status line above the fields of the Photometry and Table sections says what the image offers before a star is clicked: its calibration and where it comes from, its pixel unit, the error and flag layers a measurement will use, its pixel scale and its filter.
  - Radii are typed in pixels or arcsec, and the Table measures a list of sky positions (RA Dec, in the format of the Targets panel).
  - A source below three times its error is shown as not detected at 3 sigma, with the limit in AB magnitude and flux (the table prints `> 26.10`); when the Depth section measured the noise factor with the same aperture, it is offered beside the radius fields and applied on Use.
  - Values with an uncertainty are rounded to it, the FWHM is also given in arcsec and says when it is undersampled, and the sky card gives the error of the sky level.
  - The Regions panel shows the notes of the statistics, names the pixel unit, counts the zeros and pixels without data it left out, says when a clip entry is not a number, and shows the clipped statistics.
- General interface:
  - The Depth, Resolution and Compare sections have section tips. All tools and Search everywhere find tools by the features inside them, and Search everywhere finds files by instrument and lists files hidden by the Files filter chips, revealing the one you choose.
  - The dock resize handles work from the keyboard (arrow keys, Home and End, Enter to reset).
  - The Point tool stays on after each point until Esc or another tool is chosen.
  - Processing and Stacking reopen on the section used last.
  - The analysis plots shade spans (transit window, baseline), wrap a long legend onto more rows, draw the label of a vertical reference line near the right edge on the left of the line, save PNG files with an opaque background at twice the screen resolution or more, and have a text alternative.
  - Copy CSV buttons show "copied" for 1.5 s and announce it, failures in the analysis panels are announced to screen readers, and switches say which choice is active without relying on colour.
  - The measurement log has eleven new kinds (time-series light curves, depth spectrum, fitted transmission spectrum, period search, forced photometry, image depth, resolution check, catalogue comparison, SED, layer assignment, transit fit), and its CSV starts with provenance lines (software and version, export time in UTC, files, rows, kinds, column meanings).
  - Astrometry > Stars gives the median FWHM with its unit, in arcsec when the image has a pixel scale, and says it is a moment estimate; Astrometry > Geometry prints the target and site used for BJD_TDB with their source; the Histogram statistics line ends with the unit of the data.

### Changed
- The Cube tool is now called Spectra, as it opens 1D spectra, time-series spectra, cubes and ramps; searching for "Cube" still finds it.
- A cube whose header names its bands (`BANDS`, `BAND<n>`) opens on its first band instead of as a colour composite.
- Header zero points (`MAGZERO`, `MAGZPT`, `ZPT`, `PHOTZP`, `ZEROPT`) state their magnitude system from `PHOTSYS` or `MAGSYS` (AB, Vega, ST, or AB assumed) and list every assumption in the warnings. A `MAGZPT` zero point on a count image is divided by EXPTIME (MAGZPT 23.0 on an ADU image with EXPTIME 10 gives 15.5 mag for a sum of 10000, was 13.0), and a Vega or ST zero point no longer yields an AB magnitude or Jy: the result shows the magnitude in that system.
- An image whose BUNIT is a flux density per pixel now has Jy and AB magnitudes from its unit, where it was uncalibrated without a zero-point card. Calibrated survey units report that no gain applies, Cube Prep treats nanomaggy as a per-pixel physical unit, and resample, blend and drizzle drop a flux-density BUNIT that no longer describes their pixels (with a HISTORY line) and correct `FLUXMAG0` and `FPA.ZP` for the change of pixel area; rescaled outputs drop them.
- A zero-point card on a mask, flag, weight or variance layer, or on asinh-scaled pixels, is no longer read as a calibration (a Euclid FLAG cutout with MAGZERO 24.6 is now uncalibrated).
- Error and mask layers: a layer of another size than the image is refused with both sizes in the reason instead of being dropped silently, a DQ plane gets no error layer, a `MASK` HDU is now the DQ layer of its image, and the origins read as file facts (`EXTNAME ERR, HDU 2: as is`, was `ERR plane (HDU 2)`). STIS, COS and WFPC2 files no longer take the ACS and WFC3 flag names, and a flag layer without names excludes nothing until bits are chosen (a DES coadd mask excludes every non-zero pixel).
- Cube line fit at a redshift: the unresolved flag of the mask (bit 16) and the count in the LSF note follow the source-frame width instead of the observer-frame width.
- A spectrum in air wavelengths opens on the Air wavelength axis instead of the vacuum one, and a line under the Frame row says which card or rule gave the frame; the spectrum CSV of such a file then carries `air_wavelength_um`. The spectrum CSV starts with the provenance lines, names instrument, disperser and target, gives the unit of every column and adds the wavelength and flux in the units on screen.
- Compare in the Spectra tool drops a row only when the flag table of its file excludes it (bit 0, DO_NOT_USE, for JWST and Roman), as the plot and the line measurement do, instead of whenever any flag is set; the pipeline spectrum and the regions beyond the eighth are drawn dashed.
- 1D spectra stored as integers or with BSCALE and BZERO are read at full precision instead of through single precision (a raw 16777217 in BITPIX 32 reads 16777217, was 16777216).
- The line card labels its widths Sigma (2nd moment) and FWHM (2.355 x sigma), prints the peak with its unit and the S/N with "(emission)" or "(absorption)", counts the excluded channels in the line and in the continuum, and shows widths and positions in µm, nm or Å. The line CSV has 83 columns: the errors, the line identity and redshift, the physical flux, the file, the DQ handling and `source_series` are appended.
- Photometry: the aperture card gives the radius in pixels and arcsec (`r=4.80 px (0.480")`), and the photometry CSV starts with provenance, calibration and unit lines and has 36 columns, with `mag`, `mag_err`, `mag_system`, `upper_limit_jy` and `upper_limit_ab` appended; its saved name drops `.fz` and `.bz2`.
- Regions: sums and nets print rounded to their error (`585.0 ± 8.5`, was `585 ± 8.533`); the region CSV starts with provenance and unit lines and adds `n_nan`, `n_padding`, `mad`, the clipped statistics, the background region and `bg_median`, `bg_sigma` and `bg_n`; region log rows carry the background, the counts and the notes.
- Light-curve CSV: software, export time and source file lines, a unit line for every column, and the new columns `rel_flux`, `rel_flux_err` and `left_out`.
- ETD and ExoClock leave out the frames the light-curve view leaves out and say how many; the ExoClock BJD_TDB file states the accuracy of computed values; a saturated comparison star no longer blocks the exports while the flagged frames are left out.
- Time series: the exposure of a frame is read from EXPTIME, then XPOSURE, then EFFEXPTM, then EXPOSURE, and the series warns once when only the frame or only the reference names a filter. A time-series row of the measurement log is written again when a comparison star is left out or put back.
- Measurement log: cross-match rows name the Gaia table and the list asked for, and their unit reads `mag (Vega)`; photometry rows record the unit the radii were typed in, a typed zero point and the calibration sentence; statistics rows of a surface-brightness image say that the sum is in MJy/sr x pix.
- Gaia cross-match CSV files gain `aperture_radius`, and the matches file also `saturated` and `zp_status`.
- Cube Prep recipe validation reports skipped custom filter datasets: a custom dataset named EUCLID, HSC, DECAM, SDSS, PS1, 2MASS, WISE, GALEX or VISTA now has a built-in name, is skipped with a warning and must be renamed in `filter_datasets` to keep its filters.
- Readability: the primary blue button, input placeholders and status words reach a contrast of 4.5:1, the groups a ramp fit left out are drawn in amber, the buttons under every plot are 24 px tall, the region delete icon is a 24 px target, and exponent tick labels keep enough digits to differ from each other.
- Opening a file, or a folder that holds a file, already in Files no longer adds a second row: the row already there is selected and a note says so. A row that failed to open can be opened again.
- Archive product rows keep the end of a long file name (`_x1dints.fits`, `_whtlt.ecsv`, `_calints.fits`) in sight and shorten the part before it; the full name stays in the tooltip.

### Fixed
- A decimal RA inside 0 to 360 degrees in a target list or a sky CSV is kept exactly as typed; it used to come back with a rounding tail (53.1 read as 53.10000000000002).
- Switching Symmetric off gives back the stretch it replaced: an image shown with mtf no longer stays linear and dark until Reset display.
- Back on a cube from the Files list, the frame stepper, Frame Navigation and the arrow keys are there at once on the channel shown; they no longer wait for a click that extracts a spectrum. Frame Navigation also shows before the first spectrum.
- Plate Solve: when no offline solution is found, the best-candidate line names the test that refused the candidate and only the thresholds it missed (for example its false-alarm probability against the 10^-9 limit); it used to quote the match and RMS thresholds even when the candidate met them. The scale hint in the failure text has 4 significant digits.
- Pan-STARRS stack cutouts: the old-style `PC001001` matrix cards are read, so sky positions, the sky readout and forced photometry land on the right pixels; they were mirrored in x.
- Cube Prep refuses asinh-scaled PSF and kernel files instead of reading them as linear values.
- A folder dropped on the window opens its files like Select Folder, instead of "1 file skipped".
- The sky position under the pointer is printed to 0.001 s and 0.01 arcsec in the status strip and the Info readout, so two neighbouring JWST or HST pixels no longer read the same coordinate.
- Hide file panel keeps the keyboard focus on the rail, the GPU viewer's hint on a cube names the Spectra tool, and the close button of the shortcut sheet has a name for screen readers.
- A JWST time-series file opens on its median spectrum instead of failing with a table error, and the spectrum summary counts the samples plotted instead of the rows of the table.

### Limitations
- Transit and eclipse fits are weighted least squares with errors from the covariance: no MCMC, a circular orbit, a polynomial baseline in time, no orbital eccentricity, exposure-time smearing, detrending against airmass or position, or red-noise model, and limb-darkening coefficients are typed or fitted, not taken from stellar models. The period search fits sinusoids (a box-shaped transit shows at its period and its harmonics) and gives no period uncertainty.
- There is no atmospheric retrieval: the fitted transmission spectrum is a depth per wavelength bin with its error. A level 3 product that lost pixels during a deep, short transit biases both the quick-look and the fitted depths (the pixel-loss warning says when), and NIRISS SOSS fluxes labelled MJy are not on that scale.
- The Multi-band and Compare sections measure at given positions: there is no source detection or deblending. The SED is shown, not fitted, and Vega magnitudes are not converted to AB.
- Real files confirmed: 16 JWST `x1dints` files and 16 `_whtlt.ecsv` tables of NIRSpec, NIRISS SOSS, NIRCam grism and MIRI LRS (the pipeline-mode white light matches the archived tables within 4.1e-15 relative); the transit fit on the NIRCam white light of WASP-39 b (within 1.2 sigma of Carter et al. 2024); line measurement on a NIRSpec G395H x1d; forced photometry against the catalogue apertures of Legacy Surveys DR10, DES, SDSS, Pan-STARRS and Euclid files, and the catalogue comparison on the same five sets; the image depth on the Legacy Surveys r brick within 0.05 mag of an independent computation; an LSSTCam visit image and an LSST Science Pipelines test exposure (`afwdata`) open with their image, variance and mask layers, the Rubin mask names and, on the visit image, the nJy unit; a Pan-STARRS1 r skycell stack is linearised from its asinh scale within 1e-6 relative of astropy, with its `BLANK` pixels as no data, and takes its `.wt` weight as error and its `.mask` file with its `MSKNAM` names as flags, with the `FPA.ZP` zero point.
- Tested on synthetic files only: the eclipse fit (no real eclipse in the test data), `FLUXMAG0` zero points, the HSC mask layout, the DES coadd mask rule, PLIO, NOCOMPRESS and unsigned integer tiles, integer 1D arrays and a stated wavelength unit, and time-series files with padding, several exposures in one series or refused layouts.
- Cube Prep refuses an input whose error or mask layer is present but cannot be used (another size, unreadable, a floating-point mask) unless you switch that layer off in the Headers tool; a layer switched off, or an SDSS frame without a typed gain, goes on without it. Layer assignments last until the app closes.
- The built app was driven through a checklist of 52 steps on real files (26 for time series and spectra, 26 for survey images): 46 were run and 6 could not be (keyboard-only behaviour and a Gaia query while VizieR was down). The defects that run exposed were corrected and their corrections are covered by tests, and four of the six corrected time-series steps were seen again in the app after the correction.
- Two real-data comparisons on the Euclid VIS cutout of the test set miss their tolerance, and the data tests that hold them still fail: the empty-aperture depth is 25.80 AB against 25.61 from an independent computation (0.19 mag deeper, tolerance 0.1 mag; on the 601 pixel cutout only 352 apertures of 1 arcsec fit once the source mask is grown by 8 pixels, and they sit on the quietest sky), and the PSF FWHM is 0.190 arcsec against 0.177 (7.8 % wide, tolerance 5 %; four stars on an image sampled at 1.9 pixels per FWHM).

### Added
- Line measurement in the Spectroscopy panel uses the Redshift z of the line list: the Centroid, Sigma and FWHM rows give source-frame velocities (the centroid relative to z, the widths as c x sigma / centroid), a new Observer frame row keeps the values against the laboratory rest wavelength, and the line CSV gains `velocity_redshift`, `velocity_source_centroid_kms`, `velocity_source_sigma_kms`, `velocity_source_fwhm_kms`, `continuum_chi2_red` and `n_channels_nominal`. Photometry rows end with a note naming the unit of each value.

### Changed
- Air and vacuum wavelengths are converted with one relation, Morton (2000), for cube axes, 1D spectra, the spectrum plot and line velocities; 0.8.0 used it only for line rest wavelengths and another refractivity for the axes and the plot. An air wavelength of 0.5 µm now converts to 0.5001395 µm instead of 0.5001472 µm, a shift of 4.6 km/s, and agrees with astropy within 3e-8 (0.008 km/s).
- A 1D spectrum whose CTYPE1 is WAVE and whose header names no frame now reads as vacuum, as FITS defines WAVE; it read as air unless TELESCOP named JWST, HST or Roman, which put a line at its vacuum rest wavelength at 83.6 km/s near 5000 Å. The non-standard CTYPE1 values LAMB, WAVELENG, WAVELENGTH and LAMBDA follow the same rule, so ground-based files with CTYPE1 'Wavelength' (ISIS, BeSS), usually in air, must be switched to air in the Spectroscopy panel. AIRORVAC and VACUUM are now read, and when no card names the frame the frame line and the first note of a line measurement say that the frame was assumed.
- Line measurement without an ERR plane: the flux error now includes the uncertainty of the continuum level and slope, with the residual scatter on the correct degrees of freedom. It was 2.0 to 3.0 times smaller than the scatter of repeated noise realisations and now agrees within 7 %, so the S/N drops by 1.25 to 3 times depending on the windows. With an ERR plane the error is scaled by the square root of the continuum's reduced chi-square when that exceeds 1, the reduced chi-square is reported, and a note says when ERR understates or overstates the continuum scatter.
- Line measurement of an absorption line reports its trough: the extremum row is labelled Trough and `peak` and `peak_channel` hold the deepest channel. The equivalent width is undefined, with a note, when the continuum is negative across the line. The km/s widths of the Sigma and FWHM rows are c x sigma / centroid, which at z = 0 differs from 0.8.0 by the ratio of rest wavelength to centroid (0.1 % at 300 km/s).
- Photometry: the FWHM no longer depends on where the star falls within a pixel and is the FWHM of the star image; a 1.5 px Gaussian centred on a pixel, an edge and a corner read 0.50, 1.50 and 2.13 px and now reads 1.66 px in all three, and the automatic aperture of 1.5 x FWHM follows. A FWHM that cannot be measured is empty instead of 0 (shown as --), and the automatic aperture then falls back to 6 px with a warning. The aperture correction is now the multiplicative factor total over net flux (1.076 where 0.8.0 showed 0.929); the old number is shown as EE in aperture, and the total flux and total AB magnitude are unchanged.
- Gaia cross-match: the zero-point error is now the standard error of the zero point (the intercept at BP-RP = 0 with the colour term, the error of a median without it) instead of the rms over the square root of the star count; in simulation the ratio of the actual scatter to the reported error goes from 2.93 to 1.05. With one fitted star the error is empty instead of 0.
- Star detection FWHM no longer grows with brightness for non-Gaussian stars: a Moffat (beta 2.5) star of 4 px FWHM at 10, 100 and 1000 times the detection threshold read 4.78, 5.86 and 6.73 px and now reads 4.17 px. This moves the Plate Solve star list, the median FWHM of Subframe Selector and Stack (a saved Max FWHM limit accepts more frames), SPCC apertures and Gaia tie weights; a clipped star now reports the width at half its clipped maximum, which is wider. Star masks keep their 0.8.0 size.
- PSF estimation no longer uses stars whose FWHM cannot be measured, such as saturated stars with a hollow core, which were listed with FWHM 4.00 px and ranked first; the PSF panel, deconvolution with an estimated PSF and the proposed stars of the empirical PSF change with it (on a NIRCam frame with two such stars the average FWHM goes from 2.317 to 2.179 px). Stars wider than about 7 px measure their sky beyond the star, so 10, 12 and 15 px stars read 10.1, 12.1 and 15.0 px instead of 8.9, 9.8 and 10.5 px.
- Regions with a linked background: the net flux has an error that adds the uncertainty of the background level to the pixel noise (the ERR plane, or the background scatter without one), the S/N is the net sum over that error instead of over the pixel noise alone, and the Jy and AB errors derive from it and are present without an ERR plane. For a region as large as its annulus the reported error was 1.6 times below the actual scatter and is now 1.13 times below; for a star-sized aperture 1.08 becomes 1.03. The net cell shows the error and the region CSV gains `net_err` and `net_err_terms`.
- On rectangular or sheared pixels the pixel area is the determinant of the CD matrix instead of the square of the mean scale, so region areas in arcsec², surface brightness and the surface brightness profile change (a 0.29 x 0.68 arcsec pixel covers 0.1972 arcsec², not 0.2352, a shift of 0.19 mag/arcsec²); images with PIXAR_A2 keep that area. The empirical PSF of such an image takes the mean axis scale the other tools report, so its pixel scale, FWHM in arcsec and PIXSCALE change and kernels built from it pass the scale check of Cube Prep. Square pixels are unchanged.

### Fixed

- Observation geometry and time series: a header whose time is already at the barycentre through the FITS 4.0 card `TREFPOS = 'BARYCENTER'` keeps that time as BJD_TDB instead of getting the light-travel time a second time (up to 8.3 min either way; -455 s for an equatorial target on 2023-02-25), so the BJD_TDB axis, Save CSV and the ExoClock `bjd_tdb` column carry the header value, and JD_UTC and HJD_UTC are refused as they are for TIMEREF/BJDREF headers. TREFPOS is read by its first three letters, as the FITS standard allows, so `BARYCENT`, `TOPOCENT`, `GEOCENTR` and `TOPOCENTRIC` are understood; TREFPOS `TOPOCENTER` and `GEOCENTER`, TIMEREF `LOCAL`, `GEOCENTRIC` and `SOLARSYSTEM`, and BJDREFI are read as before.
- Time references that cannot be trusted are refused instead of corrected once too often: a header whose reference keywords disagree (TREFPOS `TOPOCENTER` with TIMEREF `SOLARSYSTEM` or with BJDREFI, or TREFPOS `BARYCENTER` with TIMEREF `LOCAL`), that names a position AstroBurst does not convert (`HELIOCENTER`, `RELOCATABLE`, `CUSTOM`, a planet, or an unknown TREFPOS or TIMEREF value), or whose TIMESYS is TCB with nothing placing the time at the barycentre, gets no time scales or light-time correction, with a note naming the cards. TIMESYS TDB with nothing placing the time at the barycentre is read as TDB at the observer, with the light-time term added and a note saying so, and a barycentric time in UTC, TT or TAI, which the FITS standard does not pair with the barycentre, is converted to TDB with a note.
- Header dates: an impossible calendar date such as `2026-02-30`, `2026-04-31`, `2025-02-29` or `1900-02-29`, in the ISO form or the old `DD/MM/YY` form, is refused instead of silently becoming a later day (`2026-02-30` was read as 2 March); leap years follow the Gregorian century rule (2000 is a leap year, 1900 and 2100 are not). - Time series: when a frame's header time was refused, the disabled ETD and ExoClock exports quote the reason (for example the time reference keywords that disagree) instead of "DATE-OBS missing or not a valid date".
- Headless server: an image replaced in a slot through the v1 API (open, stack, drizzle or pipeline) no longer leaves the v2 image listing, HDU switch or WCS route describing the old one; after a v2 open of a 4x4 image and a v1 open of an 8x8 image under the same name, the listing still gave the first file and its size, and an HDU switch reopened the first file.

- Plate Solve: stars are detected once per solve however many catalogue tiers are installed, so too few stars is reported once instead of once per tier, and an unusable scale hint is named once as a hint problem instead of as a failure of every tier. ### Added

- Live IFU kinematic maps ("Line fit maps" in Analysis > Cube > Spectrum, button Fit lines): with a line range brushed on the spectrum and a rest wavelength set, every spaxel of the cube is fitted with a Gaussian over a continuum fixed from the two side windows (the continuum windows set on the spectrum, or default windows beside the range), in a fraction of a second on a NIRSpec IFU cube, with ERR-propagated errors and DQ masking, inside the viewer; the result is one 2-D map per plane, `flux` (A·σ_v·√(2π) in `<BUNIT> km/s`), `velocity`, `sigma_obs`, `sigma_corr` (only with a resolving power), `flux_err`, `v_err`, `sigma_err`, `chi2_red`, `snr`, `mask` and `ncomp`, each shown in the viewer from its plane button; Weight by ERR (on by default) weights each channel by 1/ERR² from the cube's `ERR` HDU and reports absolute-sigma errors, inflated by √χ²_red where χ²_red > 1 (without ERR the weights come from the continuum scatter and the formal errors are scaled by √(χ²/dof)), and every error includes the continuum uncertainty, the flux error also the amplitude-sigma covariance; Mask DQ channels (on by default) drops channels flagged `DO_NOT_USE` (bit 0) in the `DQ` HDU from the line and the continuum fits; the continuum is linear when both side windows hold at least 3 usable channels and falls back to a constant per spaxel otherwise (mask bit 2), so no slope is extrapolated from one window; an optional resolving power R (never prefilled) gives `sigma_corr` with the instrumental width removed (NaN and mask bit 16 where the line is unresolved), otherwise sigma is labelled observed; Components `two` or `auto` (default `one`) adds a two-Gaussian fit where the single fit passes the S/N threshold and at least 8 channels are usable, kept only when the centres are at least one channel apart, each component is wider than the sigma lower bound and narrower than a quarter of the window, carries at least 10 % of the flux and has its centre off the window bounds, and in `auto` also when ΔBIC/max(1, χ²_red) ≥ 10 (ΔBIC of the single fit over the pair, divided by the single fit's χ²_red so a misfitting single Gaussian does not accept every pair) and both amplitudes are at least 3σ; accepted pairs fill twelve `c1_*`/`c2_*` planes (c1 bluer, c2 redder, NaN where `ncomp` is 1) while the main planes keep the single fit; with Emission only off, absorption lines are mapped too (negative flux and S/N, mask bit 1 when |S/N| reaches the threshold) and an absorption pair is split into two negative components; mask bits: 1 fitted at or above the S/N threshold (default 3), 2 constant continuum, 4 channels dropped by DQ, 8 channels dropped by a non-finite or non-positive ERR, 16 unresolved, 32 fit did not converge, 64 two components rejected or not attempted, 128 two components accepted; velocities are in the axis frame named by `SPECSYS`, with no barycentric or heliocentric correction, and the notes say so together with the single-Gaussian caveat; each plane is written as `{stem}_linefit_{plane}_{z0}-{z1}.fits` (plus a PNG) in the output folder with the celestial WCS kept, `BUNIT` per plane and the `LINEFIT`, `LFREST`, `LFZ0`, `LFZ1`, `LFCONV`, `LFWEIGHT`, `LFERRHDU`, `LFDQHDU`, `LFRPOW`, `LFSIGMA`, `LFNCOMP`, `LFSNR`, `LFCONTA` and `LFCONTB` cards, and the `mask` plane also carries `LFMASKA`, `LFMASKB` (the bit meanings) and `LFMASKC` (mask 0 with `ncomp` ≥ 1: converged below the S/N threshold; `ncomp` 0: no fit); line windows shorter than 5 or longer than 400 channels are refused; each run is added to the measurement log as a `line_fit` entry with its window, continuum windows, weighting, ERR and DQ HDUs, threshold, components and resolving power (`cube_line_fit_cmd`, `cube_line_fit_spaxel_cmd`; ERR and DQ are found by `EXTNAME` with the cube's geometry)
- Spaxel inspect for the line-fit maps: a click on a displayed line-fit plane refits that spaxel with the same settings (`cube_line_fit_spaxel_cmd`) and shows a 150 px mini plot of its spectrum over the fitted span, drawn only from the arrays the backend returns: the samples used in the fit, channels outside the windows in grey, DQ- or ERR-dropped channels as crosses, ERR bars, the line window and the continuum windows as bands, the continuum dashed, each component dashed in blue (c1) and red (c2) on top of the continuum, the total model solid and a dotted marker labelled with the velocity of each component; below it a table with V, σ (observed, or corrected with the observed value in brackets), flux, amplitude, χ²_red with its degrees of freedom, S/N, ncomp, ΔBIC (raw and divided by max(1, χ²_red)), the dropped channels, the continuum (linear or constant, channels, level, scatter), the fit status (converged, iterations, weighting, mask) and the c1 (blue) and c2 (red) components; the plot has a text summary for screen readers
- Rest-frame line list: Brα 4.052262 µm, Brβ 2.625872 µm, Pfβ 4.653778 µm and He I 2.058690 µm (vacuum), 27 built-in lines in all
- Line-fit planes open with a matching display: the velocity planes (`velocity`, `c1_velocity`, `c2_velocity`) in RdBu inverted (negative velocities blue) with limits symmetric about 0, a linear stretch and percentile limits; the flux, width, error, `chi2_red` and `snr` planes in viridis; `mask` and `ncomp` in gray with min/max limits; this is a temporary override that is never saved (changes made while it is on screen are not saved either), and the previous display returns when the viewer leaves the line-fit maps: on another result, on Revert to original, on a file switch and after a reload
- Synth stack frames vary from frame to frame by default (Vary frames, stack mode only): a dither of up to ±3 px (Dither (px), 0-20), a seeing jitter of ±10 % on the FWHM (Seeing jitter %, 0-50), ±5 % sky and transparency jitter and cosmic rays (Cosmic rays toggle; round(25 × megapixels) rays per frame, each 1-3 pixels of 5,000-50,000 e-, added after the noise), each frame rendered with its own offset and PSF width; frame 0 is the reference (no offset, jitter or cosmic rays) and equals the single image of the same settings, and with Vary frames off the frames keep identical stars and differ only in noise, as before (`frame_variation` block in the config, every field optional)
- Synth stacks write a `{prefix}_frames.csv` manifest next to the frames (`frame`, `dx`, `dy`, `fwhm_factor`, `sky_factor`, `transparency`, `n_cosmic_rays`, `date_obs`), listed on the result card as Frames manifest (`frames_manifest_path` in the response)
- Synth panel: a dice button next to Seed (New random seed) picks a new seed in 0-9999 (the same seed still reproduces the output byte for byte), and Open in viewer on a single-image result adds the generated FITS to the file list, or selects it when it is already open
- Synth provenance cards: every generated frame carries `SYNSEED`, `SYNFIELD`, `SYNPSF`, `SYNFWHM` (the rendered FWHM, including the frame's seeing jitter) and `RDNOISE`, stack frames also `SYNFRAME`, `SYNDX`, `SYNDY` and `SYNOFFS = 'star = catalog + (SYNDX, SYNDY) px'`
- JWST Level-1b `_uncal` ramps and Roman L1 resultant stacks open as ramps: the frame navigation reads "group g of N, integration i of M" (ArrowLeft/ArrowRight step the group, Shift steps by 10), the Ramp section in Analysis > Cube shows the instrument, readout pattern, groups x integrations, the NIRSpec IRS2 layout and the group time (`TGROUP`, else `TFRAME x (NFRAMES + GROUPGAP)`), lists the GROUP and INT_TIMES tables, and a click on a pixel plots its raw and reference-corrected ramp with the fitted line and the saturated, unused and jump-flagged groups (`ramp_info_cmd`, `ramp_pixel_series_cmd`, `ramp_tables_cmd`, `ramp_frame_cmd`, `ramp_pixel_fit_cmd`)
- Quick slope for JWST `_uncal` ramps (Analysis > Cube > Ramp, Run quick slope): one integration becomes `<stem>_qslope.fits` with SCI (DN/s), NGOOD, DQ and NOISE extensions, offline and without reference files, after stripping the NIRSpec IRS2 reference output and interleaved reference rows and subtracting a per-amplifier reference offset, with saturation by threshold (`sat_dn`), crude jump flagging (`jump_k`) and an OLS or median slope; it is a decision aid, not a rate (no superbias, linearity, dark, FFT reference-pixel correction or weighted fit; the header carries `ABPROC = qslope` and never `CAL_VER`, `S_RAMP` or `DATAMODL`); Compare with rate reads the official `_rate.fits` next to it (or one you pick) and reports the zero-shift alignment check, the quick-minus-official deltas per rate bin and per amplifier, JUMP_DET and SATURATED recall and precision, a verdict against the published tolerances and a ratio image (`ramp_quick_slope_cmd`, `ramp_compare_rate_cmd`); measured on 8 program-1266 NIRSpec IRS2 exposures: faint additive offset within 0.011 DN/s full frame and 0.021 DN/s per amplifier, +1.2 to +2.7 % at 1-30 DN/s (details in `docs/quick-slope.md`)

### Changed
- The Analysis panel is split into three tabs: Image (histogram, statistics, pixel table, regions, profiles, contours, FFT, Deep Zoom), Sources (stars, photometry, photometry table, time series, observation geometry, catalog, targets) and Cube (ramp, spectrum, PV), with the measurement log in a collapsible "Measurement log (N)" footer under them (collapsed by default, its open state kept for the session); the chip row jumps to the panels of the active tab only; panels in a hidden tab stay mounted, so their settings and results survive a tab switch, but ignore viewer clicks (Measure on image click and Read on image click do nothing while their tab is hidden, the ramp pixel plot does not refetch, and Deep Zoom closes); a cube or ramp opens on Cube and any other file on Image, the tab you pick is remembered per file for the session (with a fallback to the default when that tab is unavailable), Cube is disabled with "Open a data cube or a ramp" when the file has neither, switching tabs scrolls to the top of the new tab, and the tab bar works from the keyboard (arrow keys wrap and skip the disabled tab, Home and End)
- Synth: the exposure slider reads "Exposure (s)" with the hint "sets EXPTIME and dark current; star flux and sky are per-frame totals" (star flux and sky have been per-frame totals since 0.6.1; only dark current scales with the exposure)
- Synth stacks: without an explicit `cadence_seconds` the cadence is the exposure plus 10 s, so DATE-OBS and MJD-OBS no longer describe overlapping exposures (it was a fixed 60 s against the default 300 s exposure); an explicit cadence is still honoured
- Synth seeds: the panel sends one seed and the backend derives the field, noise, flat, frame and cosmic-ray streams from it (SplitMix64 mix of the seed, the purpose and the frame index) instead of `seed + 1000` and `seed + 7919 × frame`; a `noise.seed` sent by an older caller is mixed in rather than used raw; every seed now gives a different image than before
- Synth: Stars is the number of stars inside the frame: King and disk fields redraw a position until it lands on the frame (a disk that cannot fit is refused with an error instead of hanging), so the catalog and the result card list only in-frame stars (a 500-star disk on a 512×512 frame used to put 242 of them off the image and still count them)
- A 4-D FITS image (`NAXIS4` > 1) with `NGROUPS`/`NINTS` cards, or `DATAMODL` `Level1bModel`/`RampModel`, opens as a ramp instead of a plain cube of NINTS x NGROUPS planes labelled by channel, and the primary header supplies the ramp cards (the WCS and units still come from the `SCI` header)

### Fixed
- Synth images all looked alike: since 0.6.1 star flux is total electrons per frame, but the default flux range stayed at 100-50,000 e-, so about 97 % of the stars sat below the noise (14-16 of 500 above 5σ at the defaults) whatever the distribution, PSF, star count or flux max; the defaults are now 2,000-500,000 e- in the panel and in the Rust `FieldConfig::default`, and a test checks that at least half of the stars peak above 5σ of the background noise at the default settings
- Synth vignetting multiplied only the star image, so the sky never darkened towards the corners; the flat now multiplies stars and sky (`(star + sky) × flat + dark`, never dark current or bias), and the ground truth stays the noise-free, pre-flat star image without sky, which its new `SYNTHGT` card states
- Regenerating a synthetic image over a file open in a viewer tab kept showing the old image; `generate_synth_cmd` outputs (the FITS and, when saved, the ground truth) are now announced, also when the command fails after writing them, so the open tab reloads
- Synth stack mode ignored Save star catalog (.csv) and Save ground truth; it now writes `{prefix}_catalog.csv` (frame 0 positions) and `{prefix}_groundtruth.fits` (frame 0) when they are on (`generate_synth_stack_cmd` takes `save_catalog` and `save_ground_truth`, default false)
- The flat's pixel-response pattern changed on every synth stack frame; one flat is computed per stack and used by every frame
- Synth stacks of seeds 7919 apart shared their noise realisations (frame i of seed s used the noise seed of frame 0 of seed s + 7919 × i)
- The Synth result card showed the live slider size instead of the generated one; it now shows the width and height of the generated image and says "Settings changed since generation" when a parameter changes afterwards

## [0.5.8] - 2026-09-11

Code audit of the math, GPU flow, image-processing and performance paths (21 module groups, every finding reproduced by hand before being fixed), plus a validation of the ASDF reader against the ASDF Standard 1.5/1.6.

### Fixed

#### Math / processing core
- Drizzle Lanczos3 kernel was evaluated in output-pixel units, so at scale 3 (and any integer scale with zero offsets) 8/9 of the output pixels received zero weight and rendered as holes; the kernel argument and support now scale with the output grid
