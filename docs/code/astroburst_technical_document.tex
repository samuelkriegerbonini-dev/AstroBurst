%% ============================================================================
    \section{Executive Summary}
%% ============================================================================

    AstroBurst is a cross-platform desktop application for viewing, measuring, processing and composing astronomical FITS and ASDF images. The processing runs in Rust, the interface is drawn by React, and Tauri packages both as a desktop application. A WebGPU shader applies the display stretch and colormap; that is the only use of the GPU, and every algorithm also runs without it.

    The application targets two user segments:
    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item Professional astronomers working with observatory data (JWST, Hubble, Roman Space Telescope, ground-based IFU instruments)
    \item Advanced astrophotographers processing deep-sky narrowband and broadband imaging data
    \end{itemize}

    Release 0.7.0 exposes \textbf{\nCommands{} backend commands} through a typed TypeScript service layer of \nServices{} modules, a ten-step compose wizard, a processing tab whose steps form a chain with a persistent history, and an archive browser for MAST. The same core is served over HTTP by an optional headless server (Section~\ref{sec:server}), so region statistics, rendering, histograms, cutouts, WCS queries and batch stacking are reachable from a script without a display. At release the library suite runs \nLibTests{} tests (plus \nLibIgnored{} real-data and timing tests marked \code{\#[ignore]}), the server binary adds \nServerTests{} and the frontend suite runs \nVitestTests{} cases in \nVitestFiles{} files (Section~\ref{sec:testing}).

    The releases after 0.6.0 moved the project in three directions. Measurement: aperture and batch photometry with a header-derived gain model, calibrated region fluxes, line measurement and per-spaxel line-fit maps, position-velocity diagrams and time-series photometry, all logged. Instrument-level data: JWST \code{\_uncal} ramps with a quick-look slope, pipeline \code{x1d} tables, tabulated spectral axes, and the JWST and Roman generalized WCS (gWCS) evaluated from its own transform chain instead of an approximation. Integrity of the astrophotography path: calibration frames are checked against each other before any master is built, channels are aligned through their WCS, and frames that cannot be registered are left out of a stack and reported, never stacked silently.

    Versions 0.6.9 to 0.7.0 add a fourth direction: getting data and keeping work. An Archive mode searches MAST, shows the previews it publishes, draws dataset footprints on the open image and downloads products with resume and revalidation (Section~\ref{sec:mast}); every processing step on a file is recorded in a persistent history with undo and redo (Section~\ref{sec:history}); cubes and 1D spectra carry their errors into line measurements (Sections~\ref{sec:lineerr_math} and \ref{sec:spec1d}). The same releases ship the shared foundations of offline plate solving (a HEALPix catalogue reader, a catalogue store, a polynomial distortion fitter, a camera table and keyword aliases, Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations}); the solver itself is not part of 0.7.0, and no Gaia data is bundled.

    \subsection{What's New in v0.6.9 to v0.7.0}
    \label{sec:new_070}

    Version 0.6.9 (2026-10-16) hardened the FITS reader and shipped the MAST client without its screen; 0.6.9.5 and 0.7.0 (both 2026-10-17) added the archive browser, downloads, the processing history, error products and the foundations of offline plate solving. The technical sections give the rules.

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Archive} (Section~\ref{sec:mast})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{MAST client (0.6.9)}: Search, product lists, image lists, thumbnails, previews and product retrieval for ten missions, with name lookup, query building, a disk cache of resized thumbnails and previews, cancellation, rate limits and a Roman token that is checked against MAST before it is stored and never returned to the interface.
        \item \textbf{Archive browser (0.6.9.5)}: An Archive mode beside the Workspace (footer switch, Ctrl+1 and Ctrl+2). JWST and HST are searched by target name, coordinates, programme or dataset id with quick filters; results show as a gallery or a dense table; the detail pane holds the previews MAST publishes, the metadata and the product list. Every image slot shows the cause of a MAST failure, with a Retry, instead of an empty frame.
        \item \textbf{Downloads (0.7.0)}: Ticked product files download two at a time into the \code{mast} folder of the Data folder, resume after a dropped connection or a Cancel, are checked against MAST instead of fetched again, and are measured against the quota and the free disk space before anything is sent; Open loads images, cubes, spectra and ramps into the Workspace.
        \item \textbf{Presets, footprints and header peek (0.7.0)}: Seven JWST search presets; dataset footprints (\code{s\_region}) drawn on the open image through its own WCS and saved as DS9 regions; a byte-range read of a product's headers that gives HST datasets, which list no \code{s\_region}, a footprint before any download.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Processing history (0.6.9.5)} (Section~\ref{sec:history}): Every Processing step on a single file is recorded with its settings in the app data folder's \code{history/}; Ctrl+Z steps back and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y forward, the step on screen comes back after a restart, a History tool lists the steps, and steps removed by Clean up are rebuilt by replay.
    \item \textbf{Spectra and cubes} (Sections~\ref{sec:lineerr_math} and \ref{sec:spec1d})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Cube error products (0.6.9.5, interface in 0.7.0)}: Region spectra carry the error propagated from the cube's ERR extension, collapse and moment maps get error planes and are written as SCI + ERR FITS, and an empirical noise factor is measured on line-free channels. The Cube tool draws the error as a band and the scaled error as an outline.
        \item \textbf{1D spectra as documents (0.7.0)}: JWST \code{x1d} tables and 1D FITS arrays with a wavelength axis are spectrum rows in the Files panel; the Cube tool plots them, measures lines with their FLUX\_ERROR or ERR as weights and their DQ-flagged channels left out, states whether the wavelengths are air or vacuum, exports and reopens them as CSV, and compares them with a second spectrum.
        \item \textbf{Line measurement errors (0.6.9.5, 0.7.0)}: Every line measurement lists its error source, the pixel, level and slope terms of the flux error and the channels left out.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{FITS and ASDF input} (Section~\ref{sec:fitsio})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Tile compression through fitsio-pure (0.6.9)}: Reads are bit-identical to the previous reader on the test files and 3.4 to 5.6 times faster on 26\,MP files with 64 threads; HCOMPRESS\_1, PLIO\_1, any tile shape and SUBTRACTIVE\_DITHER\_2 open; malformed or hostile files are refused with a message naming the cause.
        \item \textbf{Gzip-compressed FITS (0.6.9.5, pickers in 0.7.0)}: \code{.fits.gz}, \code{.fit.gz} and \code{.fts.gz} files are decompressed as a stream capped by the memory budget.
        \item \textbf{ASDF (0.6.9)}: float16 arrays, \code{fitswcs\_imaging} transforms in pure ASDF files, Roman metadata cards and the Roman WFI pivot wavelengths.
        \item \textbf{Unknown DQ planes (0.6.9)}: A DQ or MASK plane from an instrument the app does not know shows raw bit numbers and excludes nothing.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Astrophotography} (Sections~\ref{sec:triangles_math} and \ref{sec:cfa})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Alignment at any rotation (0.7.0)}: Star alignment registers frames rotated by any angle, meridian-flip halves included, and leaves a mirrored frame out with the reason.
        \item \textbf{CFA model and extraction (0.7.0)}: One model for Bayer and X-Trans, a luminance bin for plate solving, and Debayer modes that extract Green, H$\alpha$, H$\alpha$ + OIII or the four CFA planes at half resolution with a scaled WCS and an \code{ABCFAEXT} card.
        \item \textbf{Keyword aliases (0.7.0)}: MaxIm DL, N.I.N.A. and SharpCap spellings of pixel size, focal length, temperature, binning, frame type and pointing.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Offline plate solving foundations (0.7.0)} (Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations}): A Siril HEALPix catalogue reader, a catalogue store with resumable, verified installs (commands only; none of its entries can be installed yet), a polynomial distortion fitter with holdout order selection and a camera table of 55 entries. Neither a solver nor an interface for them ships in 0.7.0.
    \item \textbf{Licences (0.6.9)} (Section~\ref{sec:deps}): \code{src-tauri/licenses/THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.md} and the licence texts it names are bundled with the desktop installers and the Linux tarball.
    \item \textbf{Interface (0.6.9.5)}: The progress strip at the top of the workspace collapses about 2.5\,s after a batch completes and comes back for the next one.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{What's New in v0.6.7}
    \label{sec:new_067}

    Version 0.6.7.0 is a correctness and integrity round. Each item below names what was wrong or missing before; the technical sections give the rules.

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Astrometry and WCS} (Sections~\ref{sec:wcs}, \ref{sec:gwcs_math}, \ref{sec:sipfit_math})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Celestial axis pair required} --- A header without \code{CTYPE1}/\code{CTYPE2}, with a non-celestial second axis, with latitude on axis 1 or with two different projection codes is refused with a reason. Before, a missing \code{CTYPE1} was read as \code{RA-{}-{}-TAN} and \code{CTYPE2} was never read, so a 2-D spectrum without \code{CTYPE} received invented sky coordinates.
        \item \textbf{SIP fit residual} --- \code{SIPMXERR}/\code{SIPIVERR} are read, reported by \code{get\_wcs\_info} and the server \code{wcs} route, shown in the Info orientation line, grouped under ``WCS / Astrometry'' in the Header Explorer, rescaled by drizzle and resample, and treated as WCS cards when a WCS is replaced.
        \item \textbf{gWCS evaluator} --- The JWST \code{\_cal} gWCS (read from the \code{ASDF} extension of the FITS file) and the Roman L2 \code{roman.meta.wcs} are evaluated exactly: polynomial distortion, mappings, 3-D rotation sequences and the TAN projection, with an exact inverse by Newton iteration on the sphere. Roman \code{\_cal.asdf} files, which previously opened without any WCS, now have sky coordinates, a grid and catalog overlays; JWST \code{\_cal} files use the gWCS instead of the header TAN-SIP and report the difference between the two.
        \item \textbf{TAN-SIP fit for derived FITS} --- A Roman image gets a TAN-SIP header fitted to its gWCS (degree chosen automatically to 0.01\,px), so cutouts, crops and exports carry a valid header WCS; a JWST cutout receives the pipeline's own residual cards.
        \item \textbf{Arithmetic gWCS operators} --- The astropy compound operators (\code{add}, \code{subtract}, \code{multiply}, \code{divide}, \code{power}) and \code{Const1D}/\code{Const2D} are evaluated, so tweakreg-corrected \code{\_crf} products use their gWCS instead of falling back to the header SIP.
        \item \textbf{Plate solving} --- Solve and Write WCS run on the image being measured (a stack or a processed result), not on the original file; every GET to nova.astrometry.net is retried on transient failures (Section~\ref{sec:platesolve}).
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Measurement} (Section~\ref{sec:measure})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Measurements follow the image on screen} --- The Info panel sky readout reads the analysis target and hides itself for a target without a celestial WCS (a PV diagram); Statistics, region statistics, profiles and the line sky readout re-run when a processing step rewrites the same file; a click on a result whose grid differs from the cube (a PV diagram) no longer extracts the spectrum of an unrelated spaxel.
        \item \textbf{Gain model for photometry} --- The gain field is prefilled from the header (\code{EGAIN}, \code{ATODGAIN}, \code{CCDGAIN}, \code{ADCGAIN}, never the camera \code{GAIN} setting), scaled for AstroBurst stacks by \code{NCOMBINE} and the combine method, and replaced by the ERR plane when there is one. Click, batch and time-series photometry warn when the errors leave out source photon noise (Section~\ref{sec:gain_math}).
        \item \textbf{Filters in Rust} --- A Rust mirror of the filter-wavelength table (74 codes, NIRISS F158M and F380M and 14 HST broadband codes added), a parity test against the TypeScript table, and the \code{PUPIL} rule in the backend, so a time series no longer mixes an F444W+F470N frame with an F444W frame.
        \item \textbf{Catalog disk cache} --- Gaia DR3 cones are cached on disk (64\,MiB, oldest first) and served memory $\to$ disk $\to$ network; the VizieR endpoint and the cache directory can be overridden; click photometry matches against one field cone instead of one request per star; SPCC uses the same cache.
        \item \textbf{Tables and \code{-TAB} axes} --- Fixed-repeat array columns of binary tables are read (with \code{TDIM}); a \code{WAVE-TAB} spectral axis is evaluated from its \code{WCS-TABLE} per FITS WCS Paper III (Section~\ref{sec:tab_math}).
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Calibration and stacking} (Section~\ref{sec:calstack})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Frame-card checks} --- Binning, \code{GAIN}, \code{OFFSET}, dark exposures, flat filters and \code{IMAGETYP} are checked before any master is built, in the Calibration tab, the Pipeline and the server; mismatches that corrupt the result are refused, the others warned.
        \item \textbf{Dark scale from exposures} --- The Calibration tab no longer asks for a dark ratio; the scale is the exposure ratio when a bias is present and 1 otherwise. Exposure is read from the merged primary and SCI header, so a JWST \code{EXPOSURE=1} (a count) is no longer read as one second.
        \item \textbf{Flat-darks and temperature groups} --- Flat-darks are a fourth master; darks are grouped by sensor temperature and each light takes the nearest group.
        \item \textbf{Provenance cards} --- Stack, Pipeline and drizzle outputs write \code{NCOMBINE}, \code{EXPTIME}, \code{TOTEXP}, \code{ABCOMB}, \code{ABREJECT}, \code{ABNORM} and \code{ABDRZSCL}.
        \item \textbf{Combine correctness} --- Noise weights are divided by the square of the normalization scale; frames of different sizes are refused without alignment; the cosmetic zero-MAD fallback uses the 90th percentile; the Masked Stretch shadow guard no longer depends on the data range; cosmetic correction detects Bayer data from the header.
        \item \textbf{Unregistered frames excluded} --- The batch Pipeline leaves out every frame it could not register and reports each one by its position in the channel; an unknown alignment method name sent to Stack, Compose Align or the aligned-channel export is an error instead of a silent phase correlation.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Compose} (Section~\ref{sec:compose})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Match levels by median and MAD} --- Each channel is scaled to the largest MAD and shifted to the reference median before blending; the composite FITS has balanced planes (Section~\ref{sec:levels_math}).
        \item \textbf{STF link rule} --- The preview STF is unlinked when the composite planes' medians differ by more than three sigma or by a ratio above 1.5.
        \item \textbf{SPCC at each channel's wavelength} --- The blackbody model is evaluated at the effective wavelength of each input filter instead of fixed 640/530/460\,nm, and the option is labelled ``SPCC (blackbody approximation)''.
        \item \textbf{Align by WCS} --- Channels with a celestial WCS are reprojected onto the reference grid through the WCS, the residual is measured on point sources, and the reference is the finest pixel scale unless the user picks one (Section~\ref{sec:reproject_math}).
        \item \textbf{Run tokens, crop and export} --- Wizard cache keys carry a per-run token so a late Align cannot overwrite a newer one; a stack that finishes after a Reset is discarded; Crop works on a single channel; star removal no longer marks Stretch done; Export shows and records the fixed channel mapping.
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Processing} (Section~\ref{sec:processing})
        \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Curves and Levels on a file} --- A Curves panel applies levels and a tone curve to a mono or RGB FITS as a chain step.
        \item \textbf{De-banding on a file} --- The Background panel offers the de-band modes for a single frame and refuses dispersed exposures.
        \item \textbf{Rotate and flip} --- Rotations by 90, 180 and 270 degrees and flips with an exact WCS update, including the SIP polynomials (Section~\ref{sec:geometry_math}).
        \end{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Interface} --- A first-run coachmark explains how to dock tool windows vertically and horizontally (Section~\ref{sec:dock}).
    \item \textbf{Headless server} --- The same WCS loader (gWCS included), the frame-card checks and flat-darks in \code{pipeline/run}, the new WCS keys on the \code{wcs} route, and the catalog cache initialised at start (Section~\ref{sec:server}).
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{What's New in v0.5.8}

    Version 0.5.8 is an audit release: the math, GPU flow, image-processing and performance paths were reviewed across 21 module groups with every finding reproduced by hand before being fixed, and the ASDF reader was validated against the ASDF Standard 1.5/1.6.

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Drizzle kernel} --- The Lanczos3 kernel was evaluated in output-pixel units, so at scale 3 (and any integer scale with zero offsets) 8/9 of the output pixels received zero weight and rendered as holes; the kernel argument and support now scale with the output grid
    \item \textbf{Sigma-clip floor} --- Sigma-clip combine and the batch pipeline used a 1e-10 sigma floor when the MAD is 0 (more than half the samples tied), rejecting every value that differed from the median; they now fall back to the mean absolute deviation and skip rejection when the scale is truly zero
    \item \textbf{Star removal} --- The soft star mask was applied twice, since push-pull fill already blends by the mask, leaving $m - m^2$ of the star flux at every mask edge
    \item \textbf{GHS stretch} --- $D = 0$ returned the raw, un-normalized data instead of the normalized identity, producing a white preview and export once the composite was cached
    \item \textbf{Wavelet reconstruction} --- Negative pixels were clamped to 0 and NaN was rewritten as 0
    \item \textbf{Phase correlation} --- A confident coarse shift was discarded whenever the fixed centre crop was featureless, falling back to identity or noise; the coarse estimate is now kept when the refine pass has no signal
    \item \textbf{Star detection} --- Saturated flat-top stars were deblended into several detections; equal-valued adjacent maxima now collapse to a single seed
    \item \textbf{GPU/CPU preview parity} --- IPC preview extrema were computed over all finite pixels while auto-STF came from padding-aware statistics, so the GPU render and the canonical PNG disagreed; NaN pixels were rewritten to 0.0 before upload and rendered gray; and linked STF on the GPU normalized each channel by its own range while the Rust path uses the combined range
    \item \textbf{WebGPU recovery} --- A failed initialisation (no adapter, device creation error) was cached for the whole session; the retry path re-probes
    \item \textbf{Compressed FITS tiles} --- Quantized GZIP\_1/GZIP\_2 tiles were decoded as raw f32 bit patterns (the integer width is now inferred from the tile byte count), ZBITPIX $\pm 64$ was rejected for every codec including AstroBurst's own GZIP\_2 files, the Rice decoder indexed the stream unchecked and panicked on short or corrupt tiles, and constant tiles quantized with scale 1 under SUBTRACTIVE\_DITHER\_1 gained $\pm 0.5$ noise on decode
    \item \textbf{ASDF block handling} --- Blocks are memory-mapped and decompressed lazily, so only the block behind the selected array is decoded (Roman L2 files carry six or more full-frame arrays), and uncompressed contiguous arrays are borrowed instead of copied three times
    \item \textbf{ASDF conformance} --- \code{\#ASDF BLOCK INDEX} trailers, CRLF line endings, tree padding, STREAMED blocks and \code{shape: ['*', ...]} are handled per spec; negative strides were silently read as contiguous; external block references now fail explicitly; gWCS chains under \code{roman.meta.wcs} are resolved with the \code{Shift} offset converted from 0-based gwcs pixels to 1-based \code{CRPIX}; YAML-tagged \code{meta} subtrees are flattened into the header instead of dropped; and allocation sizes from untrusted block headers are bounded
    \item \textbf{Headless server correctness} --- Region, histogram, cutout and pixel endpoints validated inputs only after arithmetic that could wrap or allocate unbounded memory; statistics and histograms silently discarded every pixel $\le$ 1e-7, giving wrong results for bias-subtracted or difference images; binning by a non-divisible factor averaged overlapping windows; viewport auto-STF from crop statistics was applied with global normalization; and re-opening an image under an existing name returned the stale cached array
    \item \textbf{Job state} --- Cancellation is observed by workers before results are published and terminal transitions are atomic, so a late worker can no longer overwrite \code{cancelled}
    \item \textbf{Memory and throughput} --- The batch stacking pipeline loads and stacks one channel at a time and returns 2048\,px previews instead of full-resolution base64 masters; drizzle scatters contributions directly into per-band accumulators in parallel instead of materialising every contribution as a tuple; and cube global statistics sample at most 32 frames with a pixel stride bounded to roughly 8M samples
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{Backend (Rust)}

    The library crate (\code{astroburst\_lib}) has five layers; two front ends sit on top of it.

    \begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=2cm]
    \item[\texttt{cmd/}] Tauri command handlers grouped by domain, built only with the \code{tauri} feature (\nCommands{} registered commands, Section~\ref{sec:commands}). Each handler receives deserialized arguments, moves the work off the UI thread, delegates to \code{core/} and returns JSON or a binary buffer.
    \item[\texttt{server/}] The headless HTTP front end, built only with the \code{server} feature (Section~\ref{sec:server}).
    \item[\texttt{core/}] Computation, in fifteen domains: \code{alignment} (phase correlation, the star-triangle matcher with its rotation rule, WCS reprojection with the point-source residual), \code{analysis} (histogram, FFT, star detection, aperture photometry, the gain model, line measurement with errors, x1d reader, 1D spectra, subframe metrics), \code{astrometry} (WCS wrapper, the gWCS evaluator and TAN-SIP fitter under \code{gwcs/}, coordinate frames, grid, spectral axes, time scales, observation geometry, Gaia catalog with its disk cache, the HEALPix catalogue reader, STC-S regions, SPCC), \code{compose} (channel blend, LRGB, white balance, RGB drizzle), \code{cube} (lazy and eager cubes, ERR and DQ companions, moments, PV diagrams, line-fit maps, the empirical noise factor), \code{history} (the history document, node ids, replay plans), \code{imaging} (STF and scale, colormaps, background and DBE, deband, wavelets, deconvolution, LHE, HDRMT, curves, geometry, regions, cutouts, contours, cosmetic correction, calibration pipeline, DQ flags, the CFA model and extraction), \code{mast} (MAST query building, missions, presets, product classification, previews, footprints, the error model), \code{metadata} (header discovery, photometric calibration, the filter-wavelength table, keyword aliases, the camera table), \code{ops} (the serialisable processing operations a history records), \code{pixelmath}, \code{ramp} (ramp inspection, IRS2 layout, quick slope, comparison with \code{\_rate}), \code{registration} (the polynomial distortion fitter), \code{stacking} (rejection kernel, calibration, frame cards, consistency checks, masters, CFA guard, drizzle) and \code{synth}.
    \item[\texttt{infra/}] Infrastructure: the Arc-based image cache with the ORIG/KEY layers of the composite, configuration, FITS I/O (memory-mapped reader, MEF scanner, tile compression through \crate{fitsio-pure} behind a header guard, gzip decompression, a header walker for byte-range reads, binary-table reader with array columns, the JWST \code{ASDF} extension reader, writer with multi-BITPIX output), the ASDF reader (YAML tree, blocks, zlib/bzip2/lz4) and \code{asdf\_bridge} (header synthesis, including the fitted TAN-SIP cards), \code{wcs\_source} (the single loader that chooses between a gWCS and the header WCS for a path), \code{net} (HTTP profiles, the resumable download engine, the Data folder and free-space queries), \code{mast} (the MAST client, cache, download queue and header peek), the catalogue store, the astrometry.net client, PNG and tile rendering, binary IPC encoding and progress events.
    \item[\texttt{types/}] Shared types: image references, headers, stacking and compose configuration, errors, and about 490 named constants in \code{types/constants.rs} (JSON keys, header card names, wizard cache keys).
    \item[\texttt{math/}] Primitives: NaN-safe median/MAD, sigma clipping, SIMD log approximation (Cephes degree-8 minimax, $<1$\,ULP), FFT and complex arithmetic, windows, sub-pixel peak interpolation, normalization and the bounded Gauss-Newton fitter.
    \end{description}

    \subsection{Non-Destructive Composite Pipeline (v0.4.5)}
    \label{sec:nondestructive}

    The composite cache uses a dual-layer architecture:

    \begin{infobox}[ORIG/KEY Cache Architecture]
        \begin{center}
            \begin{tabularx}{\linewidth}{llX}
                \toprule
                \textbf{Layer} & \textbf{Keys} & \textbf{Semantics} \\
                \midrule
                ORIG (immutable) & \code{\_\_composite\_orig\_r/g/b} & Written once by blend/compose/RGB-FITS load \\
                KEY (working) & \code{\_\_composite\_r/g/b} & Reconstructed: ORIG $\times$ WB $\to$ KEY, or ORIG $\times$ WB $\to$ SCNR $\to$ KEY \\
                \bottomrule
            \end{tabularx}
        \end{center}
        Every downstream operation (\code{calibrate\_composite\_cmd}, \code{apply\_scnr\_cmd}) reads from ORIG, applies the full transform chain, and writes to KEY. This makes WB and SCNR idempotent: applying the same factors twice produces identical results. \code{reset\_wb\_cmd} copies ORIG $\to$ KEY.
    \end{infobox}

    Since 0.6.4 the composite also has a processing chain of its own (\code{cmd::compose::composite\_chain}): while the colour composite is on screen, the Processing tab's background, denoise, PSF, deconvolution, stretch, masked stretch, LHE and HDRMT steps process its three planes, a re-run of a step drops the later ones, and ``Revert to original'' restores the composite as it was before the first step.

    \subsection{Frontend (React + TypeScript)}
    \label{sec:frontend}

    \begin{description}[style=nextline, leftmargin=2cm]
    \item[\texttt{services/}] \nServices{} domain service modules wrapping Tauri IPC: analysis, astrometry, catalog, channelLevels, compose, compositeChain, config, contours, cosmetic, cube, cutout, dbe, display, export, fits, geometry (observation geometry), header, imageGeometry (rotate and flip), localContrast, mast (the MAST client, 0.6.9, and archive, 0.6.9.5), ops (the processing history, 0.6.9.5), pixelmath, processing, progress, pv, ramp, regions, spectral, spectrum1d (1D spectra, 0.7.0), stacking, statistics, synth, tiles, tone and toneFile. Command name strings live only in these modules; components never call \code{typedInvoke} directly. Arguments cross the boundary in camelCase and arrive in Rust as snake\_case, so a rename on either side is a runtime failure, not a compile error.
    \item[\texttt{ComposeWizard}] Ten steps: Channels $\to$ Stack $\to$ Align $\to$ Crop $\to$ Background $\to$ Blend $\to$ Colour $\to$ Stretch $\to$ Adjust $\to$ Export. Internal \code{useReducer} state with step completion tracking and suggested-next-step logic; each step declares an \code{enabled} predicate over the wizard data, so availability is derived from state. Since 0.6.7 the state also carries the Align run token and a stack generation counter (Section~\ref{sec:compose}).
    \item[Processing chain] The Processing tab keeps one chain per file (\code{src/utils/processingChain.ts}) with fixed stages: background, denoise, deconvolution, stretch or masked stretch, LHE or HDRMT, Curves (0.6.7) and PixelMath. Re-running a step drops the later ones, and each step reads the output of the latest earlier stage. Debayer and Geometry write new files instead of joining the chain. Since 0.6.9.5 the steps of a single file are also recorded by the backend as a tree of history nodes (Section~\ref{sec:history}), so a re-run starts a new branch and the dropped steps stay reachable.
    \item[Preview Contexts] 12 split React contexts in \code{PreviewContext.tsx} (FileCtx, DisplayCtx, DoneFilesCtx, HistCtx, CubeCtx, RgbCtx, RenderCtx, RenderActionsCtx, StarOverlayCtx, RawPixelsCtx, NarrowbandCtx, DqCtx), plus three composite contexts, the wizard context and a tool-host context. Splitting them this finely keeps a cursor move over the image from re-rendering the file list or the wizard.
    \item[Dock] \code{src/components/dock/} hosts every tool window (Section~\ref{sec:dock}).
    \item[GPU Rendering] WebGPU singleton with a WGSL fragment shader for real-time display. CPU worker fallback with identical transfer functions. Both use symmetric denominator protection and the padding threshold of the Rust backend.
    \end{description}

    \subsection{Tool Windows and the Dock (v0.6.5 to v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:dock}

    Every tool is a dockable window. Four anchors take tools: Left Top and Bottom Left on the left strip, Right Top and Bottom Right on the right strip. A top anchor opens its tool as a full-height column beside the viewer; the two bottom anchors share the area under the viewer side by side, with a splitter that moves between 25 and 75\,\% of the width. Fifteen tools are registered (\code{toolRegistry.tsx}): Files, Info, Compose, Headers, Image, Astrometry, Photometry, Cube, Processing, History (0.6.9.5), Stacking, Synth, Export, Settings and Log. The Archive is not a tool window but a mode of its own beside the Workspace (Section~\ref{sec:mast}).

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Moving a tool.} Drag an icon to another group or position; a blue preview shows where the panel will open and a label reads ``Move to Bottom Left'' and so on. Right-click, Shift+F10 or the Menu key opens Move to, Move up, Move down and Reset layout. A moved panel keeps its contents, state and scroll position. Since 0.6.7.2 a tool dropped on a group whose panel is open replaces that panel, which closes; Processing and Export inputs of the closed panel that were not run are lost, as when a panel is closed.
    \item \textbf{Persistence.} Groups, order, open panels and sizes persist in \code{localStorage} under \code{ab.layout.dock.v1}; splitter sizes of earlier versions are migrated, and a saved layout with the old single Analysis tool is migrated in place.
    \item \textbf{The five Analysis tools (0.6.6.3).} Image (histogram and STF, statistics, pixel table, regions, profiles, contours, FFT, deep zoom), Astrometry (star detection, plate solve, catalog, targets, observation geometry), Photometry (aperture photometry, the photometry table, time series), Cube (ramp, spectrum with moments and line fits, PV) and Log (the measurement log, its entry count on the icon). Detected stars are shared through a store, so the Photometry table sees the stars found in Astrometry when the two sit in different anchors. When the selected file changes, an open Image or Cube switches to the one that fits the file (Cube for cubes and ramps) and remembers the choice per file. Since 0.7.0 a selected 1D spectrum always switches Image to Cube, even when Image was the last choice for that file, and the Image, Astrometry and Photometry tools see no image to measure on it.
    \item \textbf{First-run onboarding (0.6.7).} On the first launch, about 600\,ms after the dock mounts, a non-modal coachmark (\code{role="dialog"}, \code{aria-modal="false"}, focus not taken) titled ``Arrange your tool windows'' appears at the top right of the viewer. Step 1 (``Drag vertically'') animates an icon moving to the top of a strip, with the blue drop preview filling the column and the label ``Move to Right Top''; step 2 (``Drag horizontally'') moves it to the bottom of a strip with ``Move to Bottom Right''. The animations are static under \code{prefers-reduced-motion}. Closing it (the close button, ``Got it'', or Escape inside the card) or performing a real dock move writes \code{ab.onboarding.dock.v1 = "done"}; when storage is unavailable the card is shown once per session. The command palette entry ``Show tool window tips'' and the dock menu item ``Show layout tips'' show it again.
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{Screen Transfer Function (STF)}
    \label{sec:stf}

    The STF implements the PixInsight-style \textbf{Midtone Transfer Function} (MTF). Given a normalized pixel value $x \in [0, 1]$ and midtone parameter $m \in (0, 1)$:

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            \text{MTF}(x, m) = \frac{(m - 1) \cdot x}{(2m - 1) \cdot x - m}
        }
        \label{eq:mtf}
    \end{equation}

    \textbf{Properties:}
    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item $\text{MTF}(0, m) = 0$ and $\text{MTF}(1, m) = 1$ for all $m$
    \item $\text{MTF}(m, m) = 0.5$ --- the midtone maps to 50\% brightness
    \item $m < 0.5$ brightens the image; $m > 0.5$ darkens it
    \item When $m = 0.5$: $\text{MTF}(x, 0.5) = x$ (identity)
    \end{itemize}

    \begin{notebox}[Denominator Protection (v0.4.5)]
        The denominator $D = (2m - 1)x - m$ changes sign across the valid domain. The correct guard is:
        \[
            \text{if } |D| < \varepsilon \text{ then return } x; \quad \text{if } |m - 0.5| < \varepsilon \text{ then return } x
        \]
        The v0.4.2 GPU shader incorrectly used $\max(D, \varepsilon)$ which forces $D > 0$, inverting the transfer function for $m < 0.5$. v0.4.5 uses symmetric $|D|$ protection across GPU (WGSL), CPU worker (JS), and Rust backend, achieving pixel-identical output.
    \end{notebox}

    \textbf{Padding pixels} ($v \leq 10^{-7}$) are rendered as black across all three rendering paths, matching the Rust \code{is\_valid\_pixel} threshold.

    The \textbf{auto-stretch} computes parameters from image statistics:

    \begin{align}
        \text{shadow} &= \max\left(0,\; \tilde{x}_{\text{norm}} + k \cdot \sigma_{\text{norm}}\right), \quad k = -2.8 \label{eq:shadow} \\[6pt]
        \text{midtone} &= \text{MTF}_{\text{balance}}^{-1}\left(\frac{\tilde{x}_{\text{norm}} - \text{shadow}}{1 - \text{shadow}},\; t_{\text{bg}}\right), \quad t_{\text{bg}} = 0.25 \label{eq:midtone}
    \end{align}

    where $\tilde{x}_{\text{norm}}$ and $\sigma_{\text{norm}}$ are the normalized median and sigma (MAD $\times$ 1.4826).

    \subsection{Asinh Stretch}

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            f(x) = \frac{\operatorname{asinh}(\alpha \cdot x)}{\operatorname{asinh}(\alpha)}
        }
        \label{eq:asinh}
    \end{equation}

    Maps $[0, 1] \to [0, 1]$, approximately linear for small $x$ (preserving faint detail), logarithmic compression for bright values. $\alpha \in [1, 500]$ controls aggressiveness.

    \subsection{Masked Stretch (v0.4.2)}
    \label{sec:masked_stretch}

    Iterative MTF stretch with star mask protection. At each iteration $k$:

    \begin{align}
        M^{(k)} &= \text{StarMask}(I^{(k)},\; \text{growth},\; \text{softness}) \\
        I^{(k+1)} &= (1 - \alpha \cdot M^{(k)}) \cdot \text{MTF}(I^{(k)}) + \alpha \cdot M^{(k)} \cdot I^{(k)}
    \end{align}

    where $\alpha$ is the protection amount (default 0.85) and growth controls the mask dilation radius. Convergence when background level reaches target (default 0.25). JWST-optimized defaults: growth 0.8, protection 96\%.

    \subsection{Cross-Correlation Alignment (Phase Correlation)}
    \label{sec:alignment_phase}

    \begin{equation}
        \text{shift} = \arg\max_{(\Delta x, \Delta y)} \mathcal{F}^{-1}\!\left\{\frac{\hat{R} \cdot \hat{T}^*}{|\hat{R} \cdot \hat{T}^*|}\right\}
        \label{eq:phase_corr}
    \end{equation}

    Hann window: $w(n) = 0.5(1 - \cos(2\pi n / N))$. Confidence: $z = (P_{\text{peak}} - \mu_P) / \sigma_P$. Coarse-to-fine: downsample to $512 \times 512$, refine on centered crop.

    \subsection{Star-Based Affine Alignment (v0.4.0)}
    \label{sec:alignment_affine}

    Triangle descriptor (scale-invariant):
    \begin{equation}
        T = \left(\frac{d_{\text{mid}}}{d_{\text{short}}},\; \frac{d_{\text{long}}}{d_{\text{short}}}\right), \quad \text{match if } |T_r - T_t| < 0.005
    \end{equation}

    RANSAC (500 iterations) fits 6-DOF affine or 4-DOF rigid:
    \begin{equation}
        \begin{pmatrix} x_t \\ y_t \end{pmatrix} =
        \begin{pmatrix} a & b \\ c & d \end{pmatrix}
        \begin{pmatrix} x_r \\ y_r \end{pmatrix} +
        \begin{pmatrix} t_x \\ t_y \end{pmatrix}
    \end{equation}

    Sanity: offset $< 25\%$, rotation $< 5^{\circ}$, scale $\in [0.85, 1.15]$, residual $< 3$\,px, inlier ratio $> 30\%$. Fallback: affine $\to$ rigid $\to$ phase correlation $\to$ identity. Warp via bicubic Catmull-Rom, parallelized per row.

    \subsection{Sigma-Clipped Stacking}
    \label{sec:sigma_stack}

    \begin{algorithm}[H]
\SetAlgoLined
\Input{Frames $\{F_1, \ldots, F_N\}$, $\sigma_{\text{low}}, \sigma_{\text{high}}$, max iters $K$}
\Output{Stacked image $S$}
\ForEach{pixel $(i, j)$}{
    $V \leftarrow \{F_k(i,j)\}$, iter 0: $\mu = \text{median}(V)$, $\sigma = \text{MAD}(V) \times 1.4826$\;
    iters $1{+}$: $\mu = \text{mean}(V)$, $\sigma = \text{std}(V)$\;
    reject $v \notin [\mu - \sigma_{\text{low}} \cdot \sigma,\; \mu + \sigma_{\text{high}} \cdot \sigma]$\;
    $S(i,j) \leftarrow \text{mean}(V)$\;
}
\caption{Sigma-Clipped Stacking (median+MAD first iter, mean+std subsequent)}
    \end{algorithm}

    \subsection{Channel Blending (v0.4.2)}
    \label{sec:blend}

    Given $N$ input channels $C_k$ and weight matrix $W$:

    \begin{equation}
        \text{Out}_c = \sum_{k=1}^{N} W_{k,c} \cdot C_k, \quad c \in \{R, G, B\}
    \end{equation}

    Preset weights (e.g., SHO: $\text{SII} \to R$, $\text{H}\alpha \to G$, $\text{OIII} \to B$) use named channelId matching with \textbf{positional fallback} (v0.4.5): when preset channelIds don't match filled bins, weights map positionally to the first $N$ filled bins. Since 0.6.7 each channel may first be level-matched, $C_k \mapsto k_k (C_k + p_k)$ on finite non-zero pixels (Section~\ref{sec:levels_math}); exact zeros are padding and contribute nothing either way.

    \subsection{White Balance (v0.4.0, non-destructive v0.4.5)}
    \label{sec:white_balance}

    Reference channel selection by minimum coefficient of variation:

    \begin{equation}
        k^* = \arg\min_{k \in \{R, G, B\}} \frac{\text{MAD}_k}{\tilde{C}_k}; \qquad
        w_j = \frac{\tilde{C}_{k^*}}{\tilde{C}_j},\; j \neq k^*; \quad w_{k^*} = 1
    \end{equation}

    In v0.4.5, WB is applied from ORIG cache: $\text{KEY}_c = \text{ORIG}_c \times w_c$. Idempotent.

    \subsection{SCNR (v0.4.0, non-destructive v0.4.5)}
    \label{sec:scnr}

    Two limit methods and optional luminance preservation:

    \begin{align}
        G' &= \min\!\left(G,\; \tfrac{R + B}{2}\right) \quad\text{or}\quad \min(G,\; \max(R, B)) \\[4pt]
        G_{\text{new}} &= G + \alpha (G' - G), \quad \alpha \in [0, 1]
    \end{align}

    When luminance preservation is enabled (BT.709):
    \begin{equation}
        \Delta = \frac{0.7152 \cdot (G - G_{\text{new}})}{0.2126 + 0.0722}; \quad R' = \min(R + \Delta, 1); \quad B' = \min(B + \Delta, 1)
    \end{equation}

    In v0.4.5, SCNR is applied from ORIG cache with WB pre-applied: $\text{ORIG} \times \text{WB} \to \text{SCNR} \to \text{KEY}$. Idempotent.

    \subsection{Drizzle Integration}
    \label{sec:drizzle}

    \begin{equation}
        O(i, j) = \frac{\sum_p w_p \cdot K(i - x_f^p,\; j - y_f^p) \cdot I_p}{\sum_p w_p \cdot K(i - x_f^p,\; j - y_f^p)}
    \end{equation}

    Three kernels: square (top-hat), Gaussian, Lanczos-3. Flat-storage accumulator (v0.4.0): \code{Vec<f32>} + \code{Vec<u16>} replacing \code{Vec<Vec<f32>>}. Finalize uses median/MAD sigma clipping (Stetson 1987).

    \subsection{Richardson-Lucy Deconvolution}
    \label{sec:rl_deconv}

    \begin{equation}
        f^{(k+1)} = f^{(k)} \cdot \left( \frac{g}{f^{(k)} * h} * \hat{h} \right) \cdot \frac{1}{1 + \lambda}
    \end{equation}

    Tikhonov regularization as multiplicative damping ($\lambda = 0.001$ default). FFT-based convolution.

    \subsection{Background Extraction}

    \begin{equation}
        B(x, y) = \sum_{i=0}^{d} \sum_{j=0}^{d-i} a_{ij} \, x^i \, y^j
    \end{equation}

    Polynomial degree 1--5, grid 3--32, sigma-clipped sampling with \code{powi} exponentiation.

    \subsection{Wavelet Denoising}

    A trous wavelet decomposition: $I = \sum_{k=1}^{N} W_k + c_N$. Hard threshold: $W'_k = W_k$ if $|W_k| > \sigma_k t_k$, else 0. Up to 8 scales.

    \subsection{Calibration Pipeline (revised in v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:calibration_math}

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            I_{\text{cal}} = \frac{I_{\text{raw}} - M_{\text{bias}} - s \cdot M_{\text{dark}}^{(g)}}{\hat{F}}
        }
    \end{equation}

    Masters are integrated with the rejection kernel of Section~\ref{sec:rejection_families} (Winsorized sigma clipping 4/3 and a mean by default). $M_{\text{dark}}^{(g)}$ is the master of the dark temperature group $g$ nearest to the light (Section~\ref{sec:calstack}); it has the bias subtracted when bias frames are given. The dark scale is derived from the exposures, not typed in:

    \begin{equation}
        s = \begin{cases}
            \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(t_{\text{light}} / \tilde{t}_{\text{dark}},\; 0.05,\; 20\right) & \text{master bias present} \\[4pt]
            1 & \text{no bias (the dark carries the bias pedestal)}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    with $\tilde{t}_{\text{dark}}$ the median dark exposure, and $s$ snapped to 1 when it is within 1\,\% of it; an unknown exposure on either side gives $s = 1$ and a warning. Exposures are read from the merged primary and science header in the order \code{EXPTIME}, \code{XPOSURE}, \code{EFFEXPTM}, and \code{EXPOSURE} only when none of \code{XPOSURE}, \code{EFFEXPTM} and \code{DURATION} is present (on JWST \code{EXPOSURE} is an exposure count). The optional dark optimisation replaces $s$ by the value in $[0, 3]$ that minimises the robust background noise.

    The flat $\hat{F}$ is built from flats calibrated by the master flat-dark when flat-darks are given ($F_i - M_{\text{flatdark}}$, no bias, no light dark); otherwise by the bias and the dark scaled to the flat exposure when both exist, or by the unscaled dark when there is no bias (with a quantified warning when the flat and dark exposures differ by more than 1\,\%). The calibrated flats are scaled to the first one's median, combined, and normalised to a median of 1; non-positive or non-finite pixels of the master flat become 1, and the division is skipped where $|\hat{F}| \le 10^{-4}$. Calibration itself clamps nothing; the batch Pipeline clamps its stacked masters at zero.

    \subsection{Star Detection}
    \label{sec:star_detection}

    Tile-based background (parallelized). Connected-component analysis with flux-weighted centroid:

    \begin{equation}
        \bar{x}_C = \frac{\sum_{(i,j) \in C} i \cdot (I_{ij} - b)}{\sum_{(i,j) \in C} (I_{ij} - b)}
    \end{equation}

    FWHM from second intensity moments (true \textbf{median} across detected stars, v0.4.6):

    \begin{equation}
        \text{FWHM} = 2\sqrt{2\ln 2} \cdot \sqrt{\frac{\sigma_x^2 + \sigma_y^2}{2}}
    \end{equation}

    \subsection{SPCC: Blackbody Colour Calibration (v0.4.2, revised in v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:spcc_math}

    Stars detected on the composite are matched to Gaia DR3 sources (through the catalog cache, $G < 17$, at most 500 rows, cone radius at least $0.01^{\circ}$; a synthetic catalogue is used, and named, when fewer than three usable stars come back). Each star's $\text{BP}-\text{RP}$ colour is mapped to an effective temperature by a piecewise-linear table (\code{bp\_rp\_to\_teff}, $\text{BP}-\text{RP}$ clamped to $[-0.5, 5]$), and its expected colour is the Planck intensity at the three channel wavelengths, normalised to their sum:

    \begin{equation}
        e_{s,c} = \frac{B_{\lambda_c}(T_s)}{\sum_{c'} B_{\lambda_{c'}}(T_s)}, \qquad
        m_{s,c} = \frac{f_{s,c}}{\sum_{c'} f_{s,c'}}, \qquad
        B_\lambda(T) = \frac{2hc^2}{\lambda^5} \frac{1}{e^{hc/\lambda k T} - 1}
    \end{equation}

    with $f_{s,c}$ the aperture flux of star $s$ in channel $c$. The factors are flux-weighted means of the expected-to-measured ratios, divided by the white reference $W_c$ (a 5778\,K Planck spectrum for G2V, 5500\,K with a $0.98/1/1.02$ tint for the average spiral, or flat for photopic) and normalised to green:

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            k_c = \frac{1}{W_c} \cdot \frac{\sum_s \sqrt{F_s}\; e_{s,c} / m_{s,c}}{\sum_s \sqrt{F_s}}, \qquad k_c \leftarrow k_c / k_G
        }
    \end{equation}

    where $F_s$ is the star's total measured flux. A channel in which no matched star has flux is an error, not a neutral factor.

    \textbf{Wavelengths (0.6.7).} The three wavelengths $\lambda_R, \lambda_G, \lambda_B$ are the effective wavelengths of the filters feeding each plane, resolved from the headers through the filter table (nominal code wavelength, e.g.\ F814W $\to$ 814\,nm); when any of the three is unknown the defaults 640/530/460\,nm are used and the result says so (\code{wavelength\_source}). The backend accepts 300--1200\,nm; the wizard offers SPCC only for broadband visible inputs (380--830\,nm, no narrowband or medium-band filter). The difference matters: for a 4500\,K star against a 5500\,K white reference the Planck ratio $(R/G)_{4500}/(R/G)_{5500}$ is 1.2010 at 640/530\,nm but 1.3743 at 814/555\,nm, so with the fixed wavelengths an HST F814W/F555W/F435W field received 14\,\% too little red.

    \begin{notebox}[What the method is, and what it is not]
        The model is a blackbody at each channel's effective wavelength, driven by Gaia colours. It does not integrate Gaia XP spectra through the actual filter and sensor response curves, which is what the name ``spectrophotometric'' implies in other tools; the wizard option therefore reads ``SPCC (blackbody approximation)'' since 0.6.7. Integration of XP spectra through filter curves is one of the five planned gaps (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}).
    \end{notebox}

    \subsection{Pixel Rejection Families (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:rejection_families}

    The stacking kernel (\code{core/stacking/combine.rs}) separates \textbf{rejection} from \textbf{combination}. For one output pixel let $\{(v_k, c_k)\}_{k=1}^{N}$ be the samples contributed by the $N$ frames, where $v_k$ is the stored value and $c_k$ the comparison value used for the statistics (Section~\ref{sec:frame_normalization}); without rejection normalization $c_k = v_k$. Non-finite samples are discarded before any test. Rejection selects a surviving subset; the combination step then acts on the $v_k$ of the survivors only, never on the $c_k$.

    All scale estimates share one fallback: the robust scale is $1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{MAD}$, and when the MAD is zero it degrades to $1.2533141$ times the mean absolute deviation about the same centre.

    \textbf{Sigma clipping.} Iterative, with the keep test

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            \text{keep } k \iff -\sigma_{\text{low}} \, s \;\le\; c_k - \mu \;\le\; \sigma_{\text{high}} \, s
        }
        \label{eq:sigma_keep}
    \end{equation}

    On the first iteration $\mu = \operatorname{median}(c)$ and $s = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{MAD}(c)$; on every later iteration $\mu$ is the mean and $s$ the sample standard deviation (divisor $N-1$). The loop stops at \code{max\_iterations} (default 5), when an iteration removes nothing, when $s \leq 0$, or when fewer than two samples remain. Defaults are $\sigma_{\text{low}} = 4.0$ and $\sigma_{\text{high}} = 3.0$.

    \textbf{Winsorized sigma clipping.} The centre stays the median at every iteration; only the scale changes. With $s_0 = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{MAD}(c)$ and cutoff $\kappa$ (default 5.0), the samples are first Winsorized,

    \begin{equation}
        \tilde{c}_k = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(c_k,\; \mu - \kappa s_0,\; \mu + \kappa s_0\right),
    \end{equation}

    and a fixed-point iteration then re-estimates the scale with the tails held at $\pm 1.5$ current sigmas:

    \begin{align}
        u_k^{(j)} &= \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\tilde{c}_k,\; \mu - 1.5\,s^{(j)},\; \mu + 1.5\,s^{(j)}\right) \\[4pt]
        s^{(j+1)} &= 1.134 \cdot \operatorname{stdev}\!\left(u^{(j)}\right)
    \end{align}

    \begin{notebox}[Tails are replaced, not discarded]
        Every sample outside the window is \emph{set to the window edge} and stays in the sum; the divisor remains $N$ (variance uses $N-1$). The estimator therefore keeps the full sample count while the outliers lose their leverage, which is what distinguishes it from trimming. The factor $1.134$ restores consistency with the Gaussian standard deviation after that replacement. At most 10 steps, stopping when the relative change falls below $10^{-4}$.
    \end{notebox}

    The keep test is then Equation~\ref{eq:sigma_keep} with $\mu$ the median and $s$ the Winsorized sigma. Needs at least three samples.

    \textbf{Linear fit clipping.} The samples are sorted by $c$ and a straight line in \emph{rank} is fitted by least squares over the inner half of the ranks, that is $i \in [\lfloor n/4 \rfloor,\; n - \lfloor n/4 \rfloor)$:

    \begin{equation}
        c_{(i)} \approx a + b\,i, \qquad
        r_i = c_{(i)} - (a + b\,i), \qquad
        d = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{median}_i \left| r_i \right|
    \end{equation}

    Residuals are evaluated at \emph{all} ranks, not only the fitted ones, and the keep test is $-\sigma_{\text{low}} d \le r_i \le \sigma_{\text{high}} d$. The method needs at least 5 samples; below that the kernel falls back to plain sigma clipping.

    \textbf{Percentile clipping.} A single non-iterative pass about the median $m = \operatorname{median}(c)$, with bounds expressed as fractions of the median magnitude:

    \begin{equation}
        \text{keep } k \iff -p_{\text{low}} \left| m \right| \;\le\; c_k - m \;\le\; p_{\text{high}} \left| m \right|
    \end{equation}

    Defaults $p_{\text{low}} = 0.2$ and $p_{\text{high}} = 0.1$. Needs at least 3 samples and a finite, non-zero $\left| m \right|$; otherwise the set passes untouched.

    \textbf{Min/max clipping.} No statistics at all: sort by $c$, drop the $n_{\text{low}}$ smallest and the $n_{\text{high}}$ largest (both default 1). Skipped entirely when $N \le n_{\text{low}} + n_{\text{high}}$.

    \begin{table}[H]
        \centering
        \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lXcc}
            \toprule
            \textbf{Method} & \textbf{Criterion} & \textbf{Iterative} & \textbf{Min. $N$} \\
            \midrule
            Sigma clip & deviation from median/mean in units of $s$ & yes & 2 \\
            Winsorized sigma clip & same, $s$ from tail replacement & yes & 3 \\
            Linear fit clip & residual from a line fitted in rank & yes & 5 \\
            Percentile clip & deviation as a fraction of $\left| m \right|$ & no & 3 \\
            Min/max & fixed count from each end & no & $n_{\text{low}}{+}n_{\text{high}}{+}1$ \\
            \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \caption{Rejection families and their guards (\code{RejectionParams}).}
    \end{table}

    \textbf{The combination step.} Applied to the surviving $v_k$:

    \begin{equation}
        S = \begin{cases}
            \dfrac{1}{K}\displaystyle\sum_{k} v_k & \text{mean, unweighted} \\[12pt]
            \dfrac{\sum_{k} w_{f(k)} \, v_k}{\sum_{k} w_{f(k)}} & \text{mean, per-frame weights } w \\[12pt]
            \operatorname{median}_k v_k, \quad \min_k v_k, \quad \max_k v_k & \text{median, min, max}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    where $f(k)$ is the frame index of sample $k$. Weights act on the mean only; the weighted form reverts to the plain mean when $\sum w \le 10^{-12}$. If every sample is rejected the pixel takes the last centre used by the rejection loop, or $0$ when no finite centre was computed. With \code{rejection\_maps} enabled the kernel also returns two \code{u16} maps holding the low and high rejection counts per pixel.

    \subsection{Frame Normalization (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:frame_normalization}

    Normalization brings frames onto a common photometric footing before rejection. The per-frame statistics are computed on the \textbf{mutual overlap}: a linear pixel index contributes only when the value is finite in \emph{every} frame, so all frames are described over exactly the same set of pixels. The index is strided so that at most $131\,072$ positions are sampled. For each frame the location is the median and the scale is $1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{MAD}$ over that set; frame 0 is the reference $(m_0, s_0)$.

    Each frame $f$ receives an affine map $v \mapsto k_f v + z_f$ with

    \begin{equation}
        (z_f, k_f) = \begin{cases}
            (0,\; 1) & \text{none} \\[4pt]
            (m_0 - m_f,\; 1) & \text{additive} \\[4pt]
            \left(0,\; m_0 / m_f\right) & \text{multiplicative} \\[4pt]
            \left(m_0 - \dfrac{s_0}{s_f} m_f,\; \dfrac{s_0}{s_f}\right) & \text{additive with scaling (default)} \\[10pt]
            \left(0,\; s_0 / s_f\right) & \text{multiplicative with scaling}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    The plain forms equalise the sky level only; the scaling variants also equalise the dispersion, so that frames taken under different transparency or sky brightness contribute noise of comparable amplitude. Both ratios are guarded: $s_0/s_f$ becomes 1 when either scale is non-positive or non-finite, and $m_0/m_f$ becomes 1 when $\left| m_f \right| \le 10^{-12}$.

    \textbf{Scale + offset rejection normalization} is a separate, non-destructive stage. After the maps above have been applied, the post-normalization statistics are $m'_f = k_f m_f + z_f$ and $s'_f = k_f s_f$, and each frame gets a relative scale $\rho_f = s'_f / s'_0$. The rejection kernel then sees a comparison value

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            c_k = \frac{v_k - m'_f}{\rho_f} + m'_0
        }
    \end{equation}

    while the value entering the combination remains $v_k$. Rejection decisions are therefore taken in the reference frame's units, but the stacked result is still built from the frames' own pixels. A frame with $m'_f = m'_0$ and $\rho_f = 1$ is left alone. Statistics are computed whenever either normalization or scale + offset rejection normalization is requested.

    \textbf{Noise weights under normalization (0.6.7).} The $1/\sigma^2$ noise weights are measured on the raw frames, but the frames are combined after the map $v \mapsto k_f v + z_f$, which multiplies each frame's noise by $k_f$. The weights that enter the mean are therefore

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            w'_f = \frac{w_f}{k_f^{\,2}}
        }
    \end{equation}

    so that a frame dimmed by cloud to half its signal (raw $\sigma$ halved, $k_f = 2$) no longer receives four times the weight of a clear frame of equal true noise. With two such frames the old weighting gave a stack noise of $0.825$ against the optimal $1/\sqrt{2} = 0.707$. The response reports the effective weights per input (\code{weights\_applied}, \code{null} for excluded frames), and the Stack tab hint shows their range renormalised to mean 1 together with the number of frames it describes. Median combination ignores weights, as before.

    \subsection{Thin-Plate Spline Background Extraction --- DBE (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:dbe}

    The Spline background model (\code{core/imaging/dbe.rs}) fits a \textbf{regularised thin-plate spline} through box samples, in place of the global polynomial surface of the Background Extraction subsection. Coordinates are divided by $S = \max(\text{rows}, \text{cols})$ before the fit, so the node spacing is $O(1)$.

    The radial basis function is the two-dimensional thin-plate kernel

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            \varphi(r) = r^2 \ln r \quad (r > 0), \qquad \varphi(0) = 0
        }
    \end{equation}

    and the model is that kernel plus an affine term,

    \begin{equation}
        B(x, y) = a_0 + a_1 x + a_2 y + \sum_{j=1}^{n} \lambda_j \, \varphi\!\left(\left\| \mathbf{p} - \mathbf{p}_j \right\|\right)
    \end{equation}

    The coefficients come from one $(n+3) \times (n+3)$ system solved by Gaussian elimination with partial pivoting:

    \begin{equation}
        \begin{pmatrix}
            \mat{K} + \mu \mat{W}^{-1} & \mat{P} \\
            \mat{P}^{\!\top} & \mat{0}
        \end{pmatrix}
        \begin{pmatrix} \boldsymbol{\lambda} \\ \mathbf{a} \end{pmatrix}
        =
        \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{z} \\ \mathbf{0} \end{pmatrix},
        \qquad
        K_{ij} = \varphi\!\left(\left\| \mathbf{p}_i - \mathbf{p}_j \right\|\right),
        \quad
        \mat{P} = \begin{pmatrix} 1 & x_1 & y_1 \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots \\ 1 & x_n & y_n \end{pmatrix}
    \end{equation}

    The three zero rows are the usual side conditions $\sum_j \lambda_j = \sum_j \lambda_j x_j = \sum_j \lambda_j y_j = 0$, which make the affine part unique. Regularisation is the diagonal term

    \begin{equation}
        \left(\mu \mat{W}^{-1}\right)_{ii} = \frac{\mu}{\max(w_i,\, 10^{-6})},
        \qquad
        \mu = \text{smoothing} \cdot \begin{cases}
            \bar{d}^{\,2} \left| \ln \bar{d} \right| & 0 < \bar{d} < 1 \\
            1 & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    where $\bar{d}$ is the mean nearest-neighbour distance between scaled nodes, so the penalty is scaled by the kernel's own magnitude at the typical node spacing. Smoothing defaults to $0.25$. Because $\mu$ is divided by the sample weight $w_i$, a poorly filled sample box is smoothed harder than a fully covered one; at $\mu = 0$ the spline interpolates the samples exactly.

    \textbf{Sample placement.} Manual samples (Point regions) are taken first. The automatic grid divides the image into $g \times g$ cells ($g$ default 10, at least 2, each cell at least 4\,px per axis), trims a margin of a quarter cell on each side and places the sample at the centre of the remaining inner box. Automatic positions within $1$\,px of a manual one are dropped, and the automatic set is thinned uniformly so that the total does not exceed \code{max\_samples} (default 400).

    Each sample is a box of half-width \code{sample\_radius} (default 5, so $11 \times 11$\,px) reduced by sigma-clipped statistics ($\kappa = 2.5$, 3 iterations) to a median, a sigma and a maximum; the sample value is the median and its weight is the finite fraction of the nominal box area, capped at 1.

    \textbf{Sample rejection} has four recorded reasons:

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \code{bounds} --- the rounded centre falls outside the image
    \item \code{finite} --- fewer than $25\%$ of the nominal box pixels are finite
    \item \code{star} --- the box maximum exceeds $\text{median} + 5 \max(\sigma_{\text{sky}}, \sigma_{\text{box}})$, where $\sigma_{\text{sky}}$ is the median of the usable per-box sigmas
    \item \code{deviation} --- after a first fit, the residual exceeds $\max\!\left(\text{tolerance} \cdot \sigma_{\text{sky}},\; 3 \sigma_{\text{res}}\right)$, with $\sigma_{\text{res}}$ the MAD-based sigma of the residuals and tolerance default $1.0$
    \end{itemize}

    Deviation rejection runs once and the spline is refitted on the survivors; it is skipped when it would leave fewer than 4 accepted samples, which is also the minimum for any fit. The final model is evaluated on a coarse lattice of step $\max\!\left(8,\; \min(\text{rows},\text{cols})/256\right)$ and bilinearly interpolated to full resolution, then applied by subtraction or by division (guarded at $B > 10^{-10}$). The reported RMS residual is taken over the accepted samples only.

    \subsection{Aperture Photometry with Fractional Pixel Weights (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:aperture_photometry}

    \code{core/analysis/aperture.rs} replaces the centre-in-circle rule with a sub-pixel coverage weight. For a pixel whose centre is at distance $d$ from the aperture centre, with $\delta = 1/\sqrt{2}$ the half-diagonal of a pixel:

    \begin{equation}
        w = \begin{cases}
            1 & d + \delta \le r \\[4pt]
            0 & d - \delta > r \\[4pt]
            \dfrac{1}{n^2} \displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n-1} \sum_{j=0}^{n-1} \mathbf{1}\!\left[\left\| \mathbf{s}_{ij} - \mathbf{c} \right\|^2 \le r^2\right] & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    where the sampling points are $\mathbf{s}_{ij} = \mathbf{p} + \left(\tfrac{i + 0.5}{n} - \tfrac{1}{2},\; \tfrac{j + 0.5}{n} - \tfrac{1}{2}\right)$, an $n \times n$ grid over the pixel with $n = $ \code{subsamples}, default 5. Only the boundary band is sampled; interior and exterior pixels take the closed forms. An annulus weight is the difference of the two circle fractions, $w_{\text{out}} - w_{\text{in}}$, evaluated on the same grid.

    The aperture integral and its effective area are

    \begin{equation}
        F = \sum_{p} w_p \, I_p, \qquad A = \sum_{p} w_p
    \end{equation}

    over pixels that are finite and not set in the exclusion mask; masked and non-finite pixels are counted separately and reported. $A$ is what \code{aperture\_pixels} rounds, so it tracks $\pi r^2$ rather than the count of partially covered pixels.

    \textbf{Local background.} The sky annulus has radii $2 r_{\text{ap}}$ and $3 r_{\text{ap}}$ about the refined centroid. Only annulus pixels with $w \ge 0.5$ are taken as members. From the finite values in that set, $\bar{b}$ is the mean of the interquartile range (ranks $\lfloor n/4 \rfloor$ to $\lfloor 3n/4 \rfloor$) and $\sigma_{\text{sky}} = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{median}_i \left| b_i - \operatorname{median}(b) \right|$ over the whole set. The net flux is $F_{\text{net}} = F - \bar{b} A$ and the instrumental magnitude $m = -2.5 \log_{10} F_{\text{net}}$.

    The aperture radius defaults to $1.5 \times \text{FWHM}$ clamped to $[2, 60]$\,px, with the FWHM taken from second moments truncated at half maximum and corrected by the factor $1 - \ln 2$ for that truncation.

    \textbf{Error propagation} always carries the uncertainty of the sky level itself,

    \begin{equation}
        \sigma^2_{\bar{b}} = \frac{A^2 \sigma^2_{\text{sky}}}{n_{\text{bg}}},
    \end{equation}

    and then takes one of two forms depending on whether an ERR plane accompanies the data:

    \begin{align}
        \sigma^2_F &= \sum_{p} \left(w_p \, e_p\right)^2 \;+\; \sigma^2_{\bar{b}}
            && \text{ERR plane present} \label{eq:phot_err_plane} \\[4pt]
        \sigma^2_F &= \frac{\max(F_{\text{net}}, 0)}{g} \;+\; A\,\sigma^2_{\text{sky}} \;+\; \sigma^2_{\bar{b}}
            && \text{no ERR plane} \label{eq:phot_err_poisson}
    \end{align}

    In Equation~\ref{eq:phot_err_plane} each pixel's quoted error is scaled by the same fractional weight used for the flux. In Equation~\ref{eq:phot_err_poisson} the Poisson term is present only when a gain $g$ in electrons per ADU is supplied, and is zero otherwise; the second term is the shot noise of the sky within the aperture. The result reports which of the two was used, and $\text{SNR} = F_{\text{net}} / \sigma_F$. A pixel whose ERR value is not finite falls back to the sky-and-gain noise model. Since 0.6.7 the gain field is prefilled from a header-derived model (Section~\ref{sec:gain_math}); the backend still uses only the gain the request carries, and reports it as \code{gain\_used}.

    \textbf{Curve-of-growth aperture correction.} The cumulative net flux is measured at integer radii out to $4 r_{\text{ap}}$,

    \begin{equation}
        C(r) = \sum_{p \in \mathcal{A}(r)} w_p I_p \;-\; \bar{b} \sum_{p \in \mathcal{A}(r)} w_p
    \end{equation}

    and the plateau is the first radius $r \ge \max\!\left(\lfloor r_{\text{ap}} \rfloor + 1,\, 3\right)$ satisfying

    \begin{equation}
        C(r) > 0 \quad\text{and}\quad \left| C(r) - C(r-2) \right| < 0.01 \, C(r)
    \end{equation}

    that is, the growth curve has flattened to better than $1\%$ over two radial steps. If that radius reaches the inner edge of the sky annulus, $2 r_{\text{ap}}$, no correction is reported, because the curve would then be contaminated by the region used to define the background. Otherwise the correction factor is $F_{\text{net}} / C(r_{\text{plateau}})$ and $C(r_{\text{plateau}})$ is reported as the total flux.

    \subsection{Barycentric and Heliocentric Radial Velocity (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:radial_velocity}

    \code{core/astrometry/spectral.rs} computes the correction from the header alone, using the \textbf{Meeus low-precision ephemeris} (\emph{Astronomical Algorithms}, ch.~25 for the Sun and a truncated ch.~47 for the Moon). Times come from \code{core/astrometry/time.rs}: Julian dates through the Meeus calendar algorithm, $T$ in Julian centuries from J2000.0, and GMST from

    \begin{equation}
        \theta_{\text{GMST}} = 280.46061837 + 360.98564736629\,d + 0.000387933\,T^2 - \frac{T^3}{38\,710\,000}
    \end{equation}

    with $d = \text{JD} - 2451545.0$. UTC is treated as UT1 and $\text{TT} = \text{UTC} + 69.2$\,s.

    \textbf{Solar coordinates.}

    \begin{align}
        L_0 &= 280.46646 + 36000.76983\,T + 0.0003032\,T^2 \\
        M &= 357.52911 + 35999.05029\,T - 0.0001537\,T^2 \\
        e &= 0.016708634 - 0.000042037\,T - 0.0000001267\,T^2 \\
        C &= (1.914602 - 0.004817\,T - 0.000014\,T^2)\sin M \nonumber \\
        &\quad + (0.019993 - 0.000101\,T)\sin 2M + 0.000289 \sin 3M \\
        \odot &= L_0 + C, \qquad R = \frac{1.000001018\,(1 - e^2)}{1 + e \cos(M + C)}
    \end{align}

    The Earth--Moon barycentre sits at heliocentric ecliptic longitude $\odot + 180^{\circ}$ and radius $R$; the Earth itself is offset from it by the geocentric lunar vector divided by $1 + 81.30056$. The heliocentric velocity is a central difference of that position with $\Delta t = 1/48$\,day, converted with $1\,\text{AU} = 149\,597\,870.7$\,km, then rotated from the ecliptic of date to equatorial J2000 by $R_z\!\left(-(1.3969713\,T + 0.0003086\,T^2)\right)$ followed by $R_x(23.4392911^{\circ})$.

    \textbf{Diurnal term.} On the WGS84 ellipsoid ($a = 6\,378\,137$\,m, $f = 1/298.257223563$, $e^2 = 2f - f^2$), with $N = a / \sqrt{1 - e^2 \sin^2\phi}$ the prime vertical radius, the site's distance from the rotation axis and its velocity are

    \begin{equation}
        \rho = (N + h)\cos\phi, \qquad
        \mathbf{v}_{\text{rot}} = \omega \rho \left(-\sin\theta_{\text{LST}},\; \cos\theta_{\text{LST}},\; 0\right),
        \qquad \omega = 7.292115 \times 10^{-5}\,\text{rad\,s}^{-1}
    \end{equation}

    with $\theta_{\text{LST}} = \theta_{\text{GMST}} + \lambda$ and longitude read east-positive.

    \textbf{Projection.} With the target unit vector $\hat{\mathbf{u}} = (\cos\delta\cos\alpha,\; \cos\delta\sin\alpha,\; \sin\delta)$,

    \begin{align}
        v_{\text{helio}} &= \mathbf{v}_{\oplus} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}} \;+\; \mathbf{v}_{\text{rot}} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}} \\[4pt]
        v_{\text{bary}} &= v_{\text{helio}} \;+\; \mathbf{v}_{\odot} \cdot \hat{\mathbf{u}}
    \end{align}

    where $\mathbf{v}_{\odot} = -\sum_p \mathbf{v}_p \, m_p / M_{\odot}$ is the Sun's reflex motion about the solar-system barycentre, modelled as circular orbits for Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune. The sign convention is that the correction is \emph{added} to a topocentric velocity, positive when the observer moves toward the target.

    \begin{notebox}[Quoted accuracy]
        The correction is reported with \code{accuracy\_kms} $= 0.02$\,km\,s$^{-1}$ and the method string \emph{Meeus low-precision ephemeris}. A \code{SPECSYS} of \code{GEOCENTR} skips the diurnal term; frames already barycentric or beyond report a zero shift with the frame named; spacecraft headers (\code{TELESCOP} containing JWST, HST or ROMAN) take the correction as $-\text{VELOSYS}/1000$ in km\,s$^{-1}$ from the header, or are refused with the reason, since the spacecraft ephemeris is not reconstructible from the header.
    \end{notebox}

    \textbf{Air and vacuum wavelengths.} Following Greisen et al. (2006), FITS Paper~III eq.~65, with $\lambda$ in micrometres,

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            n(\lambda) = 1 + 10^{-6}\left(287.6155 + \frac{1.62887}{\lambda^2} + \frac{0.01360}{\lambda^4}\right)
        }
    \end{equation}

    The forward direction is direct, $\lambda_{\text{air}} = \lambda_{\text{vac}} / n(\lambda_{\text{vac}})$. The inverse has no closed form and is solved by fixed-point iteration $\lambda^{(k+1)} = \lambda_{\text{air}} \, n(\lambda^{(k)})$ started at $\lambda_{\text{air}} \, n(\lambda_{\text{air}})$, converging at $10^{-10}\,\mu$m within at most 50 steps. Below $0.2\,\mu$m the formula is outside its stated range and the wavelength is passed through unchanged, with a note.

    \textbf{Velocity conventions.} With $c = 299\,792.458$\,km\,s$^{-1}$, observed $\lambda$ and rest $\lambda_0$:

    \begin{align}
        v_{\text{opt}} &= c \, \frac{\lambda - \lambda_0}{\lambda_0}
            && \lambda = \lambda_0 (1 + \beta) \\[4pt]
        v_{\text{rad}} &= c \, \frac{\lambda - \lambda_0}{\lambda}
            && \lambda = \frac{\lambda_0}{1 - \beta} \\[4pt]
        v_{\text{rel}} &= c \, \frac{\lambda^2 - \lambda_0^2}{\lambda^2 + \lambda_0^2}
            && \lambda = \lambda_0 \sqrt{\frac{1 + \beta}{1 - \beta}}
    \end{align}

    with $\beta = v/c$; the right-hand column is the inversion used to place a velocity back on the wavelength axis. The three agree to first order in $\beta$ and diverge beyond it.

    \subsection{Moment Maps (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:moment_maps}

    \code{core/cube/moments.rs} collapses a spectral cube over a channel range $[z_0, z_1]$ into three maps. The spectral axis is first converted to velocity by the convention of Section~\ref{sec:radial_velocity}; an axis already stored in velocity units is used as-is. Channel widths come from a centred difference,

    \begin{equation}
        \Delta v_z = \begin{cases}
            \left| v_1 - v_0 \right| & z = 0 \\[4pt]
            \left| v_{n-1} - v_{n-2} \right| & z = n-1 \\[4pt]
            \tfrac{1}{2}\left| v_{z+1} - v_{z-1} \right| & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    \textbf{Per-pixel continuum.} Two side windows $[z_a, z_b]$ and $[z_c, z_d]$ are merged, sorted and deduplicated. When the two windows differ and at least three channels remain, a first-order baseline is fitted per pixel by ordinary least squares in the channel index; otherwise the fit collapses to a constant, and it also collapses when fewer than three channels are finite or the normal equations are degenerate:

    \begin{equation}
        b_i(z) = \alpha_i + \beta_i z, \qquad
        \beta_i = \frac{n \sum z y - \sum z \sum y}{n \sum z^2 - \left(\sum z\right)^2}, \qquad
        \alpha_i = \frac{\sum y - \beta_i \sum z}{n}
    \end{equation}

    The per-pixel noise $\sigma_i$ is the MAD-based robust sigma of that fit's residuals, $1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{MAD}$. With no windows the raw channel values are used and a single global sigma is taken from a line-free channel, $z_0 - 1$ if it exists, otherwise $z_1 + 1$.

    \textbf{The moments.} With the continuum-subtracted sample $s_{i,z} = I_{i,z} - b_i(z)$:

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            M_0 = \sum_{z=z_0}^{z_1} s_{i,z} \, \Delta v_z
        }
    \end{equation}

    \begin{align}
        M_1 &= \frac{\sum_{z \,:\, s > 0} s_{i,z} \, v_z}{\sum_{z \,:\, s > 0} s_{i,z}} \\[8pt]
        M_2 &= \sqrt{ \max\!\left( \frac{\sum_{z \,:\, s > 0} s_{i,z} \, v_z^2}{\sum_{z \,:\, s > 0} s_{i,z}} - M_1^2 ,\; 0 \right) }
    \end{align}

    $M_0$ is the integrated intensity and takes every finite channel, positive or negative, weighted by its own channel width. $M_1$ and $M_2$ are the intensity-weighted mean velocity and its dispersion, and both restrict the sum to \emph{positive} samples so that negative noise excursions cannot drive the weights or push the variance negative; the variance is clamped at zero before the root regardless.

    \textbf{SNR masking.} The noise used for a pixel is the larger of its own residual sigma and a global floor, the map median of the per-pixel sigmas. A pixel passes when

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            \frac{M_0}{\sigma_i \, \sqrt{N_{\text{ch}}} \; \overline{\Delta v}} \;\ge\; t
        }
    \end{equation}

    with $N_{\text{ch}} = z_1 - z_0 + 1$, $\overline{\Delta v}$ the mean channel width over the range and $t$ the threshold, default $3.0$. The $\sqrt{N_{\text{ch}}} \, \overline{\Delta v}$ factor is the propagated noise of the $M_0$ sum itself. $M_1$ and $M_2$ are written only where the test passes; $M_0$ is kept for failing pixels when \code{mask\_below\_threshold} is disabled, and stays NaN otherwise. When no noise estimate is available the pixel is passed and counted as unmaskable.

    \subsection{Wavelet Per-Scale Detail Bias (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:wavelet_bias}

    \code{core/imaging/wavelet.rs} implements the \`a trous (``with holes'') transform with the $B_3$ spline kernel

    \begin{equation}
        h = \tfrac{1}{16}\left[1,\; 4,\; 6,\; 4,\; 1\right]
    \end{equation}

    applied separably, with a hole spacing of $2^k$ taps at scale $k$, edge clamping, and renormalisation over the finite taps only so that NaN pixels do not spread. Starting from $c_0 = I$:

    \begin{equation}
        c_{k+1} = h_k * c_k, \qquad w_{k+1} = c_k - c_{k+1}, \qquad
        I = \sum_{k=1}^{N} w_k + c_N
    \end{equation}

    Up to 8 scales, default 5. The decomposition is exact: the layers and the residual sum back to the input.

    \textbf{Per-scale threshold.} The noise is estimated once from the finest layer, $\sigma_{w_1} = 1.4826 \cdot \operatorname{median}\left| w_1 \right|$, and referred back to the image by the $B_3$ scaling table

    \begin{equation}
        \sigma = \frac{\sigma_{w_1}}{\kappa_0}, \qquad
        \kappa = \left(0.8908,\, 0.2007,\, 0.0856,\, 0.0413,\, 0.0205,\, 0.0103,\, 0.0051\right),
        \qquad \kappa_k = \frac{\kappa_6}{2^{\,k-6}} \;\; (k > 6)
    \end{equation}

    so that scale $k$ is thresholded at its own expected noise amplitude:

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            T_k = t_k \, \sigma \, \kappa_k
        }
    \end{equation}

    with per-scale multipliers $t$ defaulting to $(3,\, 2.5,\, 2,\, 1.5,\, 1)$; scales beyond the supplied list reuse the last entry. Thresholding is soft by default, $w' = \operatorname{sgn}(w)\max(\left| w \right| - T_k,\, 0)$, or hard, $w' = w \cdot \mathbf{1}\!\left[\left| w \right| > T_k\right]$.

    \textbf{Detail bias.} Reconstruction applies a per-layer gain rather than summing the layers back at unit weight:

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            I' = c_N + \sum_{k=1}^{N} \left(1 + \beta_k\right) w'_k
        }
    \end{equation}

    The bias $\beta_k$ is exposed on $[-1, +3]$ in steps of $0.05$, default $0$, and layers with no entry take $\beta_k = 0$. At $\beta_k = 0$ the layer is restored as it stands, so a run with all biases zero and all thresholds zero is the identity. At $\beta_k = -1$ the gain vanishes and the scale is removed from the image entirely; positive values amplify structure at that scale, up to four times its measured amplitude at $\beta_k = 3$. Because each $\beta_k$ multiplies one layer of an exact decomposition, the operation is a linear per-scale filter and does not move energy between scales.

    \subsection{Local Histogram Equalization and HDR Multiscale Transform (v0.6.0)}
    \label{sec:lhe_hdrmt}

    \textbf{LHE} (\code{core/imaging/local\_contrast.rs}) is contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization. The image is covered by a grid of tiles of side $R = $ \code{kernel\_radius}, clamped to $[16, 512]$ with default 64, and a lookup table is built at each tile centre from the pixels within $R$ of it --- a disc when \code{circular} is set, the bounding square otherwise. Values are mapped onto $B = 2^b$ bins, $b \in \{8, 10, 12\}$ with default 8, by

    \begin{equation}
        p(v) = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{v - v_{\min}}{v_{\max} - v_{\min}},\, 0,\, 1\right) (B - 1),
        \qquad j = \left\lfloor p(v) + \tfrac{1}{2} \right\rfloor
    \end{equation}

    over the finite range of the whole image.

    \textbf{Clip limit and redistribution.} With $n$ pixels in the window and contrast limit $\gamma$ clamped to $[1, 64]$ (default 2.0),

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            c = \max\!\left(\frac{\gamma \, n}{B},\; 1\right),
            \qquad
            E = \sum_{j=0}^{B-1} \max\!\left(h_j - c,\; 0\right),
            \qquad
            p_j = \min(h_j,\, c) + \frac{E}{B}
        }
    \end{equation}

    The clip limit is therefore a multiple of the \emph{mean} bin population: $\gamma = 1$ flattens the histogram to uniform and gives no local contrast gain, while larger values allow progressively steeper transfer curves. The clipped mass $E$ is not discarded but spread uniformly over all $B$ bins, so $\sum_j p_j = n$ exactly and the transfer function remains a valid CDF. The lookup table is the mid-point corrected cumulative sum

    \begin{equation}
        L_j = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left(\frac{1}{n}\left(\sum_{i \le j} p_i - \tfrac{1}{2} p_j\right),\; 0,\; 1\right)
    \end{equation}

    Each output pixel is interpolated linearly between the two adjacent bins of a table and bilinearly between the four surrounding tile tables, then blended with the input by the amount $a \in [0,1]$ and clamped back into the input range:

    \begin{equation}
        v' = \operatorname{clamp}\!\left((1 - a)\, v + a\, \hat{v},\; v_{\min},\; v_{\max}\right)
    \end{equation}

    Non-finite pixels are passed through, and an image whose finite range is below $10^{-12}$ is returned unchanged.

    \textbf{HDRMT} (\code{core/imaging/hdr.rs}) compresses large-scale dynamic range through the same \`a trous decomposition. Each of \code{iterations} passes (1--4, default 1) does:

    \begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item decompose the working image into $N$ detail layers (2--8, default 6) plus the residual $c_N$;
    \item clamp the residual from above at its own median, $c_N \leftarrow \min\!\left(c_N,\; \operatorname{median}(c_N)\right)$;
    \item reconstruct with the per-layer bias of Section~\ref{sec:wavelet_bias}, $\beta_k = -\text{overdrive}$ for the two coarsest detail layers ($k + 2 \ge N$) and $\beta_k = 0$ elsewhere, with overdrive clamped to $[0,1]$;
    \item rescale the result to $[0, 1]$.
    \end{enumerate}

    Step 2 is the transform itself: every pixel whose large-scale background sits above the median is pulled down to it, so a bright core and a faint outskirt end up on the same footing, while the detail layers that carry the structure are reinjected untouched. Optional inversion runs the whole pass on $1 - v$ to compress dark rather than bright structure; optional deringing blends the original normalised image back under a star mask with weight $m \cdot a$. The image is finally mapped back to its original range. On RGB the transform runs on the BT.709 luminance ($0.2126\,R + 0.7152\,G + 0.0722\,B$) and the ratio is applied to the three channels, so hue is preserved.

    \begin{notebox}[Why linear input is refused]
        HDRMT rejects any image whose finite median is below $1\%$ of its finite maximum, with the message \emph{``HDRMT expects a non-linear (stretched) image; median is below 1\% of the maximum''}. On linear astronomical data almost every pixel sits just above the sky floor while the stars span several decades, so the residual median of step 2 would lie at that floor and the clamp would flatten essentially the whole frame. The transform assumes a stretch has already redistributed the histogram, which is why it is placed after the STF in the pipeline rather than before it.
    \end{notebox}


    \subsection{Effective Gain for Photometry (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:gain_math}

    Without an ERR plane, the Poisson term of Equation~\ref{eq:phot_err_poisson} needs a gain $g$ in electrons per data unit. \code{core/analysis/gain.rs} derives it from the header and the HDU list; the panels prefill the field with it and say where it came from, and a value the user typed survives a file change. The model has three steps.

    \textbf{Unit class.} The normalised \code{BUNIT} selects how a gain applies:

    \begin{table}[H]
        \centering
        \small
        \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{lXl}
            \toprule
            \textbf{Class} & \textbf{\code{BUNIT}} & \textbf{Unit gain $g_u$} \\
            \midrule
            counts & \code{COUNTS}, \code{COUNT}, \code{DN}, \code{ADU}, or none & header gain \\
            count rate & \code{COUNTS/S}, \code{COUNT/S}, \code{DN/S}, \code{ADU/S} & header gain $\times$ \code{EXPTIME} \\
            electrons & \code{ELECTRONS}, \code{ELECTRON}, \code{E-} & 1 \\
            electron rate & \code{ELECTRONS/S}, \code{ELECTRON/S}, \code{E-/S} & \code{EXPTIME} \\
            calibrated & \code{MJy/sr}, \code{Jy}, \code{MJy} & none \\
            other & anything else (\code{ERG/S/CM2/A}, unknown text) & none \\
            \bottomrule
        \end{tabularx}
        \caption{Unit classes of the gain model. The count-rate class is a whitelist: a test for ``\code{/S}'' alone would also match \code{ERG/S/CM2/A}.}
    \end{table}

    The header gain is the first finite positive value of \code{EGAIN} (MaxIm DL, N.I.N.A., SGP, SharpCap, ASIAIR), \code{ATODGAIN} (HST WFPC2, STIS), \code{CCDGAIN} (HST ACS, WFC3, STIS) or \code{ADCGAIN} (NICMOS). \code{GAIN} is never applied, because ZWO, QHY and ASCOM drivers write the camera gain setting there; when it is the only gain card the caption says it is present and not applied, so a user of IRAF-style headers, where \code{GAIN} is in e$^-$/ADU, can type it.

    \textbf{Combination.} A stack written by AstroBurst carries $N = $ \code{NCOMBINE}, the combine method \code{ABCOMB} and, for drizzle, the output scale $s = $ \code{ABDRZSCL} (Section~\ref{sec:calstack}). The effective gain is

    \begin{equation}
        g_{\text{eff}} = \begin{cases}
            N \, g_u & \text{mean} \\[2pt]
            2 N g_u / \pi & \text{median (its variance is } \pi/2 \text{ that of the mean)} \\[2pt]
            g_u & \text{min or max (an extreme-value combine has no Poisson scaling), with a note} \\[2pt]
            N g_u / s^2 & \text{drizzle, with a note that output pixels are correlated} \\[2pt]
            g_u & \code{NCOMBINE} \text{ without } \code{ABCOMB}\text{, with a note suggesting } N g_u \text{ for a mean stack}
        \end{cases}
    \end{equation}

    The last case covers external files: the WFPC2 mosaics that ship with the app carry \code{NCOMBINE=2} and \code{ATODGAIN=7} with no combine card, so the model prefills 7 and the note suggests 14 for a mean stack.

    \textbf{Route.} When the file has an ERR extension of the image's size, the ERR plane is the noise model whatever the unit, and a header gain is reported only as the fallback for non-finite ERR pixels (it is not prefilled); JWST \code{i2d} (\code{MJy/sr}) and NIRSpec \code{\_rate} (\code{DN/s}) files both take this route. A display-referred image (\code{ABDISP=T}) has no gain. The gain and combine cards are dropped from rescaled and display-referred outputs, so a PixelMath result in $[0,1]$ cannot inherit \code{EGAIN}.

    \textbf{Why it matters.} A CMOS frame with \code{EGAIN=0.25}, a star of net 40\,000\,ADU in a 100\,px aperture, sky $\sigma = 8$\,ADU and a 300\,px annulus gives, without a gain, $\sigma_F = \sqrt{100 \cdot 64 + 100^2 \cdot 64/300} = 92.4$\,ADU and SNR 433; with the gain the Poisson term adds $40\,000/0.25$ and $\sigma_F = 410.5$\,ADU, SNR 97. For a 16-frame mean stack of such frames, \code{EGAIN} alone would give $\sigma_F = 410.5$ while the effective gain of 4 gives 136. When no gain applies, click, batch and time-series photometry return the warning \emph{``flux errors leave out source photon noise: no gain was given and the frames carry no ERR plane; enter the gain in e-/ADU to include it''} for count units, or the model's note for calibrated and physical units.

    \subsection{Level Matching and the Composite STF Link (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:levels_math}

    Match levels (Compose \textgreater{} Blend, on by default when a narrowband filter is loaded) equalises the background and the diffuse-signal spread of the input channels before the blend. For each used channel $c$, \code{measure\_channel\_levels\_cmd} returns the median $m_c$ and the MAD $d_c$ of its finite non-zero pixels (histogram-sampled above 4\,Mpx). The reference is the channel of largest MAD, $r = \arg\max_c d_c$, and

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            k_c = \frac{d_r}{d_c} \;\ge 1, \qquad
            p_c = \frac{m_r}{k_c} - m_c, \qquad
            v \;\mapsto\; k_c\,(v + p_c) = k_c v + z_c, \quad z_c = m_r - k_c m_c
        }
    \end{equation}

    so every matched channel has median $m_r$ and MAD $d_r$. The blend weights of channel $c$ are multiplied by $k_c$ and its pedestal $p_c$ is added in the backend's per-pixel loop to finite non-zero pixels only. A channel with $d_c \le 0$ or a non-finite MAD, or a scale above $10^4$, is refused with a message. The scales go into the ``Level match:'' line under Blend and into one FITS \code{HISTORY} line per channel, \code{Level match \{ch\}: x\{k\} \{$\pm$z\}}.

    \textbf{Why median and MAD.} The 0.6.5.0 rule scaled each channel by the largest $(p_{99.5} - p_{50})$ over its own. On the JWST NIRCam trio of the Pillars (F090W, F187N, F200W in \code{MJy/sr}; medians 2.589, 40.71, 5.188; MADs 0.3058, 6.363, 0.6649) the 99.5th percentile of F200W is dominated by stars and that of F187N by diffuse Pa$\alpha$, so the old scales (1.379, 1, 14.875) left the linked preview 68\,\% green and 31\,\% blue. The MAD of these channels measures the spread of the diffuse nebula (on a $1500 \times 1500$ F090W block the MAD is 0.147 while the pixel-noise MAD is 0.031), which is what should be balanced. With the median/MAD rule the reference is F187N, the scales are 9.570 (F200W), 1 and 20.81 (F090W) and the pedestals in output units $-8.94$, 0 and $-13.17$; the preview is 81\,\% neutral, and the exported FITS planes have equal medians and MADs instead of a 15$\times$ level imbalance.

    \textbf{STF link rule.} The preview stretch of the blended composite is decided on the output planes, whether Match levels is on or off, because the blend matrix need not be diagonal (bicolour and wavelength-spreading presets mix channels). With $m_R, m_G, m_B$ the plane medians and $\sigma_R, \sigma_G, \sigma_B$ their MAD-based widths,

    \begin{equation}
        \text{unlinked} \iff \max_c m_c - \min_c m_c > 3 \max_c \sigma_c \;\;\lor\;\; \left(\min_c m_c > 0 \;\land\; \frac{\max_c m_c}{\min_c m_c} > 1.5\right)
    \end{equation}

    A level-matched diagonal blend has equal medians and stays linked; background-subtracted inputs have medians near zero and stay linked. An unlinked stretch applies to the preview, the Adjust step and the PNG export; the composite FITS data are not changed. SPCC factors computed after Match levels are divided by the scale of the channel feeding each plane.

    \subsection{Gaussian Line-Fit Maps (v0.6.6)}
    \label{sec:linefit_math}

    \code{core/cube/linefit.rs} fits every spaxel of an IFU cube over a brushed line window of 5 to 400 channels. The continuum is fitted first from the two side windows and then held fixed: linear in the channel index $x$ when both windows have at least 3 usable channels, constant otherwise (mask bit 2). The line is a Gaussian in channel space,

    \begin{equation}
        y(x) = a_0 + a_1 (x - x_{\text{ref}}) + A \exp\!\left(-\frac{(x - x_c)^2}{2 \sigma_x^2}\right)
    \end{equation}

    fitted by the damped, bounded Gauss-Newton fitter of \code{math::gauss\_newton} with weights $1/\text{ERR}^2$ from the cube's \code{ERR} extension (or from the continuum scatter without one), with channels flagged \code{DO\_NOT\_USE} in the \code{DQ} extension dropped. Velocities come from the spectral axis and the rest wavelength: $v = v(x_c)$ and $\sigma_v = \left| v(x_c + \sigma_x) - v(x_c) \right|$. The integrated flux and its error are

    \begin{equation}
        F = A \, \sigma_v \sqrt{2\pi}, \qquad
        \sigma_F^2 = 2\pi\left[(\sigma_v \sigma_A)^2 + (A\, \sigma_{\sigma_v})^2 + 2 A \sigma_v \operatorname{cov}(A, \sigma_v)\right]
    \end{equation}

    in \code{BUNIT}$\cdot$km/s. With ERR weighting the errors are absolute and inflated by $\sqrt{\chi^2_{\text{red}}}$ where $\chi^2_{\text{red}} > 1$; without ERR they are scaled by $\sqrt{\chi^2/\text{dof}}$; every error includes the continuum uncertainty. Given a resolving power $R$, the instrumental width $\sigma_{\text{inst}} = c / (2\sqrt{2 \ln 2}\, R)$ is removed, $\sigma_{\text{corr}} = \sqrt{\sigma_v^2 - \sigma_{\text{inst}}^2}$, and the spaxel is NaN with mask bit 16 when $\sigma_v \le \sigma_{\text{inst}}$.

    A two-component fit is tried where the single fit passes the S/N threshold and at least 8 channels are usable; the pair is kept only when the centres are at least one channel apart, each component is wider than the sigma lower bound and narrower than a quarter of the window, carries at least 10\,\% of the flux and has its centre inside the window, and, in \code{auto} mode, when

    \begin{equation}
        \frac{\text{BIC}_1 - \text{BIC}_2}{\max(1, \chi^2_{\text{red},1})} \ge 10
    \end{equation}

    with both amplitudes at least $3\sigma$. Dividing by the single fit's $\chi^2_{\text{red}}$ keeps a misfitting single Gaussian from accepting every pair. The mask bits are: 1 fitted at or above the S/N threshold, 2 constant continuum, 4 DQ-dropped channels, 8 ERR-dropped channels, 16 unresolved, 32 not converged, 64 pair rejected or not attempted, 128 pair accepted. Velocities are in the frame named by \code{SPECSYS}; no barycentric correction is applied, and the notes say so.

    \subsection{Tabulated Spectral Axes (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:tab_math}

    A spectral axis whose \code{CTYPE} ends in \code{-TAB} (FITS WCS Paper~III, Greisen et al.\ 2006, \S 6) takes its coordinates from a binary-table column: \code{PS$i$\_0} names the extension, \code{PS$i$\_1} the coordinate column, \code{PS$i$\_2} an optional index column, \code{PV$i$\_1} the \code{EXTVER} (default 1) and \code{PV$i$\_3} the coordinate-array axis, which must be 1. Only separable one-axis tables ($M = 1$, shape $[K]$ or $[1, K]$) are accepted. The intermediate coordinate of the 1-based pixel $p$ is

    \begin{equation}
        \psi = \text{CRVAL}_i + \delta_i\,(p - \text{CRPIX}_i)
    \end{equation}

    with the usual CD/CDELT/PC step $\delta_i$ (each default recorded in the axis notes). Without an index column the 1-based table index is $\upsilon = \psi$; with a monotonic index column $I$ it is found by inverse linear interpolation in $I$, accepted half a spacing beyond either end. The coordinate follows wcslib's \code{tabx2s}, including its linear extrapolation in the half-pixel margins: with $K$ entries $C_0 \ldots C_{K-1}$,

    \begin{equation}
        \upsilon \notin [0.5,\; K + 0.5] \Rightarrow \text{NaN}; \qquad
        p_1 = \lfloor \upsilon \rfloor,\; \delta = \upsilon - p_1,\; p_0 = p_1 - 1; \qquad
        C(\upsilon) = C_{p_0} + \delta\,(C_{p_0+1} - C_{p_0})
    \end{equation}

    where $p_1 = 0$ shifts to $p_0 = 0,\; \delta - 1$ and $p_1 = K$ to $p_0 = K - 2,\; \delta + 1$. For $C = [1, 1.5, 2.25]$\,$\mu$m this gives $\psi = 0.5 \to 0.75$, $1.5 \to 1.25$, $3.25 \to 2.4375$ and NaN below 0.5 or above 3.5. The unit is \code{CUNIT$i$}, else the column's \code{TUNIT} (with a note), else the FITS default. The axis carries \code{tabulated: true} and, for code that needs one, a linear approximation (\code{crval} the first entry, \code{cdelt} the median step).

    Every consumer with a file gets the tabulated axis: cube spectra, line measurement and line-fit maps, moments and collapse, and the \code{x1d} comparison. A PV diagram can only write a linear \code{CDELT2}, so a PV from a \code{-TAB} axis is written when the table deviates from linear by at most 1\,\% of its step (with a \code{HISTORY} line giving the deviation) and refused otherwise. JWST MIRI MRS cubes carry a \code{WCS-TABLE} extension but a linear \code{WAVE} axis without \code{PS3} cards; the app keeps using the linear axis there, which agrees with the table to one \code{float32} ulp (at most $9.5 \times 10^{-7}$\,$\mu$m on the local channel 4 cubes).

    \subsection{Reprojection Through the WCS (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:reproject_math}

    When the reference channel and another channel both have a celestial WCS, Compose Align reprojects the channel onto the reference pixel grid instead of resampling it by array size and shifting it. For each output pixel $(X, Y)$ of the reference grid,

    \begin{equation}
        \begin{gathered}
        (\alpha, \delta) = W_{\text{ref}}(X, Y), \qquad
        (x, y) = W_{\text{ch}}^{-1}(\alpha, \delta), \\[4pt]
        O(X, Y) = \begin{cases}
            \operatorname{bicubic}(I_{\text{ch}};\, x, y) & -0.5 \le x \le n_x - 0.5 \;\text{and}\; -0.5 \le y \le n_y - 0.5 \\
            \text{NaN} & \text{otherwise}
        \end{cases}
        \end{gathered}
    \end{equation}

    The mapping is evaluated exactly on a node grid of 8\,px in the reference frame and interpolated bilinearly between nodes (below $10^{-3}$\,px for TAN-to-TAN mappings with a 2$\times$ scale and 0.5$^{\circ}$ rotation). Pixel values are never rescaled: interpolation preserves surface brightness (\code{MJy/sr}) or counts per original pixel, and the per-pixel calibration cards of the output header are rescaled by the pixel-area ratio $A_{\text{ref}}/A_{\text{ch}} = |\det \mat{CD}_{\text{ref}}| / |\det \mat{CD}_{\text{ch}}|$. On the heavyTest pair, $\text{PIXAR\_SR}(\text{F444W}) \times 0.246403 = 2.29183 \times 10^{-14}$\,sr, the F200W pixel solid angle.

    \textbf{Coarser reference.} When the reference pixel is coarser by a ratio $\rho \ge 1.5$, the channel is first box-reduced by $k = \max(\lfloor \rho \rfloor, 2)$ with its WCS adjusted ($\mat{CD}' = k\,\mat{CD}$, $\text{CRPIX}' = (\text{CRPIX} - 0.5)/k + 0.5$, SIP coefficients rescaled so block centres keep their sky positions), because point-sampled bicubic interpolation of undersampled stars does not conserve their flux.

    \textbf{Relative rotation} is reported with a pinned sign: with $\mat{M} = \mat{CD}_{\text{ref}}^{-1} \mat{CD}_{\text{ch}}$, $\theta = \operatorname{atan2}(M_{10} - M_{01},\; M_{00} + M_{11})$, positive when the channel's pixel axes are rotated counter-clockwise relative to the reference ($+0.486^{\circ}$ for F444W on F200W).

    \textbf{Point-source residual.} After reprojection, the remaining offset is measured on stars, not by phase correlation alone. Stars are detected at $5\sigma$ in up to $3 \times 3$ windows of at most 512\,px on both the reference and the reprojected channel; each of up to 30 reference stars per window is matched to the nearest reprojected star within 3\,px of its position plus a seed. There are two seeds, $(0, 0)$ (the WCS prior) and the phase-correlation offset; the seed with the most agreeing matches wins, ties going to $(0,0)$. The residual is the median displacement of the matches that lie within 1\,px of the median, with their rms; fewer than 4 agreeing stars means no measurement. The residual is applied (the reprojection is shifted by it) only above 1\,px, and a residual above 5\,px is also warned. Without point sources the reprojection alone is stored and the note reads ``residual not measured''.

    \begin{notebox}[Why phase correlation is only a seed]
        Above 512\,px the phase-correlation refinement runs on one central $512 \times 512$ crop. On the Pillars F200W/F444W pair that crop is extended emission that differs between the bands, and phase correlation reported an offset of $(-1.69, -1.31)$\,px; applying it put the stars a median 2.20\,px off. Measured on 219 matched stars (177 agreeing, rms 0.344\,px) the residual is $(0.079, 0.045)$\,px, below the 1\,px floor, so the reprojection is stored as is; an independent centroid check on the written file gives a median separation of 0.175\,px.
    \end{notebox}

    \subsection{Exact Rotations and Flips (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:geometry_math}

    Processing \textgreater{} Geometry rotates an image by 90, 180 or 270 degrees clockwise or flips it horizontally or vertically. For an image of $H$ rows and $W$ columns each operation is a signed permutation $\mat{M}$ with offset $\mathbf{t}_0$ on 0-based pixels:

    \begin{equation}
        \begin{gathered}
        \text{rot90}: (x, y) \mapsto (H{-}1{-}y,\; x), \qquad
        \text{rot270}: (x, y) \mapsto (y,\; W{-}1{-}x), \\[4pt]
        \text{rot180}: (x, y) \mapsto (W{-}1{-}x,\; H{-}1{-}y), \\[4pt]
        \text{flip}_h: (x, y) \mapsto (W{-}1{-}x,\; y), \qquad
        \text{flip}_v: (x, y) \mapsto (x,\; H{-}1{-}y)
        \end{gathered}
    \end{equation}

    In FITS 1-based pixels $\mathbf{p}'_f = \mat{M}\mathbf{p}_f + \mathbf{t}_f$ with $\mathbf{t}_f = \mathbf{t}_0 + \mathbf{1} - \mat{M}\mathbf{1}$. Because $\mat{M}^{-1} = \mat{M}^{\top}$, the world coordinate $\mat{CD}(\mathbf{p} - \text{CRPIX}) = \mat{CD}\mat{M}^{\top}(\mathbf{p}' - \text{CRPIX}')$ is preserved exactly by

    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            \begin{gathered}
            \text{CRPIX}' = \mat{M}\,\text{CRPIX} + \mathbf{t}_f, \qquad
            \mat{CD}' = \mat{CD}\,\mat{M}^{\top}, \\
            \text{LTM}' = \mat{M}\,\text{LTM}, \qquad
            \text{LTV}' = \mat{M}\,\text{LTV} + \mathbf{t}_f
            \end{gathered}
        }
    \end{equation}

    \code{PC}/\code{CDELT}/\code{CROTA} forms are converted to \code{CD} first. SIP polynomials are defined on $\mathbf{u} = \mathbf{p} - \text{CRPIX}$, which transforms as $\mathbf{u}' = \mat{M}\mathbf{u}$, so $(A', B') = \mat{M}\,(A \circ \mat{M}^{\top}, B \circ \mat{M}^{\top})$ and likewise for $AP, BP$; for signed permutations this is an exact relabelling and sign change of the coefficients, with the orders and \code{DMAX} values swapped on the transposing operations. Cards that describe the original pixel axes and that the app does not transform (\code{ORIENTAT}, \code{PA\_APER}, \code{PA\_V3}, \code{VPARITY}, \code{ROLL\_REF} and every alternate-WCS card) are removed with a \code{HISTORY} line. On the F200W header all five operations reproduce the sky positions of the four corners, the centre and an off-centre pixel to 0.00\,arcsec.

    \subsection{gWCS Evaluation and the Exact Inverse (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:gwcs_math}

    A gWCS is a pipeline of frames joined by transforms; for JWST \code{\_cal} and Roman L2 imaging it has three steps (detector $\to$ v2v3 $\to$ v2v3vacorr $\to$ world), and four for a tweakreg-corrected \code{\_crf}, which adds v2v3corr before world. \code{core/astrometry/gwcs/} parses the ASDF tree into a node graph and evaluates it in \code{f64}, scalar and allocation-free. The supported tags are \code{compose}, \code{concatenate}, \code{shift}, \code{scale}, \code{polynomial} (1-D and 2-D with \code{domain}/\code{window}), \code{remap\_axes}, \code{identity}, \code{affine}, \code{rotate2d}, \code{rotate\_sequence\_3d} (Cartesian and spherical), \code{spherical\_cartesian}, \code{gnomonic} (both directions), \code{rotate3d}, the arithmetic operators \code{add}, \code{subtract}, \code{multiply}, \code{divide}, \code{power}, and \code{constant}; every other tag (\code{tabular}, \code{fix\_inputs}, \code{jwst\_pipeline/*}, unknown) is refused with its name and tree path. Semantics follow astropy and gwcs to rounding, including wcslib's special cases ($\phi = 0$ at $R = 0$ in \code{gnomonic}, NaN for $\theta \le 0$ in \code{sky2pix}, the $+360^{\circ}$ wrap of a negative longitude in \code{rotate3d}). Stored inverses win at every nesting level; derived inverses exist for the invertible tags; only the first step's bounding box is applied, as gwcs does. ASDF writes gwcs tags in the verbatim form \code{!<tag:\ldots>}, which \crate{serde\_yaml} 0.9 discards, so the parser rewrites them to the shorthand form before parsing; without that, \code{icrs} and \code{galactic} frames would be indistinguishable.

    \textbf{Exact inverse.} The analytic backward chain (stored polynomial inverses, accurate to 0.000\,45\,px on NIRCam and 0.15\,px on FGS) only starts a Newton iteration on the forward chain. With $\mathbf{t}$ the unit vector of the target $(\alpha, \delta)$, the east and north vectors at the target $\mathbf{e} = (-\sin\alpha, \cos\alpha, 0)$ and $\mathbf{n} = (-\sin\delta\cos\alpha, -\sin\delta\sin\alpha, \cos\delta)$, and $\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{p})$ the unit vector of the forward evaluation,

    \begin{equation}
        \mathbf{r}(\mathbf{p}) = \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{e} \cdot (\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{p})) \\ \mathbf{n} \cdot (\mathbf{t} - \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{p})) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
        \mat{J}_{:,j} = \frac{1}{2h}\begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{e} \cdot (\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{p} + h\hat{\mathbf{j}}) - \mathbf{u}(\mathbf{p} - h\hat{\mathbf{j}})) \\ \mathbf{n} \cdot (\ldots) \end{pmatrix}, \qquad
        \mathbf{p} \leftarrow \mathbf{p} + \mat{J}^{-1}\mathbf{r}
    \end{equation}

    with $h = 0.01$\,px. The residual lives on the tangent plane of the sphere, so RA wrap and the poles need no special case. The iteration stops when the step is below $10^{-7}$\,px (at most 10 steps) or diverges ($|\det \mat{J}| < 10^{-30}$ or a step above $10^4$\,px). A non-converged result is accepted only when the forward evaluation of the returned pixel is within 0.5\,px of the target; otherwise the answer is NaN, never the analytic start, because far outside the field the stored inverses fold sky points back inside the bounding box. When the analytic start lies more than 8\,px outside the bounding box, it is returned without Newton (dense off-footprint use such as reprojection). Measured (release build, $10^5$ random points in the NIRCam bounding box): forward 271\,ns, analytic inverse 274\,ns, exact inverse 3.75\,$\mu$s per point, 2.0 iterations on average, maximum error $1.1 \times 10^{-8}$\,px.

    \textbf{Linear surrogate.} Code that reads \code{CRPIX}/\code{CRVAL}/\code{CD} directly (region size conversions, the pixel-area fallback of photometric calibration, orientation) receives a surrogate in gWCS mode: $\text{CRPIX}$ the bounding-box centre $+1$, $\text{CRVAL}$ its forward evaluation, and $\mat{CD}$ the local Jacobian there ($h = 0.5$\,px, the RA row multiplied by $\cos\delta$). Across a NIRCam field this differs from the full model by the $\le 1\,\%$ distortion; Roman pixels are not square, and the surrogate reports $0.1103'' \times 0.1083''$.

    \subsection{TAN-SIP Fit of a gWCS (v0.6.7)}
    \label{sec:sipfit_math}

    FITS cannot hold a gWCS, so derived outputs of a Roman image need a header approximation. \code{gwcs/sip\_fit.rs} ports gwcs \code{WCS.to\_fits\_sip}. The reference pixel is the bounding-box centre rounded to 0.1\,px (half to even, as Python's \code{round}); the forward model is sampled on an $n \times n$ grid over the bounding box ($n = 32$) and projected to the TAN plane at $\text{CRVAL}$ with $\text{LONPOLE} = 180$. Polynomials without a constant term,

    \begin{equation}
        \xi(u, v) = \sum_{1 \le i+j \le d} a_{ij}\, u^i v^j, \qquad \eta(u, v) = \sum_{1 \le i+j \le d} b_{ij}\, u^i v^j, \qquad (u, v) = \mathbf{p} - \text{CRPIX}
    \end{equation}

    are fitted by LU-solved normal equations for $d = 1, 2, \ldots, 6$ until the maximum residual is below 0.01\,px (in units of the plate scale $\sqrt{|\det \partial(\xi,\eta)/\partial(u,v)|}$), with a check on a $2n$ grid. The linear terms form $\mat{CD}$ and the higher terms the SIP coefficients, $(A_{ij}, B_{ij}) = \mat{CD}^{-1}(a_{ij}, b_{ij})$ for $i + j \ge 2$. The inverse polynomials \code{AP}/\code{BP} (linear terms included) are fitted the same way to 0.01\,px. The header receives \code{RA-{}-{}-TAN-SIP}/\code{DEC-{}-TAN-SIP}, \code{CRPIX}, \code{CRVAL}, \code{CD}, the SIP coefficients in shortest round-trip exponent form, and the two residuals \code{SIPMXERR} and \code{SIPIVERR}. On the Roman WFI01 F129 L2 file the fit selects degree 5 (degree 3 would leave 0.109\,px, degree 4 0.074\,px): \code{SIPMXERR} $3.48 \times 10^{-7}$\,px, \code{SIPIVERR} $9.53 \times 10^{-4}$\,px, and the header SIP agrees with the gwcs truth to $3.5 \times 10^{-5}$\,mas over 122 points. The JWST pipeline writes its own TAN-SIP with the same targets (and \code{npoints} 12), which is why a JWST \code{\_cal} header is kept rather than refitted.

    \subsection{Star-Triangle Matching and the Rotation Rule (v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:triangles_math}

    In 0.7.0 the star matcher of Section~\ref{sec:alignment_affine} moved to \code{core/alignment/triangles.rs}, where its parameters form one record (\code{TriangleParams::ALIGNMENT}): at most 60 stars spread over a $4 \times 4$ grid of the frame, triangle descriptors matched within 0.02, triangles whose side ratios come within 0.03 of a tie left out, sides of at least 15\,px, RANSAC with 2000 iterations in 16 chunks, an inlier radius of 3\,px, at least 4 inliers and an inlier ratio of 0.20, and a mean residual of at most 5\,px. The thresholds quoted in Section~\ref{sec:alignment_affine} are those of 0.4.0.

    \textbf{Parity.} The triangle vote runs on the target stars and, with parity \code{Either}, a second time on the target list mirrored in $x$; the parity with more votes wins (a tie keeps the unmirrored one) and the mirrored pairs are mapped back, so a mirrored frame yields a transform with $\det < 0$ rather than a wrong rotation. Alignment first fits the unmirrored matches (affine, then rigid) and consults this vote only when neither fit passes the rule below.

    \textbf{The rotation rule.} A fitted transform $\mathbf{p}' = \mat{A}\mathbf{p} + \mathbf{t}$ with rotation $\theta$ and axis scales $s_x, s_y$ is checked in this order:
    \begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item every coefficient is finite;
    \item $s_x, s_y \in [0.70, 1.40]$;
    \item $\det \mat{A} > 0$. A reflection is refused. When the unmirrored fits have failed, the mirrored parity has more votes and an affine fit of the mirrored pairs is a reflection supported by at least 5 inliers with $|\det \mat{A}| \ge 0.70^2$, the refusal is decisive: the frame is returned as identity with confidence 0 and the reason (for example ``reflection (det=-1.000) rejected''), and phase correlation is not tried;
    \item the offset stays within 40\,\% of each axis: for $|\theta| \le 30^{\circ}$ the translation $\mathbf{t}$ itself, above $30^{\circ}$ the displacement of the image centre $\mathbf{c}$, $\mat{A}\mathbf{c} + \mathbf{t} - \mathbf{c}$, because a rotation about the centre has a large $\mathbf{t}$ when the transform is written about pixel $(0, 0)$;
    \item above $30^{\circ}$ the fit must be tight: with $M$ matched stars,
        \begin{equation}
            n_{\text{inliers}} \ge \max\!\left(8,\; \lceil 0.25\,M \rceil\right) \qquad\text{and}\qquad \text{RMS}_{\text{inliers}} \le 1\,\text{px}.
        \end{equation}
    \end{enumerate}
    Below $30^{\circ}$ the earlier thresholds apply unchanged. The rule replaces the fixed $30^{\circ}$ rotation cap of earlier releases, so Stack and Compose with Align = Affine and Drizzle with method Affine register frames rotated by any angle, meridian-flip halves included; Drizzle's default method and the Pipeline still try phase correlation first. When the star fit is refused, phase correlation is tried as before, except after a decisive reflection; a frame that stays unregistered is left out of the stack with the reason appended to the warning, and the results carry the star-fit refusal as \code{star\_match\_rejection}. In the tests a synthetic 2048\,px field of 300 stars registers at 0, 37, 90, 179.5 and $180^{\circ}$ within 0.05\,px RMS of the truth, and its mirror image is left out of a stack with a reflection warning.

    \subsection{Polynomial Distortion Fit with Holdout Selection (v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:poly_math}

    \code{core/registration/poly.rs} fits matched positions $(x, y) \to (x', y')$ with a bivariate polynomial of order $d \le 9$ in coordinates normalised to the extent of the inputs,
    \begin{equation}
        u = \frac{x - x_0}{s_x}, \quad v = \frac{y - y_0}{s_y}, \qquad
        x' \approx \sum_{i + j \le d} a_{ij}\, u^i v^j, \qquad y' \approx \sum_{i + j \le d} b_{ij}\, u^i v^j,
    \end{equation}
    with $(x_0, y_0)$ the centre and $(s_x, s_y)$ the half-ranges of the input positions, so $u, v \in [-1, 1]$. Each axis has $T = (d+1)(d+2)/2$ terms, and a fit needs at least $4T$ pairs. The normal equations are scaled by the square roots of their diagonal, solved by LU decomposition with one step of iterative refinement, and refused when the 1-norm condition number reaches $10^{12}$.

    \textbf{Order selection.} \code{fit\_select} fits every order of a range and scores each twice: by the $k$-fold holdout RMS, where the pairs are dealt into $k$ folds in a fixed scrambled order and each fold is predicted by a fit of the others,
    \begin{equation}
        \text{RMS}_{\text{hold}} = \sqrt{\frac{1}{N} \sum_{f=1}^{k} \sum_{p \in f} \left\| \hat{\mathbf{x}}'_{-f}(\mathbf{x}_p) - \mathbf{x}'_p \right\|^2},
    \end{equation}
    and by the Bayesian information criterion of the fit on all $N$ pairs,
    \begin{equation}
        \text{BIC} = N \ln\!\left(\frac{\text{RSS}}{N}\right) + 2T \ln N .
    \end{equation}
    The lowest order with a finite holdout RMS is the incumbent; a higher order replaces it only when it lowers the holdout RMS by at least 0.03\,px and by at least 10\,\%, and also lowers the BIC. The result reports the holdout RMS of the chosen order and the order, holdout RMS and BIC of every order considered. Given a TAN reference (\code{CRVAL}, \code{CRPIX}, \code{CD}) and a bounding box, a model exports to TAN-SIP cards through the fitter of Section~\ref{sec:sipfit_math}. Nothing in 0.7.0 calls the fitter outside its tests; it is a foundation for the offline solver (Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations}).

    \subsection{Spectral Errors and the Empirical Noise Factor (v0.6.9.5, v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:lineerr_math}

    \textbf{Region spectra.} For a region of pixels $p$ with fractional weights $w_p$ (Section~\ref{sec:aperture_photometry}), channel $z$ of a cube with an ERR extension gives
    \begin{equation}
        S_z = \sum_{p} w_p I_{p,z} - \bar{b}_z W_z, \qquad
        \sigma_{S,z}^2 = \sum_{p} w_p^2\, e_{p,z}^2 + W_z^2\, \sigma_{\bar{b},z}^2, \qquad
        W_z = \sum_{p} w_p ,
    \end{equation}
    with the sums over the finite pixels not flagged in the DQ extension (flagged pixels are counted per channel). The background $\bar{b}_z$ is the median of the annulus after $3\sigma$ clipping (5 iterations), and its variance is $\tfrac{\pi}{2} \sum e^2 / n^2$ over the $n$ surviving annulus pixels, $\pi/2$ being the variance of a median relative to a mean (1 for two samples or fewer); without an annulus the second term is absent. A range collapse over $n$ channels with summed variance $V$ reports $\sqrt{V}$ for a sum, $\sqrt{V}/n$ for a mean and $\sqrt{\pi/2}\,\sqrt{V}/n$ for a median, which the notes call approximate. Moment maps get error planes from the same ERR cube, and collapse and moment results are written as SCI + ERR FITS.

    \textbf{Line flux.} The line measurement (0.6.2) integrates the continuum-subtracted flux over the brushed channels, $F = \sum_z (y_z - c(x_z))\,\Delta x_z$. With per-channel errors $e_z$ (the ERR plane of a cube since 0.6.9.5, the \code{FLUX\_ERROR} or \code{ERR} column of a spectrum since 0.7.0), the linear continuum $c(x) = \alpha + \beta\,(x - \bar{x}_w)$ is fitted with weights $w_z = 1/e_z^2$ about the weighted mean position $\bar{x}_w$, which makes the level and slope errors independent,
    \begin{equation}
        \sigma_\alpha^2 = \frac{1}{\sum_z w_z}, \qquad \sigma_\beta^2 = \frac{1}{\sum_z w_z\, (x_z - \bar{x}_w)^2},
    \end{equation}
    and the flux error has three terms, reported separately as \code{flux\_err\_terms}:
    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            \sigma_F^2 = \underbrace{\sum_{z \in \text{line}} \left(e_z \Delta x_z\right)^2}_{\text{pixel}}
            + \underbrace{\left(\Delta\, \sigma_\alpha\right)^2}_{\text{level}}
            + \underbrace{\left(L\, \sigma_\beta\right)^2}_{\text{slope}},
            \qquad \Delta = \sum_{z \in \text{line}} \Delta x_z, \quad L = \sum_{z \in \text{line}} \Delta x_z\, (x_z - \bar{x}_w)
        }
    \end{equation}
    A channel is left out when its flux is not finite, when it is DQ-flagged (Mask DQ channels) or when its error is not finite or not positive; each reason is counted, and a channel can count under several. Without per-channel errors the measurement is unchanged from earlier releases. In the tests, constant errors give a pixel term of $\sigma \sqrt{\sum \Delta x_z^2}$, and the total agrees with the scatter of 10\,000 noise realisations within 3\,\%.

    \textbf{Empirical noise factor.} The propagated error ignores correlations between channels, so a correlated-noise factor is measured on the data. With two separate line-free continuum windows of at least 5 usable channels each, \code{core/cube/noise\_factor.rs} fits a straight line to each window and divides the residuals by the propagated error, $r_z = (y_z - \hat{y}_z)/e_z$. Over the $N$ residuals and the $p$ fitted parameters, two per window ($p = 4$ for two windows),
    \begin{equation}
        \boxed{
            f = 1.4826\,\operatorname{MAD}(r)\,\sqrt{\frac{N}{N - p}}, \qquad
            \sigma_f = f\,\frac{1.1664}{\sqrt{N - p}}
        }
    \end{equation}
    and the scaled error is $f\,e$. A warning says that the ERR plane does not describe the scatter when the factor lies outside $[0.8, 1.25]$ by more than two standard errors, that is when $f\,\exp(2\sigma_f/f) < 0.8$ or $f\,\exp(-2\sigma_f/f) > 1.25$. The Cube tool prints the factor in the form \code{ERR HDU n x 1.31 +- 0.05 (12 line-free channels)}, draws the propagated error as a band and the scaled one as a dashed outline, and its CSV exports write \code{err} and then \code{err\_scaled}, with the error source and the factor in the header.

    \section{World Coordinate Systems}
    \label{sec:wcs}
%% ============================================================================

    One type, \code{WcsTransform} (\code{core/astrometry/wcs.rs}), answers every pixel-to-sky and sky-to-pixel question in the app. Since 0.6.7 it has two backends: \textbf{header}, the \crate{wcs} crate's projection engine with SIP applied by AstroBurst's own code, and \textbf{gWCS}, the evaluator of Section~\ref{sec:gwcs_math}. Every production consumer obtains it through one loader, \code{infra::wcs\_source::load\_wcs(path, header)}: the desktop WCS commands, photometry, time series, regions, cutouts, cubes, the catalog overlay and cross-match, observation geometry, SPCC, Compose Align, the MAST footprint overlay (0.7.0) and the server routes. Four places build a header WCS directly, each on a header it assembles itself: the plate-solve probe, the pre-reduced channel of a coarser-reference alignment, the RGB FITS export's check that the header it writes carries a celestial WCS and, since 0.7.0, the MAST header peek, which projects a remote file's corners through the merged primary and extension header it read in byte ranges.

    \subsection{Header WCS: the Celestial Axis Pair (v0.6.7)}

    A header is accepted as a celestial WCS only when
    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \code{CTYPE1} and \code{CTYPE2} are both present (a missing \code{CTYPE} is a linear axis in the FITS standard, so it is never read as \code{RA-{}-{}-TAN});
    \item after stripping a \code{-SIP} suffix, both are 8-character ASCII types with longitude on axis 1 and the matching latitude on axis 2 (\code{RA}/\code{DEC}, \code{GLON}/\code{GLAT}, \code{ELON}/\code{ELAT});
    \item both carry the same three-letter projection code.
    \end{itemize}
    Each failure has its own message (``CTYPE1/CTYPE2 missing: the header has no celestial axis types (FITS treats a missing CTYPE as a linear axis), so no sky WCS is read'', ``\ldots{} puts latitude on axis 1; only longitude-first celestial headers are supported'', ``\ldots{} are not a celestial longitude/latitude pair'', ``\ldots{} projection \ldots{} differ''). The message reaches the user as the tooltip of the amber ``(no WCS)'' note when the grid or compass is requested, and as the rejection of \code{get\_wcs\_info}. Before 0.6.7 a 2-D spectrum with \code{CRPIX}/\code{CRVAL}/\code{CDELT} but no \code{CTYPE} was read as a TAN image at a pixel scale of 2700\,arcsec, and its grid, compass, catalog cone and SPCC ran on invented coordinates. All local \code{i2d} and \code{s3d} products carry a valid pair and are unaffected.

    \subsection{SIP Fit Residual Cards (v0.6.7)}

    A JWST \code{\_cal} imaging header carries a TAN-SIP fit of the gWCS with its residuals: \code{SIPMXERR} (maximum forward difference, in pixels) and \code{SIPIVERR} (maximum inverse difference). They are read when finite and non-negative, reported as \code{sip\_max\_err\_px} and \code{sip\_inv\_err\_px} by \code{get\_wcs\_info} and the server \code{wcs} route, and appended to the Info orientation line, for example \code{TAN - 0.031"/px x 0.031"/px - rot \ldots{} - E left - SIP (gWCS fit} $\le$\code{0.010 px, inverse} $\le$\code{0.012 px)}. Both are in pixels of the grid they were fitted on, so drizzle multiplies them by its output scale and resample divides them by the smaller of the two axis scale factors; a header whose WCS is replaced (Write WCS, a copy without WCS) loses them, so a stale residual never sits next to a new solution.

    \subsection{Derived FITS Outputs}

    Derived files inherit a header WCS through the existing card-copy rules: a cutout shifts \code{CRPIX} and records \code{LTV}/\code{LTM}, crop and resample rescale the SIP coefficients, drizzle and resample rescale the residual cards, and Geometry transforms the WCS exactly (Section~\ref{sec:geometry_math}). For a Roman image these are the fitted TAN-SIP cards, so a $400 \times 400$ cutout with only \code{CRPIX} shifted agrees with the parent gWCS to about $6 \times 10^{-6}$\,mas. For a JWST image the pipeline's own TAN-SIP is kept, and a cutout adds \code{SIPMXERR}/\code{SIPIVERR} from \code{meta.wcsinfo} when the header lacks them. No per-output refit is done.

    \subsection{Plate Solving and Write WCS}
    \label{sec:platesolve}

    Plate solving uploads the image to nova.astrometry.net and needs a free API key in Settings; there is no local solver (\code{solve\_offline\_placeholder} always fails with a message), and the foundations that 0.7.0 lays for one are described in Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations}. The scale range is hinted at 0.8 to 1.25 times the header pixel scale and the centre from the header WCS when one exists; the centre and radius fields accept a dot decimal only. Large images are downsampled for the upload and the solution is rescaled to the full grid. A reply without a usable RA, Dec or positive pixel scale is an error rather than a solve at $(0, 0)$.

    \textbf{Write WCS} (0.6.5.0) writes \code{<name>\_wcs.fits} with the astrometry.net TAN-SIP solution, replacing every old celestial WCS card (CD, PC, CDELT, CROTA, SIP, PV and the residual cards) and setting \code{ABPROC='platesolved'}, then shows it as ``Plate-solved WCS'' so the grid, compass, Targets, photometry and the Gaia catalog use it. Since 0.6.7 both the solve and the write run on the image being measured, the processed result on screen, not the original file: before, stars were detected on a stack while the first raw frame was uploaded, and Write WCS replaced the stack on screen with that frame. The Plate Solve header shows the same source badge as the other measurement panels, the output is \code{\{processed\_stem\}\_wcs.fits}, and a processing result published while a solve is running cancels that solve.

    \textbf{Retries (0.6.7).} Every GET of a solve (submission status, job status, calibration, annotations, WCS file) is attempted up to three times with 1\,s and 2\,s backoff when the failure is a transient transport error or an HTTP 5xx; the login and the upload are not retried. Idle pooled connections are dropped after 4\,s, under the server's keep-alive timeout of 5\,s, and error messages carry the full cause chain. The defect this closes: a job that astrometry.net had solved was reported as ``error sending request for url (\ldots/calibration)'' after one dropped connection. In the release acceptance run a real solve of a background-corrected NIRCam F200W image followed by Write WCS produced \code{\ldots\_bg\_corrected\_wcs.fits} with its centre at $(274.729885^{\circ}, -13.851857^{\circ})$ and $0.03122''$/px.

    \subsection{Offline Plate Solving: the Foundations (v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:offline_foundations}

    \begin{notebox}[Foundations, not a feature]
        Offline plate solving is not part of 0.7.0. What shipped are the shared pieces the solver will be built on (roadmap item N1), with their tests and, for the catalogue store, Tauri commands, but with no interface and no solver. No Gaia data is bundled with the application.
    \end{notebox}

    \textbf{HEALPix catalogue reader.} \code{core/astrometry/healpix\_cat.rs} reads the catalogue format that Siril uses: a 128-byte header (a 48-character title, the Gaia release, the HEALPix level, at most 12, the catalogue type and, for chunked files, the chunk level, the chunk pixel and the first and last pixel), a nested index with one 4-byte entry per HEALPix pixel, and fixed-length records sorted by pixel. Whole-sky and chunked files are read from a memory map or over HTTP byte ranges, with reads less than 64\,KiB apart coalesced and a 4\,MiB cache of index pages. Cones are served from the Siril astrometric records (16 bytes, with $T_{\text{eff}}$) and from two compact record types of AstroBurst's own (6 and 12 bytes, types 128 and 129, with their epoch in spare header bytes); the XP types are recognised but not served. A cone query asks \crate{cdshealpix} 0.9.1 for the nested coverage of the cone at the file's level (\code{cone\_coverage\_approx\_custom}, delta depth 2), refuses a cone that covers more than 2\,000\,000 records, sorts the rows by magnitude and, given an epoch, propagates the positions to it. Catalogue rows carry $T_{\text{eff}}$ when the source provides it. The reader is tested on files written by its own writer; a test on a real Siril file runs when \code{ASTROBURST\_SIRIL\_CATALOG\_DIR} points at a folder holding \code{siril\_cat\_healpix8\_astro.dat}.

    \textbf{Catalogue store.} \code{infra/catalog\_store.rs} keeps catalogues under the \code{catalogs/} folder of the Data folder (Section~\ref{sec:net}). It knows three entries: a Tycho-2 tier-0 slot, meant to ship with the app and never downloaded, with no file bundled in 0.7.0; a hosted 2MASS file; and the Siril Gaia astrometric catalogue, a 1.14\,GB bzip2 download from Zenodo. An install checks the free space, downloads through the engine of Section~\ref{sec:net}, so it resumes and is verified against a SHA-256 digest, and decodes a bzip2 archive into place; \crate{bzip2} is therefore in every build since 0.7.0. An install needs the entry's published size and digest; neither downloadable entry carries a digest yet (the 2MASS entry has no size either), so in 0.7.0 the install command refuses all three, and only the Siril entry can be pointed at an existing file or Siril catalogue folder instead. Five commands report the status, install, point the Siril entry at a file, point it at a Siril folder and remove a dataset; no part of the interface calls them yet.

    \textbf{Matcher and fitter.} The star-triangle matcher with its parity mode and rotation rule (Section~\ref{sec:triangles_math}) and the polynomial distortion fitter with holdout order selection and TAN-SIP export (Section~\ref{sec:poly_math}).

    \textbf{Camera table.} \code{core/metadata/camera\_db.rs} holds 55 entries (ZWO, QHY and Player One cameras with their colour and mono variants, Seestar units and a few Canon, Nikon, Sony and Fujifilm bodies), matched against \code{INSTRUME} by the longest pattern, each with the sensor, the pixel pitch, the CFA (mono, Bayer, X-Trans or either) and, where the sources give them, a quantum-efficiency curve id (50 of the 55), the unity gain and the read noise. With a focal length $f$ in mm and a pitch $p$ in $\mu$m the plate scale is $206\,264.8\,p / (1000 f)$\,arcsec/px. When the \code{INSTRUME} text names only a sensor format (APS-C, full frame, Four Thirds, APS-H, one inch, medium format, or a body known by its format), the pitch is estimated as the format's width over the long side of the frame in pixels, with a window of $\pm 15$\,\%, and labelled ``format class estimate''.

    \textbf{Keyword aliases.} \code{core/metadata/header\_aliases.rs} gives one list of spellings per quantity, shared by the frame cards, the masters, the CFA model and the spectral header reader: pixel size (\code{XPIXSZ}, \code{XPIXELSZ}, \code{PIXSIZE1}, \code{PIXSIZEX}, \code{XPIXSIZE}), focal length (\code{FOCALLEN}, \code{FOCAL}, and \code{FLENGTH} read in metres), temperature (\code{CCD-TEMP}, \code{CCD\_TEMP}, \code{CCDTEMP}, \code{TEMPERAT}, \code{CAMTCCD}, \code{SET-TEMP}), binning (\code{XBINNING}, \code{BINX}), frame type (\code{IMAGETYP}, \code{FRAMETYP}), pointing (\code{RA\_TARG}/\code{DEC\_TARG}, \code{TARG\_RA}/\code{TARG\_DEC}, \code{RA\_D}/\code{DEC\_D} in degrees, \code{RA}/\code{DEC}, \code{OBJRA}/\code{OBJDEC}, \code{RA\_OBJ}/\code{DEC\_OBJ}); the site reader of \code{core/astrometry/spectral.rs} accepts \code{SITE-LAT}/\code{SITE-LONG} among its five latitude and longitude pairs. These cover MaxIm DL, N.I.N.A. and SharpCap headers.

    \textbf{Luminance bin.} The CFA model of Section~\ref{sec:cfa} bins a one-shot-colour mosaic to one value per CFA cell ($2 \times 2$ for Bayer, $3 \times 3$ for X-Trans), the input a star detector needs on such a frame.

    \subsection{The Measurement Contract}

    A measurement is taken on the image on screen: the processed result when there is one, otherwise the file. Each measurement panel carries a badge naming its source (``Values from Background'', ``Plate-solved WCS'', ``selected file'' while the colour composite is shown). Version 0.6.7 closed the three places that broke the rule:
    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Info panel sky readout.} It reads the analysis target and hides itself when the target has no celestial WCS (a PV diagram, whose first axis is \code{OFFSET}) or when a PNG-only result sits on another grid than the file. Before, hovering a PV diagram printed the RA/Dec of the cube spaxel with the same pixel numbers.
    \item \textbf{Plate Solve and Write WCS} run on the measured image (Section~\ref{sec:platesolve}).
    \item \textbf{Stale numbers after a rewrite.} Statistics, region statistics, region profiles and the line sky readout re-run on a key made of the path and a render version (\code{path@version}), so re-running Background with another degree, which rewrites the same \code{\_bg\_corrected.fits}, refreshes them without a click.
    \end{itemize}
    A click on a result whose grid differs from the cube's spatial grid (a PV diagram) no longer extracts a spectrum; before, it plotted the spectrum of the spaxel with the same numbers, or failed with a row index beyond the cube. Ramps keep their own pixel mapping.

    \subsection{Regions and Exclusions}

    Circles, ellipses, boxes, annuli and polygons have an include/exclude toggle (0.6.5.0), the DS9 \code{-} prefix, kept by \code{.reg} import and export. The pixels inside every exclude region are left out of the other regions' statistics and background annuli and of radial profiles, line cuts and surface-brightness profiles, merged with the DQ mask when DQ exclusion is on; the \code{n} cell reads \code{(-N excl)} and counts both causes, and an exclude region's own row is measured on its own pixels. \code{region\_stats\_cmd}, \code{radial\_profile\_cmd}, \code{line\_cut\_cmd} and \code{sb\_profile\_cmd} take at most 512 exclude shapes; an invalid shape (zero radius, polygon with fewer than three points) is skipped and named in \code{notes}. When the header carries a flux calibration and a WCS, every region also reports flux in Jy, AB magnitude, surface brightness, area, centre RA/Dec and sky position angle (0.6.2).

    \subsection{Photometry}

    Aperture photometry (Section~\ref{sec:aperture_photometry}) runs on a click with the Crosshair tool, on a list in the Photometry table (detected stars, Point regions or pasted positions, with apertures drawn on the image), or across frames as a time series with drift tracking and a differential light curve. The gain field is prefilled from the model of Section~\ref{sec:gain_math} with a caption naming the source, and click and batch responses report \code{gain\_used}.

    The optional Gaia DR3 match of click photometry (opt-in, remembered) queries one field cone with $G < 17$ through the catalog cache and matches locally within 5\,arcsec. When that cone comes back at its row cap, so that stars brighter than $G = 17$ may have been cut, the match falls back to a $0.01^{\circ}$ cone around the star, also cached. Before 0.6.7 every click was one VizieR request and nothing was cached. A failed query, an empty cone or a missing WCS is a warning, never a missing box.

    \subsection{Catalogs and the Disk Cache (v0.6.7)}

    Gaia DR3 cone searches go through three tiers: the in-process LRU (16 cones), a disk cache, and VizieR. A cone is keyed by its centre and radius quantised to $10^{-4}$\,deg, the magnitude limit to 0.01 and the row limit, and stored as one JSON file per key (\code{gaia-dr3\_\{ra\}\_\{dec\}\allowbreak\_\{r\}\_\{mag\}\allowbreak\_\{rows\}.json}, written to a temporary file and renamed). The directory is \code{dirs::cache\_dir()/\allowbreak astroburst/\allowbreak catalog-cache} (\code{\%LOCALAPPDATA\%} on Windows, \code{\textasciitilde/Library/Caches} on macOS, \code{\textasciitilde/.cache} on Linux), capped at 64\,MiB with the oldest files evicted first. Gaia DR3 is a fixed release, so entries have no expiry and the network is used only on a miss; an empty result is never written, so a bad response cannot poison the cache. Cone search and cross-match report \code{catalog\_source} (\code{network}, \code{memory} or \code{disk}).

    Two environment variables are read once at start, by the desktop app and the server: \code{ASTROBURST\_CATALOG\_CACHE\_DIR} moves the cache, and \code{ASTROBURST\_VIZIER\_URL} replaces the VizieR endpoint (a mirror, or an unreachable address to test offline behaviour). CSV exports write numbers exactly as JavaScript's \code{String(number)} does (exponent form below $10^{-6}$ and from $10^{21}$), so a file saved from Rust and a clipboard copy from the frontend agree byte for byte; \crate{serde\_json} is built with \code{float\_roundtrip} so the IPC body keeps every digit.

    \subsection{Filters}

    The filter-wavelength table exists in TypeScript (\code{src/utils/filterWavelengths.ts}) and, since 0.6.7, in Rust (\code{core/metadata/filter\_wavelengths.rs}), with a vitest that parses the Rust table and requires both to hold the same codes: 74 in 0.6.7, 82 since 0.6.9 added the eight Roman WFI filters F062 to F213. Values are the nominal code wavelength (F444W $\to$ 4440\,nm, F158M $\to$ 1580\,nm), not pivots, except for the Roman filters, which take their pivot wavelengths (F062 $\to$ 629\,nm, F213 $\to$ 2123\,nm). The effective filter of a header follows one rule on both sides: when \code{PUPIL} holds a known code (a NIRCam pupil-wheel filter such as F470N) it wins; otherwise \code{FILTER}, \code{FILTER1}, \code{FILTER2}, \code{FILTNAM1}, \code{FILTNAM2} and finally \code{PUPIL} are tried, skipping blanks and one- or two-digit wheel positions. Time-series photometry uses it to skip a frame taken through another filter, now compared case-insensitively.

    \subsection{Spectra}

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Spectral axes.} Linear axes as in 0.6.0 (Section~\ref{sec:radial_velocity}), plus tabulated \code{-TAB} axes (Section~\ref{sec:tab_math}); \code{-LOG} and other non-linear codes are refused with a reason.
    \item \textbf{Binary tables.} The BINTABLE reader (0.6.5.0) reads scalar columns and, since 0.6.7, fixed-repeat array columns (\code{rE}, \code{rD}, \code{rB}, \code{rI}, \code{rJ}, \code{rK}) as flattened row-major vectors, shaped by \code{TDIM} (whose product must equal the repeat) with \code{TNULL}/\code{TSCAL}/\code{TZERO} applied per element. Variable-length \code{P}/\code{Q} columns are refused with a message. A table is located by \code{EXTNAME} and \code{EXTVER} from the raw headers, so an unrelated table with a layout the parser rejects (a JWST \code{ASDF} or \code{HDRTAB} extension) does not hide it.
    \item \textbf{Pipeline \code{x1d}.} ``Compare with pipeline x1d'' (0.6.5.0) reads the \code{EXTRACT1D} table next to a cube, resamples it onto the cube's channels in vacuum $\mu$m without crossing DQ-flagged rows, and plots \code{FLUX} (Jy) or \code{SURF\_BRIGHT} beside the region spectrum; a header that declares more data than the file holds is refused.
    \item \textbf{Wavelength readout.} On a frame with a same-size \code{WAVELENGTH} extension (NIRSpec \code{\_cal}), the status strip and the pixel table show the wavelength at the cursor (0.6.5.0).
    \item \textbf{Line list and measurement.} 27 rest-frame lines (0.6.3, 0.6.6) drawn at a typed redshift or systemic velocity; line measurement over a brushed range with an optional Gaussian fit (0.6.2), weighted by the per-channel errors when the data carry them (0.6.9.5 for cubes, 0.7.0 for 1D spectra, Section~\ref{sec:lineerr_math}); spectrum export and comparison with provenance lines (0.6.3).
    \end{itemize}

    \subsection{1D Spectra as Documents (v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:spec1d}

    A 1D spectrum is a file in its own right, not a failed image. On load, JWST \code{x1d} tables (\code{EXTRACT1D}) and 1D FITS arrays with a wavelength axis are recognised, listed in the Files panel as spectrum rows, counted as spectra in the status bar and selectable from the list, the keyboard and the palette; a 1D array without a wavelength axis still falls back to a cube plane or is reported as an error. Selecting a spectrum shows a viewer panel that names the file and offers to open the Cube tool, and no image, histogram, scale-limit, cube, DQ, raw-pixel or WCS request is sent for it. \code{core/analysis/spectrum1d.rs} reads three sources into one model (flux, optional error and DQ with its flag table, flux unit and spectral axis): the \code{x1d} table, a 1D FITS array with a linear spectral axis (FITS \code{WAVE} and \code{AWAV} or the IRAF linear forms; multispec and logarithmic axes are refused with a reason) and a CSV file.

    \textbf{Air and vacuum.} The model states whether its wavelengths are air or vacuum: \code{AWAV} is air, \code{WAVE} vacuum, and a ground-based file without a JWST, HST or Roman card reads as air. The panel switches between the two, and a switch clears a line measurement made in the other frame. Velocities on an air axis use the vacuum rest wavelength converted to air with Morton (2000, ApJS 130, 403),
    \begin{equation}
        n = 1 + 8.34254 \times 10^{-5} + \frac{2.406147 \times 10^{-2}}{130 - \sigma^2} + \frac{1.5998 \times 10^{-4}}{38.9 - \sigma^2},
        \qquad \sigma = \frac{1}{\lambda_{\text{vac}}}\ [\mu\text{m}^{-1}], \qquad
        \lambda_{\text{air}} = \frac{\lambda_{\text{vac}}}{n},
    \end{equation}
    and a rest wavelength below $0.2\,\mu$m, where the formula does not apply, is left as vacuum with a note.

    \textbf{Measurement.} The Cube tool plots a selected spectrum and measures lines on it as on a cube spectrum: brush a line range and continuum windows, pick a rest line from the line list, and measure with the file's \code{FLUX\_ERROR} or \code{ERR} as weights and its DQ-flagged and non-finite channels left out (Section~\ref{sec:lineerr_math}); Weight by ERR and Mask DQ channels turn either off. Collapse, moments, line-fit maps, frame navigation and region controls are hidden, and no barycentric or heliocentric correction is computed for a spectrum. Every line measurement, on a cube or a spectrum, lists its error source, the pixel, level and slope terms of the flux error and the channels left out, in the panel, its CSV row and the measurement log.

    \textbf{CSV round trip and comparison.} Copy CSV and Save CSV write the flux with \code{err} and \code{dq} columns and the DQ table (an air spectrum also keeps \code{wavelength\_air\_um} next to the converted \code{wavelength\_vacuum\_um}), and the measurement log records the file's path. Open spectrum (CSV), in the Cube tool and the command palette, reads back a CSV with a \code{wavelength\_air\_um} or \code{wavelength\_vacuum\_um} column and a \code{flux} column, with optional \code{err}, \code{dq} and \code{flux\_unit}; a velocity spectrum exported without a rest wavelength has neither wavelength column and cannot be reopened. Compare plots a second \code{x1d}, 1D FITS or CSV spectrum resampled onto the spectrum's own vacuum wavelengths, and an \code{x1d} whose \code{\_s3d} cube is loaded offers Compare with its cube.

    \subsection{Cubes}

    Region spectra, range collapse and moment maps follow Section~\ref{sec:moment_maps}; since 0.6.9.5 they carry errors when the cube has an ERR extension (Section~\ref{sec:lineerr_math}). In the Cube tool (0.7.0) the propagated error is drawn as a band around the sum, mean or Jy series, each view with its own error; when continuum windows are set before Region spectrum the empirical noise factor is measured on them and the scaled error is outlined, with a note when the windows could not be used and the backend's warning when the ERR plane does not describe the scatter. Region CSV exports write \code{err} and \code{err\_scaled}, and the measurement log records both and whether DQ-flagged spaxels were excluded. Moment and range-collapse results say whether an ERR plane was written and from which ERR cube. Line-fit maps (0.6.6, Section~\ref{sec:linefit_math}) fit a brushed line in every spaxel in a fraction of a second on a NIRSpec IFU cube and write one 2-D FITS per plane (\code{flux}, \code{velocity}, \code{sigma\_obs}, \code{sigma\_corr}, the errors, \code{chi2\_red}, \code{snr}, \code{mask}, \code{ncomp} and, for accepted pairs, twelve \code{c1\_*}/\code{c2\_*} planes) with the celestial WCS and \code{LF*} provenance cards. A click on a displayed plane refits that spaxel and draws a mini plot of the fit from the arrays the backend returns. Velocity planes open in an inverted RdBu map symmetric about zero, a temporary display that is never saved. Position-velocity diagrams (0.6.3) sample a cube along a line region without loading it whole, with offsets in arcsec from the CD matrix, and are written as a 2-D FITS with an \code{OFFSET} axis and the native spectral axis.

    \subsection{Ramps (v0.6.6)}

    A 4-D FITS image with \code{NGROUPS}/\code{NINTS} cards, or a \code{DATAMODL} of \code{Level1bModel}/\code{RampModel}, opens as a ramp (JWST \code{\_uncal}, Roman L1 resultant stacks) with group and integration navigation, the readout identity (including the NIRSpec IRS2 layout and the group time) and a per-pixel ramp plot with saturated, unused and jump-flagged groups. ``Run quick slope'' turns one integration into \code{<stem>\_qslope.fits} with SCI (DN/s), NGOOD, DQ and NOISE extensions, offline and without reference files: the IRS2 reference output and interleaved reference rows are stripped, a per-amplifier reference offset is subtracted, saturation is flagged by threshold and jumps crudely, and the slope is an ordinary least-squares or median fit. It is a decision aid, not a rate: there is no superbias, linearity, dark or weighted fit, and the header carries \code{ABPROC = qslope} and never \code{CAL\_VER}, \code{S\_RAMP} or \code{DATAMODL}. ``Compare with rate'' reads the official \code{\_rate.fits} and reports the alignment check, per-bin and per-amplifier differences, \code{JUMP\_DET} and \code{SATURATED} recall and precision, and a verdict against the published tolerances. On 8 NIRSpec IRS2 exposures of program 1266 the faint additive offset is within 0.011\,DN/s over the full frame and 0.021\,DN/s per amplifier, and the quick slope differs from the official one by $+1.2$ to $+2.7\,\%$ at 1--30\,DN/s (\code{docs/quick-slope.md}).

    \subsection{Time Series, Observation Geometry and the Log}

    Observation geometry (0.6.3) reports BJD\_TDB and HJD\_UTC of mid-exposure (leap-second table, TT and TDB, light time within 1\,s of astropy), local sidereal time, hour angle, altitude and azimuth, Kasten-Young airmass, parallactic angle, and the Sun and Moon, for the loaded file and for every frame of a time series, whose light curve uses BJD\_TDB when every frame has one. The measurement log (0.6.3, the Log tool since 0.6.6.3) records every photometry click, batch run, line measurement, line-fit run, time series, cross-match, spectrum export and PV run with its UTC timestamp, measured image, DQ handling, parameters and values; it is kept for the session and exports as CSV.

    \subsection{Frame Cards and Consistency Checks (v0.6.7)}

    Before any master is built, the headers of every bias, dark, flat, flat-dark and light frame are read once (merged primary and science header, so JWST MEF values are seen): exposure (Section~\ref{sec:calibration_math}), sensor temperature (\code{CCD-TEMP}, else \code{SET-TEMP}), effective filter, \code{IMAGETYP}, \code{GAIN}, \code{OFFSET}, \code{XBINNING}/\code{YBINNING} and the Bayer cards. Since 0.7.0 temperature, frame type and binning are also read under the keyword aliases of Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations} (for example \code{CCD\_TEMP}, \code{FRAMETYP}, \code{BINX}). The checks run identically in the Calibration tab, the desktop Pipeline and the server's \code{pipeline/run}; a refusal fails before any pixel is loaded (\code{400} on the server), and warnings travel in the response.

    \begin{table}[H]
        \centering
        \small
        \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{Xl}
            \toprule
            \textbf{Rule} & \textbf{Outcome} \\

        \end{tabularx}

    \end{table}

    \textbf{Flat-darks.} A fourth master. When flat-dark frames are given, flats are calibrated as $F_i - M_{\text{flatdark}}$. Without them, a dark without bias is still subtracted from the flats unscaled (which removes the bias pedestal and over-subtracts the thermal signal by the exposure difference) and the warning quantifies it; the run is not refused, since that is the usual cooled-CMOS setup.

    \textbf{Temperature groups.} Darks are sorted by temperature and split only at gaps larger than 3\,$^{\circ}$C, so a set-point library at $-20$ and $-10\,^{\circ}$C becomes two groups while a continuously drifting uncooled sensor stays one; a group of fewer than 5 frames is merged into its nearest neighbour. Each light takes the group whose mean temperature is nearest to its own. A group spanning more than 2\,$^{\circ}$C, or a light more than 2\,$^{\circ}$C from its group, is warned (dark current roughly doubles every 7\,$^{\circ}$C, so 2\,$^{\circ}$C is about 22\,\%); a run is never refused for temperature. Lights without a temperature use the largest group; when any dark lacks a temperature, all darks form a single group. With a $-20/-10\,^{\circ}$C library and lights at $-10\,^{\circ}$C a single averaged master under-corrected the dark current by 31\,\%.

    \textbf{Cosmetic correction on Bayer data.} \code{CosmeticConfig.cfa} is optional: when absent, the CFA lattice is detected from \code{BAYERPAT}/\code{COLORTYP}, so a hot red site is replaced from red neighbours, not from green and blue ones; the panel shows the detected pattern and the response says which setting was applied. When the MAD of the residuals is zero, the robust scale now falls back to the 90th percentile of $|v - \text{median}|$ divided by 1.6449, and to the mean absolute deviation only when 90\,\% of the pixels sit exactly at the median, because a few hot pixels inflated the old fallback until a warm pixel at 50 quantisation levels was never flagged.

    Stack-tab results, Pipeline channel masters and drizzle outputs carry their provenance in the header:

    The combination itself changed in three places: noise weights are divided by $k_f^2$ (Section~\ref{sec:frame_normalization}); frames of different sizes are refused when alignment is off (before, they were cropped from the top-left corner and stacked misregistered) and listed in a warning when alignment is on; and the Stack panel disables its frame rows while a stack runs and shows the effective weights the backend used. One-shot-colour lights (\code{BAYERPAT} or \code{COLORTYP}) are refused before alignment or drizzle since 0.6.5.0, with the debayer route named.

    \subsection{Batch Pipeline}

    The Stack-tab Pipeline calibrates, registers and stacks each channel, writes one master FITS and one auto-STF PNG per channel plus the RGB quick look to the output folder (0.6.5.0), and leaves out the frames rejected in the Subframe Selector. Each frame is registered against the channel's first frame by phase correlation, then by the affine matcher. Since 0.6.7 a frame that neither method registers is excluded from the stack. A warning in the Stack tab's wording, shown in the Pipeline panel, identifies it by position, for example ``Channel 'L': frame 3 of 3 was left out of the stack: it could not be aligned to frame 1 (identity, confidence 0.00)''. The master's \code{NCOMBINE} and \code{TOTEXP} count only the stacked frames, and when fewer than two frames of a channel remain the run fails with an error naming the channel. Before, the pipeline stacked such a frame as it came out of the failed alignment. The Pipeline takes no method name; \code{stack}, \code{align\_channels\_cmd} and \code{export\_aligned\_channels\_cmd} now reject an unknown one instead of running phase correlation.

    \subsection{Compose Wizard}
    \label{sec:compose}

    The ten steps are unchanged; what they do changed as follows.

    \textbf{Stack.} A stack that finishes after a Reset, or after its bin's files or exclusions changed, is discarded with ``Inputs changed while stacking; result discarded'', and Reset cancels a running stack.

    \textbf{Align.} When the channels carry a celestial WCS they are reprojected through it (Section~\ref{sec:reproject_math}). The reference is the channel with the finest WCS pixel scale, channels within 0.5\,\% of the finest counting as tied and the first filled bin among them winning; a REF selector overrides it, and the response names the rule (\code{finest\_wcs}, \code{selected} or \code{first}). Each channel's note reads, for example, ``reprojected through WCS (scale x2.0145, rotation +0.49$^{\circ}$); residual $\Delta$x 0.0\,px $\Delta$y +0.1\,px'', or ``\ldots{} pre-reduced x2'' when the reference is coarser; a gWCS channel is reprojected through its gWCS. A channel whose footprint does not overlap the reference is left empty with a warning. Without a WCS on either side the previous path (resample by array size, then registration) is used and the result says so. On the Pillars F200W/F444W pair the old array-size mapping left the F444W corners 95 to 149\,px from their WCS positions; after reprojection the residual measured on stars is below 0.1\,px. In the app the F444W channel is reprojected onto the $14344 \times 8589$ F200W grid in about 2\,s.

    \textbf{Run tokens.} Wizard cache keys carry a per-run token (\code{\_\_wizard\_ch\_t<token>\_<bin>\_<stage>}); Crop and Background use the token of the Align run they continue, a run removes only keys of other tokens, and a run that finishes after a newer one removes its own keys. Before, an older Align finishing last overwrote the newer run's pixels under shared keys.

    \textbf{Crop} opens for a single filled channel (its stacked or raw file), not only after a two-channel Align.

    \textbf{Blend} applies Match levels by median and MAD and decides the preview STF link on the composite output (Section~\ref{sec:levels_math}).

    \textbf{Colour.} SPCC is offered for broadband visible inputs and evaluates the blackbody model at each channel's filter wavelength (Section~\ref{sec:spcc_math}); the option reads ``SPCC (blackbody approximation)'', or ``SPCC (broadband RGB only)'' and disabled, with the filter named, when an input is narrowband, medium-band or outside 380--830\,nm.

    \textbf{Stretch.} Star removal no longer marks the Stretch step done; it invalidates Stretch and the later steps, keeping the starless channel.

    \textbf{Export.} Without a blended composite, exporting two or more channels uses the fixed mapping R$\leftarrow$\{r, sii, ha\}, G$\leftarrow$\{g, ha, oiii\}, B$\leftarrow$\{b, oiii, sii\} (first unused bin wins, so \{H$\alpha$, OIII, SII\} gives R = SII, G = H$\alpha$, B = OIII). A banner now shows that mapping, and the FITS gains an ASCII \code{HISTORY} line such as \code{Channel mapping: R=SII G=HA B=OIII (fixed, Blend weights not applied)}.

    \section{Processing Tools}
    \label{sec:processing}
%% ============================================================================

    The Processing tab holds Debayer, Background (polynomial and DBE), Denoise, PSF, Deconvolution, Stretch (arcsinh, GHS), Masked Stretch, LHE, HDRMT, Curves, PixelMath and Geometry. The chain stages are listed in Section~\ref{sec:frontend}; while the Compose composite is on screen, the steps that support it run on its three planes with the composite's own chain (0.6.4). The algorithms of 0.6.0 are described in Section~\ref{sec:math}; this section covers what 0.6.7 and the releases after it added.

    \subsection{Curves and Levels on a File}

    The Curves panel applies levels (black point 0--0.5, gamma 0.2--5 on a logarithmic slider, white point 0.5--1) and one tone curve, edited with the same curve editor as Compose \textgreater{} Adjust, to a mono FITS or to each plane of an RGB FITS (\code{apply\_tone\_cmd}). The output is display-referred, so a linear input is first brought to display space with its own auto-STF, the mapping the viewer shows (one linked STF for the three planes of an RGB FITS, so the colour balance is kept), and a \code{HISTORY} line records the STF parameters; an input already marked \code{ABDISP=T} is taken as $[0, 1]$. Min/max normalisation was rejected because on linear data the sky sits near 0.7\,\% of the range (WFPC2 502\,nm: median $7 \times 10^{-3}$ after min/max scaling), where any black point above 0.01 clips the whole image. The result is written with \code{ABPROC='tone'} and \code{ABDISP=T}, joins the chain after LHE/HDRMT and before PixelMath, and is refused when both levels and curve are identity (``Nothing to apply: the levels and the curve are both identity.'') or the input is a cube. On the composite the panel points to Compose \textgreater{} Adjust.

    \subsection{Background and De-Banding on a File}

    The Background panel adds four modes to Subtract and Divide: de-band with an auto-detected axis, rows, columns or both, using the polynomial model's sigma clip and 1--5 iterations; the ``model'' preview then shows the removed band pattern, the header gains \code{ABDEBAND} and the result reports the axis. De-banding was available only in the Compose Background step before. A guard refuses dispersed exposures, whose rows and columns are spectra: \code{EXP\_TYPE} starting with \code{NRS\_}, \code{MIR\_MRS}, \code{MIR\_LRS}, \code{NIS\_SOSS}, \code{NIS\_WFSS}, or a NIRCam grism mode (``De-banding is for imaging frames; this is a dispersed exposure \ldots{} so a band model would subtract signal.''). The modes are disabled under the Spline model and on the composite, whose background command has no de-band branch.

    \subsection{Geometry: Rotate and Flip}

    Five buttons rotate the displayed image by 90, 180 or 270 degrees clockwise or flip it horizontally or vertically (\code{transform\_geometry\_cmd}), with the exact WCS update of Section~\ref{sec:geometry_math}. Arbitrary angles are not offered. The input is the image on screen; the output is a new file, \code{<stem>\_<op>.fits} with \code{ABPROC='<op>'}, added to the Files list, because a rotation inside the fixed-stage chain would either be dropped by a later re-run or leave the next step working on the unrotated original. RGB FITS planes are transformed together; cubes are refused. Since 0.6.9 the ERR and DQ planes are transformed with the image and written as extensions with the source's observatory cards, so photometry after a geometry step keeps its errors and DQ masking (before, only the science plane was written). Per-pixel calibration cards are unchanged, since the pixel area is preserved. A Bayer mosaic's \code{BAYERPAT} is remapped with the transform; since 0.7.0 an X-Trans mosaic is refused by name.

    \subsection{Processing History: Undo, Redo and Replay (v0.6.9.5)}
    \label{sec:history}

    \textbf{Operations.} Every Processing step on a single file (background extraction with its polynomial, DBE and de-band models, wavelet denoise, deconvolution, arcsinh, GHS and masked stretch, LHE, HDRMT, Curves and PixelMath) runs through \code{run\_op\_cmd} as an operation: a serialisable \code{OpSpec} tagged with its name and a format version, for example \code{\{"op": "arcsinh", "v": 1, "factor": 50.0\}}, executed by \code{core::ops::run\_op}. A spec of another version is refused by name, and golden JSON files pin the serialised form of each of the ten operations. A PSF supplied to deconvolution is stored by its hash in the history's \code{kernels/} folder, so the step can be replayed.

    \textbf{Storage.} Histories live in the app data folder's \code{history/}, which the launch cleanup of \code{output/} never touches: one folder per image, named by a 64-bit FNV-1a hash of the canonical image reference (and of the chain base for a stack or cube-frame base, so a rebased chain does not share the file's history). Each folder holds a \code{history.json} sidecar (\code{"format": "astroburst-history"}, version 1) and the node outputs. The sidecar records the source's size and modification time, the head and the nodes; a node has an id (\code{n0001}, \ldots), a parent, its operation, its output (FITS and PNG files, dimensions, planes, and whether the values are linear, rescaled or display-referred), the time, the elapsed time, the app version and thread count, its size in bytes and whether its files are present. Every fourth node along a path is a checkpoint. Node FITS headers carry \code{ABHIST} (the image id) and \code{ABNODE} (the node id).

    \textbf{Branches.} A step's parent is the node whose output it read, or none when it read the source file, so a step run on an earlier node starts a new branch and the later steps stay reachable. Along one branch the stages keep the chain order of Section~\ref{sec:frontend} (PixelMath may follow any stage); a step that would follow a later stage is refused with a message to run it on an earlier step. A step whose input is not part of the image's history, such as a Solved WCS copy on screen, is refused with a message asking to revert to the original or re-select the file. PixelMath on a file no longer takes an output name: its result is a history step, and a copy named \code{<stem>\_pixelmath.fits} is written to the output folder and added to the Files list.

    \textbf{Undo, redo and the History tool.} Ctrl+Z steps back and Ctrl+Shift+Z or Ctrl+Y forward in both viewers, quick repeated presses are applied in turn, and neither does anything while a composite is on screen. The history and the step on screen come back after a restart, and the chain crumbs in Processing jump to any step. The History tool lists the steps of the active path with a marker where another branch leaves it, shows the size of the next step and of \code{history/}, and offers Go to, Re-run with (the step's panel opens with its settings), Re-run downstream (the steps listed after it, including steps undone with Ctrl+Z, are re-applied on a new branch), Delete branch and Export history JSON. Revert to original keeps the history.

    \textbf{Clean up and replay.} The history sweep uses the output folder's size cap. Over the cap it lowers what each history keeps, oldest history first: at first the first step of the head's path, the head and its parent and the checkpoints of that path; then the head and its parent only; and last, never for the most recently used history, nothing beyond the nodes that replay cannot rebuild. Those are kept at every level: a node is not replayable when it read a file the history does not keep (a chain base, a changed source or a PixelMath slot). A removed step is rebuilt by replay when it is shown again: the operations are re-run from the nearest present ancestor, or from the source, and each result must have the recorded dimensions and plane count. When the source file changed on disk since its history was recorded, selecting it shows the original instead of the last step, with a note in the History tool; the next step records from the new file, and the steps of the old file stay in the history and are never removed by Clean up.

    \subsection{The CFA Model and Channel Extraction (v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:cfa}

    \code{core/imaging/cfa.rs} describes a one-shot-colour mosaic once for every consumer. A pattern is a $2 \times 2$ Bayer cell (\code{RGGB}, \code{BGGR}, \code{GRBG}, \code{GBRG}) or a $6 \times 6$ X-Trans layout written as a 36-character \code{BAYERPAT}. \code{CfaPattern::detect} reads \code{BAYERPAT} or \code{COLORTYP}, shifts the pattern by \code{XBAYROFF}/\code{YBAYROFF} modulo its period and, for a header with \code{ROWORDER = 'BOTTOM-UP'}, reads the rows as a reflection about the last row (from \code{ZNAXIS2} or \code{NAXIS2}), so the pattern describes the array as stored. The luminance bin averages each $2 \times 2$ Bayer or $3 \times 3$ X-Trans cell into one value from the pattern's aligned origin; it is the input the offline solver will detect stars on (Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations}). X-Trans frames are refused by name in aligned stacks, cosmetic correction and rotate or flip.

    \textbf{Extraction.} Debayer offers four extraction modes for Bayer mosaics (\code{extract\_cfa\_cmd}, single and batch): Green (the mean of the two green sites), H$\alpha$ (the red site), H$\alpha$ + OIII and Split CFA (the four sites as four planes, \code{CFA0} to \code{CFA3}), each at half resolution. For OIII the blue site is first brought to the green level,
    \begin{equation}
        k = \frac{\operatorname{MAD}(G)}{\operatorname{MAD}(B)}, \qquad c = \tilde{G} - k\,\tilde{B}, \qquad
        \text{OIII} = \frac{G_1 + G_2 + k\,B + c}{3},
    \end{equation}
    with $\tilde{G}$ and $\tilde{B}$ the medians of the green and blue sites and $k = 1$ when the blue MAD is zero. Every plane is written as its own FITS file with the WCS scaled to the half-resolution grid (SIP included), \code{LTV} and \code{LTM}, the binning and pixel-size cards scaled, the CFA cards removed, a \code{FILTER} card for the H$\alpha$, OIII and green planes and an \code{ABCFAEXT} card naming the plane; an odd last row or column is trimmed. A pattern selected in the panel is used as given; with none selected the pattern is read from the header; X-Trans and mono frames are refused by name.

    \section{MAST Archive (v0.6.9 to v0.7.0)}
    \label{sec:mast}
%% ============================================================================

    AstroBurst queries MAST, the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes, through its Mission Search API. The client and its cache arrived in 0.6.9 (\code{infra/mast/}, \code{core/mast/}), the Archive screen in 0.6.9.5, and downloads, presets, footprints and the header peek in 0.7.0. The Archive is a mode beside the Workspace, switched from the footer, with Ctrl+1 and Ctrl+2, from a button on the empty screen or from the palette. The code is behind the \code{mast} Cargo feature, which is in the default set; the headless server is built without it.

    \subsection{Missions, Search and Results}

    \code{core/mast/missions.rs} lists ten missions in a fixed order: JWST, HST, Roman, IUE, CLASSY, ULLYSES, Roman CGI, Roman Spectra, TESS and the multi-mission search. Each declares what MAST offers for it (search, images, products) and which result views apply, and a mission without image endpoints is listed with the reason. The browser searches JWST and HST by target name (resolved by MAST's name lookup), by coordinates with a cone of 0.1 to 30\,arcmin, by programme or by dataset id, with quick filters, in pages of 200 rows up to 2000. Results show as a gallery of cards or a dense table; the detail pane shows the previews MAST publishes for the dataset, its metadata and its product list, with file classes, sizes and which files the app opens, and with reference and guide-star files hidden. Roman stays locked until a MAST token is entered: the token must be 16 to 512 printable ASCII characters without spaces, is checked against MAST before it is stored and is never shown again.

    \textbf{Presets (0.7.0).} A Preset picker in the query bar narrows a JWST search to TSO, IFU cubes, Slit spectra, 1D spectra, PSF calibration, Pure parallel or Pipeline catalogues. The preset's MAST conditions are added after the quick filters, the summary line names the preset and any condition MAST left out of its answer, and the Products tab lists the preset's files first (for example \code{x1dints} and \code{calints} for TSO). JWST searches also return the target type (\code{targtype}).

    \subsection{Previews and the Cache}

    Thumbnails and previews come from the image lists MAST publishes for a dataset. A preview is resized to a long edge of 2048\,px for the detail pane and of 4096\,px for the Large view (JPEG quality 90), and an image above 400\,Mpx is not decoded. When the JPEG comment segment carries FITS WCS cards (\code{CD}, or \code{CDELT} with \code{CROTA2}), they are read and rescaled with the resize, and the Large view draws its scale bar from them. The browser cache lives in a \code{mast} folder of the app's local data folder: images under \code{img/<mission>/<dataset>/}, capped at 1\,GiB, and image and product lists under \code{lists/}, capped at 50\,MiB and expiring after 24\,h, both evicted least recently used first; searches are kept in memory (32 entries for 10 minutes). Since 0.6.9.5 an empty image list is no longer cached for a day and HST thumbnails stay cached across restarts; since 0.7.0 the first six filmstrip thumbnails of the selected dataset load right after the first visible row of cards. Development builds now compile the dependencies with optimisation (\code{[profile.dev.package."*"] opt-level = 3}), which brought the preparation of a $7065 \times 4177$ MAST preview in \code{tauri dev} from 9.6\,s to about 0.25\,s; release builds are unchanged.

    \subsection{Error Model and Rate Limits}

    Every MAST failure crosses the IPC boundary as one structure, \code{\{code, message, context\}}. The code is one of 21 values, each \code{mast\_} followed by \code{network}, \code{timeout}, \code{cancelled}, \code{rate\_limited}, \code{server}, \code{rejected}, \code{unauthorized}, \code{no\_image}, \code{not\_deployed}, \code{decode}, \code{too\_large}, \code{not\_image}, \code{bad\_input}, \code{token\_rejected}, \code{cache}, \code{size\_mismatch}, \code{busy}, \code{stale}, \code{quota}, \code{uri\_refused} or \code{peek\_incomplete}. The context carries what is known: among other fields the HTTP status, the \code{Retry-After} delay, MAST's own detail text (at most 300 characters), the mission, the operation, the timeout, the attempts and the elapsed time. Every image slot (gallery card, table thumbnail, preview strip, preview pane, Large view) shows that cause instead of an empty frame, the HTTP status with MAST's detail or the seconds waited for a timeout, with a Retry. Thumbnails and image lists retry once after a server error or a timeout.

    \code{infra/mast/ratelimit.rs} keeps a closed or open window. A 429, or a 503 with \code{Retry-After}, is a rate limit and opens the window for the advertised delay, capped at 120\,s; without the header the delay follows the ladder 10, 30 and 60\,s by the number of consecutive limits. A 503 without \code{Retry-After} on an image request counts as busy, and three consecutive ones open the window as well. Any success closes it and resets the count.

    \subsection{HTTP Profiles and the Download Engine}
    \label{sec:net}

    \code{infra/net/} is shared by the MAST downloads, the header peek and the catalogue store. Every client built from a profile sends \code{User-Agent: AstroBurst/<version>} and follows a redirect only over HTTPS to a host the profile lists, at most three hops; the default profiles list none, and MAST downloads list \code{mast.stsci.edu} and \code{stpubdata.s3.us-east-1.amazonaws.com}. The request profile allows 30\,s to connect and 300\,s in total; the transfer profile allows 30\,s to connect and 60\,s between reads with no total limit, so a slow but steady body is never cut while a stalled one fails. \code{get\_with\_retry} tries a GET three times, waiting 1\,s and then 2\,s, after a transport failure or a 5xx.

    \textbf{Resumable downloads.} \code{infra/net/download.rs} writes a download through three companion files: \code{<dest>.part} holds the bytes, \code{<dest>.part.json} records the URL, the \code{ETag} and \code{Last-Modified} validators, the expected size and the bytes received (rewritten every 8\,MiB), and \code{<dest>.done.json} describes a finished file. A resumed request sends \code{Range: bytes=<received>-} with \code{If-Range} set to the strong \code{ETag} (a weak one is never sent) or to \code{Last-Modified}; a \code{206} continues the part, a full \code{200} or changed validators restart from zero, and a connection that ends early resumes within the same call. The part is renamed into place only when its size matches the expected one and, when the caller supplies a SHA-256, when the digest matches; a mismatch discards the part and names both values. The destination is claimed for the duration of the call, so two downloads cannot write one file.

    \textbf{Revalidation.} A file whose record matches its URL and size is not fetched again. The engine sends \code{Range: bytes=0-0} with \code{If-None-Match}, or \code{If-Modified-Since} when only a date is known: a \code{304}, or a \code{200} or \code{206} with the same validators, confirms the copy; different validators make it stale (\code{mast\_stale}, which offers Re-download); a 5xx, a failed request or a record without validators keeps the copy in use, reported as not revalidated (``not verified against MAST'' in the interface).

    \textbf{The Data folder and the quota (0.6.9.5).} \code{infra/net/paths.rs} resolves the Data folder from the \code{ASTROBURST\_DATA\_DIR} environment variable, then the Data folder of Settings (applied with Enter), then the platform default, \code{\%LOCALAPPDATA\%\textbackslash astroburst} on Windows and the user data folder elsewhere. A relative, uncreatable or read-only folder is refused with a message naming the field, and so is a folder inside the app's own data folders, which the app clears (the output folder at every launch, the MAST browser cache with Clear cache). MAST downloads go to its \code{mast} subfolder and the catalogue store to \code{catalogs/}. The MAST download quota is 20\,GB when empty ($10^9$ bytes per GB) and counts only files downloaded from MAST; a quota of zero or less is refused before it is saved. Free space is read with \code{GetDiskFreeSpaceExW} on Windows, \code{statvfs} on Linux and \code{statfs} on macOS.

    \subsection{Downloads and Open (v0.7.0)}

    Product files ticked in the Products tab are queued with Download or Download and open, with a confirmation above 1\,GB. A product URI (\code{mast:JWST/product/<file>} and its HST and TESS forms, or an HLSP URI, at most 512 characters) is validated before a destination is planned under the \code{mast} folder, for example \code{JWST/<programme>/}, \code{HST/<programme>/} or \code{HLSP/<collection>/}. Before anything is sent, the queue adds the requested sizes to what is already downloaded or queued and refuses a request that would exceed the quota or the free disk space. Downloads run two at a time through the transfer profile and the engine above, at most 500 files per request, with the size MAST lists checked on completion. A file downloaded before is revalidated instead of fetched; a newer version at MAST offers Re-download, and a file AstroBurst did not download offers Replace. Two HLSP files that would be saved under the same name are refused, naming both URIs. Roman products are listed without download controls until the Roman URI form is confirmed, and so are IUE products; an HLSP file whose URI holds characters other than letters, digits and \code{. \_ - /} gets a disabled checkbox that says why.

    A Downloads pill in the Archive footer opens the list, newest first, with progress, Cancel, Open and Show in folder. Open loads images, cubes, spectra and ramps into the Workspace, or selects them when the Files list already holds them; catalogues and files the Files panel does not read stay download-only. A Re-download or Replace of a file the Files list holds reloads it there, and Download pressed again on a file whose Cancel has not taken effect yet keeps it downloading.

    \subsection{Footprints on the Open Image (v0.7.0)}

    A dataset's footprint is its \code{s\_region}, an STC-S string. \code{core/astrometry/stcs.rs} parses \code{POLYGON}, \code{CIRCLE}, \code{BOX} and \code{UNION} (nested at most 8 deep) in the ICRS, FK5 or J2000 frames, and refuses with a reason other frames (galactic, ecliptic, FK4 and the like), other operators (\code{INTERSECTION}, \code{NOT}, \code{DIFFERENCE}) and polygons of more than 4096 vertices; one footprint request projects at most 20\,000 vertices in all. A circle becomes a 64-vertex polygon. \code{mast\_footprints\_cmd} loads the WCS of the image on screen through \code{load\_wcs} (Section~\ref{sec:wcs}), so a plate-solved or processed version and a gWCS are used when present, and maps the vertices with \code{world\_to\_pixel\_batch}; a polygon with a vertex that does not project is dropped with a note, and moving targets get no footprint. The Show on toggle is enabled once the image has a celestial WCS and says why when it is not; it draws several datasets at once (rows that share a dataset id, such as IUE apertures, each draw their own footprint) and highlights the hovered or selected row, and the Metadata tab then gives the footprint's vertex count, its bounding cone and whether it overlaps the image. Save .reg writes the drawn footprints that overlap the image as a DS9 region file in image pixels or, when none does, the selected footprint in ICRS; both re-import through Regions within $10^{-6}$\,px.

    \subsection{Header Peek (v0.7.0)}

    HST datasets list no \code{s\_region}. Fetch footprint, on a FITS image in the Products tab, reads only the file's headers from MAST in HTTP byte ranges (\code{infra/fits/header\_peek.rs}, \code{infra/mast/peek.rs}). The header walker parses cards as they arrive, skips each data unit by the size its header declares and asks for the next range at the next header: 128\,KiB first, then 64\,KiB, at most 8 ranges and 512\,KiB in all, about 2\,s for an HST ACS/WFC \code{\_flc}. When MAST answers the first range with the whole file, at most 384\,KiB of it are read. For HST every science extension is read, for the other missions the first image; each yields one footprint (the four pixel-edge corners and the centre projected through the WCS of the merged primary and extension header), with the image size, pixel type, instrument, filter and exposure. Results are cached for 30 days under the lists cache, named by the SHA-256 of the product URI, and cleared with it; a peek that stops before the last science header is marked partial and draws no footprint. When several files of a dataset were peeked, Show on and Save .reg use a drizzled \code{\_drc} or \code{\_drz} before a \code{\_flc} or \code{\_flt}, and a footprint already drawn stays under the toggle while other files are peeked.

    \section{FITS and ASDF I/O}
    \label{sec:fitsio}
%% ============================================================================

    FITS via \crate{memmap2} with BSCALE/BZERO: $v_{\text{phys}} = \text{BSCALE} \times v_{\text{stored}} + \text{BZERO}$. Multi-BITPIX export: 16 (auto BZERO/BSCALE), $-32$ (float32), $-64$ (float64).

    ASDF: a native Rust reader. YAML tree $\to$ NdArrayMeta $\to$ binary block (zlib/bzip2/lz4) $\to$ Array2, with Roman data-model traversal and auto-dispatch by extension. The reader was rewritten in 0.5.8 around \crate{memmap2} with lazily decoded blocks and validated against the ASDF Standard. Since 0.6.7 verbatim YAML tags (\code{!<tag:stsci.edu:gwcs/\ldots>}) are kept, the gWCS of an image is evaluated (Section~\ref{sec:wcs}), and a Roman image's synthesised header carries a fitted TAN-SIP WCS. Since 0.6.9 float16 arrays decode (the err and variance planes of Roman \code{wfi\_image} 1.6.0 and later load and feed photometry), \code{fitswcs\_imaging} transforms in pure ASDF files evaluate as an exact gWCS, Roman metadata reaches the \code{FILTER}, \code{PUPIL}, \code{DETECTOR}, \code{DATE-OBS}, \code{DATE-END}, \code{MJD-OBS}, \code{EXPMID} and \code{TIMESYS} cards with ASDF time tags decoded, and the filter table holds the pivot wavelengths of the Roman WFI filters F062 to F213.

    \subsection{Image References (v0.6.0)}

    Every path-based command accepts a reference rather than a bare path: \code{<path>\#hdu=<n>} selects any FITS HDU and \code{<path>\#array=<key>} any ASDF array, including dotted keys such as \code{roman.dq}. Parsing lives in \code{types/image\_ref.rs}; a single loader in \code{infra/image\_source.rs} resolves references for both the desktop commands and the headless server. The reference, not the path, is the cache key, and output filenames derive their stem from it, so \code{jw01234\_cal.fits\#hdu=3} writes \code{jw01234\_cal\_hdu3.png}. This is what makes ERR, DQ and variance planes first-class images that can be displayed and measured like any other.

    \subsection{Lossless Integer Planes (v0.6.0)}

    A DQ plane is a bit field, so rounding it through \code{f32} destroys individual flags. BITPIX 8/16/32 HDUs and int8--int32, uint8--uint32 and bool8 ASDF arrays are therefore decoded a second time into an \code{IntPlane} that sits beside the float view in the cache and counts against the same budget. The BZERO offsets 32768 and 2147483648 that FITS uses to store unsigned data in signed types are applied in integer arithmetic, never through a float intermediate. Display and processing stay in \code{f32}/\code{f64}; only flag decoding reads the integer plane.

    \subsection{Multi-Extension Output (v0.6.0)}

    \code{write\_mef\_images} writes plain multi-HDU IMAGE files from \code{f32}, \code{i32} and \code{u32} arrays. The science cutout path uses it to emit SCI, ERR and DQ in one file with \code{CRPIX} shifted and \code{LTV1}/\code{LTV2}/\code{LTM1\_1}/\code{LTM2\_2} recorded, so the cutout remains registerable against its parent. \code{export\_fits} can additionally write RICE\_1-compressed tiles with a quantization level, for BITPIX 16 or $-32$ only (written through \crate{fitsio-pure} since 0.6.9, below). Export with ``Apply current STF stretch'' (0.6.5.0) writes a display-referred header: \code{ABPROC='stf\_export'}, \code{ABDISP=T}, no \code{BUNIT}, photometric or saturation cards, so region fluxes and photometry treat the file as uncalibrated.

    \subsection{Binary Tables (v0.6.5, v0.6.7)}

    Binary tables are not opened as images. \code{infra/fits/table.rs} reads columns by name for the consumers that need them: the JWST \code{EXTRACT1D} table of an \code{x1d} file, the \code{WCS-TABLE} of a \code{-TAB} spectral axis, and the ramp tables (\code{GROUP}, \code{INT\_TIMES}). Fixed-repeat array columns are read since 0.6.7 (Section~\ref{sec:measure}); the reader refuses a table whose header declares more data than the file holds, a \code{TFIELDS} outside 0--999 and a duplicated column name.

    \subsection{The JWST \texttt{ASDF} Extension (v0.6.7)}

    A JWST \code{\_cal} FITS file carries an \code{ASDF} BINTABLE extension (HDU 8 in the local files) with one row and one byte column (\code{ASDF\_METADATA}) holding a complete ASDF file of 60--72\,KB, with its own blocks and block index. \code{infra/fits/asdf\_hdu.rs} finds it by its header (\code{EXTNAME='ASDF'}, one \code{<n>B} column, one row), reads the cell as raw bytes from the data area without going through the table reader, checks the \code{\#ASDF} magic and hands it to the ASDF parser, so the gWCS and \code{meta.wcsinfo} of the exposure are available beside the FITS header.

    \subsection{Ramps (v0.6.6)}

    A 4-D FITS image with \code{NAXIS4} $> 1$ and \code{NGROUPS}/\code{NINTS} cards, or a \code{DATAMODL} of \code{Level1bModel} or \code{RampModel}, opens as a ramp instead of a cube of \code{NINTS} $\times$ \code{NGROUPS} planes; the primary header supplies the ramp cards and the \code{SCI} header the WCS and units. Roman L1 ASDF resultant stacks open the same way.

    \subsection{Tile-Compressed FITS through fitsio-pure (v0.6.9)}

    Tile-compressed FITS is read, and RICE\_1 and GZIP\_2 written, through \crate{fitsio-pure} (pinned to 0.21.6, with its \code{parallel} feature), a pure-Rust port of cfitsio's codecs, in place of the Rice, GZIP and quantization code that \code{infra/fits/compress} held before (net 1\,388 lines removed). Reads are bit-identical to the previous reader on the bundled fixtures, the HST sample data, astropy-written 26\,MP RICE\_1, GZIP\_1 and GZIP\_2 files and Solar Orbiter EUI L2 frames; 26\,MP compressed reads are 3.4 to 5.6 times faster on 64 threads and 1.1 to 1.4 times faster on one, and RICE\_1 exports 1.2 to 2.2 times faster. Float exports quantize as cfitsio does (the same \code{ZSCALE}, \code{ZZERO} and error bound, with some pixels one step apart from before), lossless GZIP\_2 floats carry \code{ZQUANTIZ = 'NONE'}, and the reader also opens HCOMPRESS\_1, PLIO\_1, any tile shape and SUBTRACTIVE\_DITHER\_2.

    \textbf{Hardening.} \code{infra/fits/compressed\_guard.rs} validates a compressed HDU before any tile is decoded, because a malformed or hostile file used to be able to kill the app. It refuses, with a message naming the cause, non-ASCII header bytes, a tile grid that does not match the table, a non-positive tile size, a Rice \code{BLOCKSIZE} $\le 0$ (which looped forever), a \code{BYTEPIX} outside 1, 2, 4 or 8, column widths or a heap that run past the data, 64-bit descriptors that overflow, a GZIP tile that inflates beyond its size, a degenerate HCOMPRESS tile and a decoded size beyond the memory budget. An integer compressed image whose nulls are declared only by \code{ZBLANK} shows them as NaN. On export, quantized RICE\_1 or GZIP\_2 stores $\pm\infty$ as NaN, refuses finite values too large to quantize (above \code{f32::MAX}/16) with a message suggesting a lossless or 16-bit export, and leaves no partial file behind.

    \subsection{Gzip-Compressed FITS (v0.6.9.5, v0.7.0)}

    A \code{.fits.gz}, \code{.fit.gz} or \code{.fts.gz} file is recognised by the gzip magic bytes and decompressed by \code{infra/fits/gzip.rs} as a stream, in 1\,MiB chunks, into a temporary copy that the memory-mapped reader then opens. The decompressed size is capped by the memory budget (which \code{ASTROBURST\_MEMORY\_BUDGET\_MB} can set) or, when smaller, by the free space of the temporary volume less 1\,GiB, so a decompression bomb or a truncated file is refused with a message before it fills the disk. Two decompressed copies are kept warm, the last 16 refusals are remembered, and copies older than a day are swept at the first use. In 0.6.9.5 the reader accepted these files; since 0.7.0 the Open dialog, drag and drop, folders and MAST downloads accept them, while any other \code{.gz}, such as a \code{.tar.gz}, is still skipped (on macOS the Open dialog lists every \code{.gz} file, since its panel matches only the last extension). A gzip \code{x1d} pairs with its \code{\_s3d} cube in either compression, the spectrum pickers list gzip FITS, Compress FITS on a gzip source gives the ratio against the decompressed size, and the Files list drops \code{.fits.gz} from a name as it drops \code{.fits}.

    \subsection{DQ Planes of Unknown Instruments (v0.6.9)}

    A DQ or MASK plane from an instrument the app does not know (no JWST, Roman or HST telescope or instrument card on the image plane or on the DQ plane), such as the MASK HDU of a Subaru/HSC file opened as the active HDU, shows raw bit numbers (\code{BIT n}, only the bits present in the plane), overlays nothing by default and excludes nothing from statistics, contours and photometry. Before, it received the JWST flag names, the JWST overlay and the JWST exclusion of bit 0. Cutouts of such files flag padded pixels with raw bits 0 and 9 and, when the box extends past the image, record it in a \code{HISTORY} card of the DQ extension.

    \section{WebGPU Compute Pipeline}
%% ============================================================================

    WGSL fragment shader implementing MTF with symmetric denominator guard. \code{r32float} texture, 6-vertex fullscreen quad. Uniform buffer (32 bytes): dataMin, dataMax, shadow, midtone, highlight, $W$, $H$ and a padding word. CPU worker fallback with identical \code{stfTransfer} function.

    \subsection{Display Path Unification (v0.6.0, extended in v0.6.3 and v0.6.5)}

    The shader evaluates the six scale modes (\code{mtf}, \code{linear}, \code{log}, \code{sqrt}, \code{asinh}, \code{power}) and samples a $256 \times 1$ LUT texture for the colormap, so a colormap change uploads 1\,KiB rather than re-rendering on the CPU. Limits come from the four limit modes (\code{minmax}, \code{zscale}, \code{percentile}, \code{user}) computed in Rust by \code{compute\_scale\_limits\_cmd}; the LUT itself comes from \code{get\_colormap\_lut\_cmd}.

    The point of the exercise is that the stretch, the limit algorithms and the float-to-byte rule are written once, in \code{core/imaging/scale.rs}, and three consumers agree on them: the WGSL shader, the TypeScript worker fallback, and the headless server which returns a PNG. The parity is asserted rather than assumed --- a vitest case sweeps every scale kind and checks the WGSL port against the canonical transfer within \code{f32} rounding.

    Since 0.6.3 there are 13 colormaps: nine sequential (gray, viridis, inferno, magma, plasma, cividis, heat, cool, rainbow) and four diverging (RdBu, coolwarm, bwr, seismic, from the matplotlib knots). Symmetric limits mirror the resolved pair about a centre $c$ (default 0): $(\text{lo}, \text{hi}) \mapsto (c - a, c + a)$ with $a = \max(|\text{lo} - c|, |\text{hi} - c|)$, falling back to a half-width of $\max(1, |c|\,2^{-20})$ for a degenerate pair. No-data pixels (NaN, infinity, exact 0) take a per-colormap colour that ignores \code{invert}: the first LUT entry for the sequential maps and neutral grey \code{\#404040} for the diverging ones, identically on the desktop shader, the worker and the server. Since 0.6.5.0 a colorbar under the mono GPU viewer shows the current colormap with value ticks placed through the current stretch and limits, the \code{BUNIT} unit and the no-data swatch.

    Device loss is recoverable. A failed initialisation used to be cached for the session, so one transient adapter error left the app on the CPU path until restart; the retry path now re-probes. When WebGPU is unavailable, the CPU viewer shows the auto-stretched preview with zoom, pan, regions, the grid and the compass, and disables the stretch, limit and colormap controls with the reason.

    \begin{notebox}[GPU scope]
        The GPU is used for display only. Stacking, alignment, drizzle, deconvolution, background extraction, PixelMath, wavelets, line fits, reprojection and the compose pipeline all run on the CPU in Rust, parallelised with \crate{rayon}. No processing result depends on a GPU being present.
    \end{notebox}

    \section{Headless HTTP Server}
    \label{sec:server}
%% ============================================================================

    AstroBurst ships a second binary that exposes the same imaging core over HTTP, with no window, no WebView and no Tauri runtime. It is an \crate{axum} 0.7 service on \crate{tokio}, built around short-lived sessions that hold a per-session image cache, so a script or notebook can open a FITS or ASDF file once and then issue many inspection calls against the decoded array instead of re-reading the file each time. The headless server was contributed by Jae-Joon Lee (\url{https://github.com/leejjoon}); the Cargo manifest records this on the server dependency block and on the \code{[[bin]]} entry, and every source file under \code{src-tauri/src/server/} carries the same note.

    The server is gated behind the \code{server} Cargo feature, which is \emph{not} in \code{default}. The feature pulls in \crate{axum}, \crate{dashmap}, \crate{uuid}, \crate{tokio-stream}, \crate{tokio-util} and \crate{env\_logger}, all declared optional; \crate{tower} is a development dependency of the server tests only. The binary is \code{astroburst-server} (\code{src/server/main.rs}) and declares \code{required-features = ["server"]}, so it is only built when that feature is on. Because the desktop feature \code{tauri} is a default, the server build turns the defaults off and re-enables the non-UI ones explicitly:

    \begin{infobox}[Build]
        \cmd{cargo build -{}-no-default-features \textbackslash}\\
        \cmd{\hspace*{2em}-{}-features server,astrometry-net,asdf-full,vizier}
    \end{infobox}

    On start the process reads its configuration from the environment, initialises \crate{env\_logger}, resolves the catalog cache directory and the VizieR endpoint (\code{ASTROBURST\_CATALOG\_CACHE\_DIR}, \code{ASTROBURST\_VIZIER\_URL}, as the desktop app does), spawns the TTL cleaner and serves on the configured address. Every response carries an \code{x-request-id} header, echoed from the request when present and otherwise a fresh UUIDv4, applied by an \crate{axum} \code{middleware::from\_fn} layer over the whole router.

    \subsection{Session Model}

    A session is created by \code{POST /v2/sessions} (or the identical \code{POST /sessions}), which answers \code{201} with a UUIDv4 \code{session\_id}. Sessions live in a \crate{dashmap} keyed by that id; there is no authentication and no per-session credential.

    Each session owns four things (\code{server/session.rs}):

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item an \code{ImageCache} --- the same LRU cache the desktop app uses --- bounded by both \code{cache\_max\_entries} and \code{cache\_max\_bytes};
        \item a map of jobs, keyed by job id;
        \item the v2 state: a monotonic counter, an \code{active\_ref} under an \code{RwLock}, and a map of \code{ImageMeta} records;
        \item a \code{last\_accessed} timestamp.
    \end{itemize}

    Images are referenced by name inside the session rather than by path. The v2 routes mint a ref on each producing call --- \code{img\_0}, \code{img\_1}, \code{cutout\_0}, \code{bin\_0} and so on, from a per-session counter --- unless the caller supplies \code{name}. Each ref carries an \code{ImageMeta} record holding \code{image\_ref}, \code{source}, \code{hdu}, \code{array}, \code{plane\_ref}, \code{is\_dq}, \code{width}, \code{height}, \code{wcs\_present} and \code{extname}. The most recently opened, cut or switched image becomes the session's \code{active\_ref}, and every consuming endpoint treats a missing \code{ref} field as ``use the active ref'', failing with \code{400} if there is none. The older slot-based routes instead take a caller-chosen \code{slot} string, defaulting to the file path.

    Lifetime is bounded on three axes. Session creation fails with \code{503} once \code{session\_max} sessions exist. Any request that resolves a session touches its timestamp through the \code{SessionExtractor}, and \code{POST /v2/sessions/:sid/keepalive} exists to do only that. A background task ticks every \code{cleanup\_interval}, skips any session with a running job, and removes sessions idle longer than \code{session\_ttl}, logging the eviction. \code{DELETE /v2/sessions/:sid} removes one immediately.

    Eviction inside a session is independent of session lifetime: the image cache drops least-recently-used entries when either bound is exceeded, which can silently invalidate a ref the client still holds. The server reconciles rather than pretending otherwise --- \code{prune\_evicted\_meta} drops metadata whose cache entry is gone, and \code{reconcile\_active\_ref} additionally clears \code{active\_ref} when it points at an evicted image. \code{GET /v2/sessions/:sid} and \code{GET /v2/sessions/:sid/images} run that reconciliation before reporting, so a listing never advertises an image the cache no longer holds.

    The v2 endpoints share one region grammar (\code{server/v2/region.rs}): a request may pass a pixel rectangle, a sky rectangle in arcminutes around an RA/Dec, or a full shape --- the core \code{RegionShape} set --- in pixel or sky coordinates. A region that leaves the frame is rejected with \code{region\_out\_of\_bounds} unless the caller passes \code{clip: true}, and a sky region on an image without WCS is rejected with \code{wcs\_required}.

    \code{POST /v2/sessions/:sid/render} is the only route that does not answer JSON: it returns \code{image/png} and puts the resolved parameters --- the region actually used, \code{vmin}/\code{vmax}, the limit algorithm, stretch, colormap, the binning factor applied to honour \code{max\_dim}, the PNG's arcsec-per-pixel scale and the fraction of pixels clipped at each end --- into an \code{x-render-resolved} JSON header. Crosshair and scalebar overlays are drawn into the buffer before encoding. The \code{scale} block accepts the 13 colormaps and \code{symmetric}/\code{centre} (alias \code{center}), and the resolved header echoes them with any notes.

    \textbf{WCS on the server (0.6.7).} Every server route that needs a WCS goes through the same loader as the desktop app (Section~\ref{sec:wcs}), keyed by the source file of the image ref, so a JWST \code{\_cal} or Roman image opened in a session is served by its gWCS and a derived ref (a cutout or a binned image) by its own header. The \code{wcs} route returns the keys of \code{get\_wcs\_info}: \code{sip\_max\_err\_px}, \code{sip\_inv\_err\_px}, \code{wcs\_kind}, \code{gwcs\_steps}, \code{gwcs\_frames}, \code{gwcs\_source}, \code{gwcs\_vs\_header\_sip\_max\_mas}/\code{\_px} and \code{gwcs\_refusal}. \code{wcs/pix2sky} applies the gWCS bounding box and returns \code{null} coordinates for a pixel outside it. The stricter header rule (a celestial \code{CTYPE} pair) applies here too.

    Alongside these, the router keeps an earlier slot-based surface: \code{POST /sessions} and, under \code{/sessions/:sid}, \code{fits/open} and \code{fits/header} for I/O; \code{image/render}, \code{image/stf} and \code{image/viewport} for STF-based PNG output; \code{stacking/stack}, \code{stacking/drizzle} and \code{pipeline/run} for batch work; and the three job routes below. These address images by \code{slot} and do not participate in the v2 ref or active-ref model.

    \subsection{Job Model}

    Only three routes start work that can outlive a request: \code{stacking/stack}, \code{stacking/drizzle} and \code{pipeline/run}. All three follow the same contract (\code{server/job.rs}, \code{server/handlers/}):

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \textbf{Admission.} The handler validates its parameters, then takes a permit from a \code{Semaphore} sized at \code{jobs\_max} with \code{try\_acquire\_owned}. No permit means an immediate \code{429}; the queue never builds up. Validation includes refusing one-shot-colour lights (\code{BAYERPAT} or \code{COLORTYP}) for stacking with alignment, drizzle and the aligned pipeline (0.6.5.0), and, for \code{pipeline/run}, the frame-card checks of Section~\ref{sec:calstack} (0.6.7): a refused calibration set is a \code{400} before any job starts, and the warnings are returned in the \code{202} body. The pipeline body accepts \code{flat\_dark\_paths} since 0.6.7.
        \item \textbf{Submission.} A \code{Job} is created with a bounded 256-slot event channel, registered in \code{session.jobs}, and the work is moved onto \code{spawn\_blocking} holding the permit. The handler answers \code{202 Accepted} with \code{job\_id}, \code{status: "running"} and the cache slot(s) the result will be stored under. The pipeline route additionally returns any DQ cosmetic warnings raised while inspecting the input frames.
        \item \textbf{Polling.} \code{GET /sessions/:sid/jobs/:jid} returns \code{id}, \code{action}, \code{status} (\code{running}, \code{done}, \code{error}, \code{cancelled}), \code{pct}, and \code{started\_at}/\code{completed\_at} as milliseconds since the epoch.
        \item \textbf{Streaming.} \code{GET /sessions/:sid/jobs/:jid/stream} is a Server-Sent Events stream of \code{progress}, \code{complete} and \code{error} events, with a 30-second keep-alive. The receiver is taken out of the job on first subscribe, so a second subscriber gets \code{409}.
        \item \textbf{Cancellation.} \code{DELETE /sessions/:sid/jobs/:jid} cancels a running job's \code{CancellationToken}, marks it cancelled and returns the job record. Transitions out of \code{running} go through a compare-and-exchange, and \code{set\_cancelled} is a plain store, so a worker that finishes after the cancel cannot overwrite the cancelled state with \code{done} or \code{error}.
    \end{itemize}

    Results are written into the session's image cache under the requested slot names; there is no separate result store and no download route for them. Sessions with a running job are skipped by the TTL cleaner, so a long stack cannot have its own session evicted underneath it.

    \subsection{What Is Shared, and What Is Not}

    The server is a second front end over the library crate, not a reimplementation. It links \code{astroburst\_lib} and calls the same core modules the Tauri commands call:

    \begin{itemize}[nosep]
        \item \code{core/imaging/scale.rs} --- \code{LimitMode}, \code{StretchKind}, \code{resolve\_limits}, \code{normalize\_and\_stretch}: used by \code{server/v2/render/handler.rs} and by \code{cmd/visualization/mod.rs}, so limits and stretches resolve identically on both;
        \item \code{core/imaging/region.rs} --- the shape set, \code{shape\_to\_pixel}, masked values: used by \code{server/v2/region.rs} and by \code{cmd/regions.rs}, \code{cmd/statistics.rs}, \code{cmd/cutout.rs};
        \item \code{core/imaging/cutout.rs} --- \code{resolve\_cutout\_rect}, \code{cut\_plane\_within}, \code{shift\_header}, \code{reported\_ltv}: used by \code{server/v2/cutout.rs} and \code{cmd/cutout.rs};
        \item \code{core/imaging/pixel\_probe.rs} --- \code{probe\_pixel}, \code{probe\_companions}, \code{data\_unit}: used by \code{server/v2/pixel.rs} and by \code{cmd/statistics.rs} and \code{cmd/visualization/mod.rs}.
    \end{itemize}

    The same holds for \code{core/imaging/colormap.rs}, \code{core/imaging/statistics.rs}, \code{core/astrometry/} (\code{wcs.rs}, the \code{gwcs/} evaluator, \code{grid.rs}, \code{frames.rs}), \code{core/stacking/} (the rejection kernel, frame cards, consistency checks and masters) and the infrastructure layer --- \code{infra/cache.rs}, \code{infra/image\_source.rs} and \code{infra/wcs\_source.rs} --- all of which both binaries import.

    The split is in the command layer, not the core. Radial profiles, line cuts, exclude regions and DS9 \code{.reg} import/export exist as core functions (\code{radial\_profile} and \code{line\_cut} in \code{core/imaging/region.rs}, and \code{core/imaging/region\_file.rs}) but are reachable only through \code{cmd/regions.rs}; no route in \code{server/router.rs} exposes them. Likewise, the \code{exclude\_dq} flag that drives \code{resolve\_dq\_mask} appears only under \code{cmd/} --- in \code{cmd/analysis/mod.rs}, \code{cmd/regions.rs} and \code{cmd/statistics.rs} --- and is absent from the server's \code{StatsParams} and \code{HistogramParams}.

    \begin{notebox}[DQ on the server]
        The asymmetry is narrower than ``no DQ''. \code{POST /v2/sessions/:sid/pixel} does load the DQ and ERR companions of the probed plane and returns decoded DQ flags for the sampled pixel. What the server lacks is DQ-\emph{masked} aggregation: its stats and histogram endpoints have no exclusion flag, so they measure every finite pixel in the region regardless of its DQ state. Since 0.6.9 a DQ plane of an instrument the app does not know is decoded as raw bits, named \code{BITn} by the pixel endpoint (Section~\ref{sec:fitsio}).
    \end{notebox}

    Further desktop-only surface: photometry and the gain model, catalogs, cube science (spectra, moments, line fits, PV), 1D spectra, ramps, contours, time series, observation geometry, the processing tools and their history, the MAST archive and the compose wizard have no server routes, and \code{histogram} rejects \code{render\_png: true} with a \code{not\_implemented} code rather than plotting server-side.

    \subsection{Error Model}

    All failures go through one enum in \code{server/error.rs} and serialise to a single envelope, \code{\{"success": false, "error": \{"code", "message", "hint"?\}\}}, where \code{hint} is present only on the variant that carries one.

    \begin{table}[H]
        \centering
        \small
        \begin{tabularx}{\textwidth}{llX}
            \toprule
            \textbf{Status} & \textbf{Code} & \textbf{Raised for} \\
            \midrule
            400 & \code{bad\_request} & Malformed path or body, unknown colormap, stretch, algorithm or frame, both \code{hdu} and \code{array} given, zero or oversized bin factor, no active image in the session, one-shot-colour lights for an aligned stack or drizzle, a calibration set refused by the frame-card checks \\

    Two details are worth noting for clients. The \code{429} message, \code{"job queue full (max N concurrent)"}, names the configured \code{jobs\_max}; the status code, not the text, is the contract. And errors that originate in the core --- \code{RegionError}, \code{CutoutError}, \code{ProbeError} --- are mapped explicitly to \code{400} with a hint, so a caller mistake is never reported as \code{500}. The exception is plane loading, where only the two recognised messages (no ASDF arrays, no HDU index) become \code{400} and everything else is surfaced as \code{internal\_error}.

%% ============================================================================
    \section{Known Limitations}
    \label{sec:limitations}
%% ============================================================================

    The first five items are the competitive gaps against Siril and PixInsight that the roadmap plans to close (Section~\ref{sec:roadmap}, N1--N5).

    \begin{enumerate}[nosep]
    \item \textbf{Plate solving is online only (N1).} Solving uploads the frame to nova.astrometry.net and needs an API key; there is no local solver. Since SPCC and every WCS feature need a solution, most amateur frames depend on it. 0.7.0 ships only the foundations of an offline solver (Section~\ref{sec:offline_foundations}): no interface uses them, none of the catalogue store's entries is installable yet, and no Gaia data is bundled.
    \item \textbf{No RAW, TIFF or XISF input (N2).} Camera RAW files, TIFF (including Astro-TIFF) and XISF are not read; FITS, ASDF and ZIP containers are.
    \item \textbf{No masks or presets (N3).} Since 0.6.9.5 the Processing steps of a single file have undo, redo and a persistent history (Section~\ref{sec:history}), but masks are not first-class objects, there is no process container or saved preset, and the composite chain, the Compose wizard, Debayer and Geometry have no undo.
    \item \textbf{Registration of sequences is basic (N4).} The batch Pipeline registers each frame against the first by phase correlation or the affine matcher and now excludes what it cannot register; there is no local normalization, mosaic assembly, comet alignment or distortion-aware sequence registration, and integration holds every frame in memory.
    \item \textbf{SPCC is a blackbody approximation (N5).} Expected colours come from a Planck spectrum at each channel's effective wavelength, driven by Gaia BP$-$RP; Gaia XP spectra are not integrated through filter and sensor curves.
    \item \textbf{gWCS coverage.} Imaging gWCS only: spectroscopic, slit and IFU gWCS are not evaluated and those files keep their header WCS; Roman \code{\_uncal} files do not look up their \code{\_wcs.asdf} sidecar; transforms outside the supported set (\code{tabular}, \code{fix\_inputs}, \code{jwst\_pipeline/*}) fall back to the header with the reason shown. A derived Roman FITS carries the fitted TAN-SIP, accurate to the stated residual, not the gWCS.
    \item \textbf{Tabulated axes and tables.} Only separable one-axis \code{-TAB} tables are read; variable-length \code{P}/\code{Q} columns are not; a PV diagram of a \code{-TAB} cube is refused when the table departs from linear by more than 1\,\% of its step.
    \item \textbf{No distortion-correcting export.} Compose Align reprojects channels through their full WCS (SIP or gWCS), but there is no tool that resamples a single image onto an undistorted grid.
    \item \textbf{Provenance gaps.} The RGB drizzle FITS does not carry the stack cards (\code{NCOMBINE}, \code{ABCOMB}, \code{ABDRZSCL}).
    \item \textbf{ASDF is read-only.} The reader is complete (memory-mapped, lazy blocks, gWCS); no writer is implemented, so Roman products are exported as FITS.
    \item \textbf{MAST coverage is partial.} The Archive searches JWST and HST, and Roman once a token is entered; missions without image endpoints are listed with the reason. Downloads cover JWST, HST and HLSP products: Roman products are listed without download controls until the Roman URI form is confirmed, IUE products have no product URI the app downloads, and footprints are not drawn for moving targets.
    \item \textbf{No session persistence.} The dock layout, display settings (per file since 0.6.7.2), per-file regions and panel preferences survive a restart through \code{localStorage}, and the processing history of single files survives in \code{history/}; the loaded files and the composite do not. There is no project file.
    \item \textbf{WebGPU is preferred, not required.} The display path needs a WebGPU-capable WebView; otherwise the CPU viewer is used without the stretch, limit and colormap controls.
    \item \textbf{Server parity is partial.} Rendering, region statistics, histograms, cutouts, WCS queries, stacking, drizzle and the pipeline are available over HTTP. Radial profiles, line cuts, exclude regions, \code{.reg} import/export, DQ-masked statistics, photometry, catalogs, cube science, 1D spectra, ramps, the processing tools and their history, the MAST archive and the compose wizard are desktop-only, and the Python client covers the v1 routes only.
    \end{enumerate}
